# Proof of the F170 dual ordinary/torus order theorem

## 1. Local order divisibility

An ordinary element of exact order (A) in both hidden fields gives

\[
 A\mid p-1,
 \qquad
 A\mid q-1.
\]

Therefore (p\equiv q\equiv1\pmod A) and (A\mid N-1).

For the Jacobi-minus-one discriminant (D), let

\[
 \epsilon=(D/p)=-(D/q).
\]

The norm-one torus over a finite field of odd order (r) is cyclic of order

\[
 r-(D/r).
\]

An exact common torus order (B) therefore satisfies

\[
 B\mid p-\epsilon,
 \qquad
 B\mid q+\epsilon.
\tag{1}
\]

It follows that

\[
 p\equiv\epsilon\pmod B,
 \qquad
 q\equiv-\epsilon\pmod B,
\]

and hence (N=pq\equiv-1\pmod B). Thus (B\mid N+1).

Every common divisor of (A) and (B) divides both (N-1) and (N+1).
Since (N) is odd,

\[
 \gcd(N-1,N+1)=2.
\]

This proves (3). It uses only the exact orders of the supplied elements. The
elements do not need to generate either ambient local group.

## 2. Generalized CRT classes

Put (h=\gcd(A,B)). By Section 1, (h\in\{1,2\}). A generalized CRT
system

\[
 x\equiv a\pmod A,
 \qquad
 x\equiv b\pmod B
\]

is consistent exactly when (a\equiv b\pmod h), and then has one class
modulo (L=\operatorname{lcm}(A,B)).

For (7), (a=1) and (b=e\in\{1,-1\}). If (h=1), consistency is
automatic. If (h=2), both (1) and (e) are odd. Thus both sign systems
are consistent, including when the two orders are even. The actual (p)
obeys the system with (e=\epsilon).

Adding the congruences for (p) and (q) gives

\[
 p+q\equiv2\pmod A,
 \qquad
 p+q\equiv0\pmod B.
\]

The two residues agree modulo (h), because (h\mid2), so they give one
class modulo (L). Subtracting gives

\[
 q-p\equiv0\pmod A,
 \qquad
 q-p\equiv-2\epsilon\pmod B.
\]

These are the sum and gap claims. The gap relation

\[
 B\mid q-p+2\epsilon
\]

is exact, but it has two public sign branches. The sum relation cancels the
orientation and is therefore the smaller balanced search.

## 3. Candidate counts and verification

For an arbitrary distinct semiprime, the smaller prime satisfies

\[
 3\le p<\sqrt N.
\]

An interval of length less than (sqrt N) contains at most

\[
 \frac{\sqrt N}{L}+1
\]

members of one residue class modulo (L). There are two classes in (7).
This proves (10). The actual (p) is present, and a gcd with (N) verifies
it without knowing the orientation.

Now assume (1<q/p<2). Then

\[
 \frac{(p+q)^2}{pq}
 =\frac pq+2+\frac qp.
\]

The function (x+x^{-1}+2) is increasing for (x>1). Its value at (2)
is (9/2). Since (p\ne q), this gives the strict bounds

\[
 4N<(p+q)^2<\frac92N,
\]

which are (12). The interval width is

\[
 \left(\frac3{\sqrt2}-2\right)\sqrt N=c_0\sqrt N.
\]

One residue class modulo (L) has at most (c_0\sqrt N/L+1) members in
that interval. For any candidate (S), the polynomial

\[
 X^2-SX+N
\]

has integral roots exactly when (S^2-4N) is a nonnegative square with the
correct parity. Direct multiplication verifies the roots. This proves
(13)--(15) and the deterministic quasipolynomial cost.

The complete factorizations of (A) and (B) are needed by the order-state
updaters. The CRT search itself needs only their public integer values.

## 4. Torus equality screens

Multiplication in the public quadratic algebra is

\[
 (a+bw)(c+dw)=(ac+Dbd)+(ad+bc)w\pmod N.
\]

For two points (Y=y_0+y_1w) and (Z=z_0+z_1w), their reductions are
equal modulo a hidden prime (r) exactly when

\[
 y_0-z_0\equiv y_1-z_1\equiv0\pmod r.
\]

Thus

\[
 \gcd(N,y_0-z_0,y_1-z_1)
\]

is the product of the hidden prime components on which the two points are
equal. A proper value factors (N). On a no-factor run, every pair of
globally distinct stored points remains distinct in both local tori.

## 5. Closed torus fingerprints

Fix one hidden prime (r). Let

\[
 H_r=\langle G\rangle,
 \qquad
 K_r=\langle H_r,U_1,\ldots,U_t\rangle.
\]

Both are subgroups of the cyclic local torus. Define

\[
 \psi_r:K_r/H_r\longrightarrow T_D(\mathbf F_r),
 \qquad
 XH_r\longmapsto X^B.
\tag{2}
\]

This is well-defined. The cyclic group (K_r) contains the subgroup
(H_r) of order (B). The kernel of the (B)-power map on (K_r) is
the unique subgroup of order (B), hence it is exactly (H_r). Therefore
(\psi_r) is injective. Its image is generated by the local fingerprints
(U_i^B), so

\[
 K_r/H_r\cong\langle U_1^B,\ldots,U_t^B\rangle.
\tag{3}
\]

If the global breadth-first table closes and no factor was found, Section 4
shows that projection of the complete global table into either local torus
is injective. It is surjective onto the local fingerprint group by
construction. The projection is therefore an isomorphism in both
components. This proves (18).

The closed global group is cyclic. Factor (\kappa\le C), test powers in
the stored table, and retain a generator (Y=U^B) with a public predecessor
word (U). Under (3), the coset (UH_r) has exact order (\kappa) in both
local quotients.

The Harvey--Hittmeir/P154 primary-component construction uses only cyclic
local groups, public group multiplication and powering, known order
factorizations, and factor-first equality tests. All of those operations
exist in (T_D(R)) by Section 4. It therefore returns a proper factor or a
public torus element of exact order (B\kappa) in both local tori. No
comparison of hidden logarithms is needed for this state construction.
Hidden-log alignment remains necessary if the output contract asks for one
global presentation of the full named subgroup.

## 6. Capacity branch

If the search stores (C+1) global fingerprints without a proper equality
gcd, their projections are (C+1) distinct points in each local
fingerprint group. Equation (3) gives

\[
 |K_r/H_r|\ge C+1,
 \qquad
 |K_r|\ge B(C+1).
\]

This is an inequality, not an exact group order. The two local quotient
orders can be different and can have no common nontrivial divisor. No
integer obtained from the lower bound is therefore known to divide either
(N-1) or (N+1). It cannot enter the CRT systems of Section 2.

For the explicit example, the squares modulo (11) show
((5/11)=1), while the squares modulo (13) show ((5/13)=-1). Thus
((5/143)=-1). The Cayley denominator at (t=40) is

\[
 1-5t^2\equiv9\pmod {143},
\]

which is a unit, and direct substitution gives

\[
 U_5(40)=31+136w.
\]

Modulo (11), this is (9+4w). With the split root (w=4), its scalar
coordinate is (9+4\cdot4=3), which has order (5) in
(\mathbf F_{11}^\times).

Modulo (13), the point is (5+6w). Its powers satisfy

\[
 U^2=(10,8),
 \qquad
 U^4=(4,4),
 \qquad
 U^6=(5,7),
 \qquad
 U^7=(1,0).
\]

Since (7) is prime and (U\ne1), its local order is (7). Therefore
the first four powers are distinct in both components, but the complete
local generated orders are (5) and (7). This proves the capacity
counterexample.

For the general synchronized obstruction, suppose (m) divides both local
torus orders. Cyclicity supplies local points of exact order (m), and CRT
lifts them to one public coefficient pair modulo (N). The first (C+1)
powers are distinct in both components when (m>C). For odd (m), neither
local point is (-1). The inverse Cayley map is then defined locally, and
CRT gives one clean global Cayley parameter. Thus the natural one-parameter
torus chart does not itself exclude the obstruction.

## 7. Monotone potential and complexity

The public element (-1) has exact order two in every ordinary local unit
group and in every local norm-one torus. Start both states there. Every later
state order is a multiple of two. Combined with (3), this forces

\[
 \gcd(A,B)=2,
 \qquad
 L=AB/2
\]

on every no-factor branch.

Suppose an updater gives an exact strict ordinary growth
(A'=A\kappa). The new exact state also satisfies
(\gcd(A',B)=2). Hence

\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(A',B)
 =\frac{A\kappa B}{2}
 =\kappa L.
\]

The torus case is identical. This proves (22). The order-one caveat in the
statement is real: (A=2,B=1\to B'=2) is strict torus growth but leaves
(\operatorname{lcm}(A,B)=2). Initialization at order two removes exactly
this exception.

Each strict event has (\kappa\ge2). Starting at (L=2), after (k)
strict events (L\ge2^{k+1}). Therefore only (O(n)) strict events can
occur before the CRT threshold is met.

One torus breadth-first call uses (O(tC^2)) coefficient gcd comparisons
and (O(tC)) group edges. Factoring a closed (\kappa\le C), storing
predecessor words, and running the primary construction are
quasipolynomial under quasipolynomial numerical and transcript caps. An
(O(n)) number of such calls remains quasipolynomial. This cost statement
includes all retained source records, words, coefficients, and reset tables;
a cap on (C) alone is not sufficient.

## 8. Scope

The proof applies to distinct odd semiprimes. The sum threshold uses the
additional balance promise (p<q<2p). The two-class smaller-factor search
does not need balance.

The torus updater is an abstract extension of P154, not a stronger closure
dichotomy. The dual modulus is the new part. It depends on retaining two
separate exact common-order states. P140 rules out the tested direct
homomorphic transfer into the nonsplit torus, but it does not block this
separate-state combination.

No claim is made for prime powers, arbitrary nonsquarefree inputs, multiple
discriminants, a forced torus block source, or repeated source progress.
Capacity lower bounds do not trigger the CRT terminal rule.
