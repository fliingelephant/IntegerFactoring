# Hostile audit of F182

## Verdict

**PASS.** I found no correctness defect in the frozen statement or proof. The
two-menu filter is exact, the repeated-prime-power gcd semantics are valid,
all four types of cross-menu resultants are nonzero and satisfy the stated
bound, and the same-original-unit double-identity argument is sound.

## Imported interface

The proof needs only the rough branch of F181. For the chosen fixed numerical
QP cap (T), it supplies one public unit (w) such that, for every maximal
prime-power component (R_j),

\[
g_j=\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(w)>1,
\qquad \gcd(g_j,N)=1,
\qquad P^-(g_j)>T.
\]

This is exactly what F181 proves from the P160 hard branch. P160 permits any
fixed numerical QP cap above its earlier fixed caps. Thus one can choose the
F182 cap, or the maximum of it and those earlier caps. No stronger P160 or
F181 conclusion is used.

## Exact filter and repeated prime powers

Fix (ell^a\parallel g_j). The strict inequality (g_j<N<2^n) implies
(a<n). For (b=N+\delta),

\[
\ell\mid b\prod_{k=1}^K(b^k-1)
\]

holds exactly when either (ell\mid b), or (b) is a unit modulo (ell)
and (\operatorname{ord}_\ell(b)\le K). In the latter case the factor with
(k=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(b)) occurs. Therefore a primary factor
(ell^a) is removed exactly when one shift in the menu is easy for
(ell). If it is removed, the outer (n)-th power gives
(v_\ell(P_{\mathcal S}^n)\ge n>a). If it is not removed, its full
(ell^a) part survives. This proves the displayed exact order formula,
including primary multiplicities.

Let (R_j=p_j^{e_j}). Every filtered local order divides (g_j), so it is
coprime to (p_j). The reduction kernel

\[
(\mathbb Z/p_j^{e_j}\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow
(\mathbb Z/p_j\mathbb Z)^\times
\]

is a (p_j)-group. Its intersection with the cyclic group generated by the
filtered element is trivial. Hence the filtered element is (1) modulo
(p_j) if and only if it is (1) modulo all of (R_j). The gcd screen
therefore selects whole maximal prime-power components. In particular,
(H_{\mathcal S}=1) means that every local order is nontrivial, and a proper
screen value is a proper factor even when (N) is not squarefree.

## Cross-menu resultants

For (delta\in\mathcal A), (epsilon\in\mathcal B), put
(D=\epsilon-\delta). The exact range is

\[
3\le D\le 2L+1.
\]

The zero-index cases are valid and nonzero:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(k,l) & |\operatorname{Res}(F_{\delta,k},F_{\epsilon,l})|\\ \hline
(0,0) & D\\
(0,l),\ l>0 & D^l-1\\
(k,0),\ k>0 & |(-D)^k-1|.
\end{array}
\]

The gap (D\ge3) excludes zero in all three rows. For (k,l>0), a common
complex root would be on two radius-one circles whose centers are distance
(D>2) apart. Thus that resultant is also nonzero. Evaluating the second
polynomial at the roots (-\delta+u), (u^k=1), gives

\[
R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
=\prod_{u^k=1}|(D+u)^l-1|
<\bigl(2(D+1)^l\bigr)^k.
\]

If (h=\lceil\log_2(2L+2)\rceil), all four cases consequently satisfy

\[
0<R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
<2^{K(1+Kh)}
<2^{K(2+Kh)}=\widehat T\le T.
\]

For the mixed case (l=0<k), where odd (k) gives (D^k+1), the extra
factor two is still below the displayed coarse bound. Thus neither a zero
resultant nor a missed zero-index case escapes the roughness contradiction.

## Double identity and original-unit semantics

Both descendants are explicitly powers of the same F181 unit:

\[
z_{\mathcal A}=w^{P_{\mathcal A}^n},
\qquad
z_{\mathcal B}=w^{P_{\mathcal B}^n}.
\]

The second filter is not applied to (z_{\mathcal A}). This point is
essential and is stated in both the algorithm and the contradiction proof.

If both gcd screens were (N), choose any (ell^a\parallel g_j), which
exists because (g_j>1). The first identity forces
(ell\mid F_{\delta,k}(N)) for some (delta\in\mathcal A) and
(0\le k\le K). The second forces
(ell\mid F_{\epsilon,l}(N)) for some (epsilon\in\mathcal B) and
(0\le l\le K). Reduction modulo (ell), or the integral Sylvester
Bezout identity, then gives

\[
\ell\mid R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}.
\]

But F181 gives (ell>T), while the preceding section gives a positive
resultant smaller than (T). This is impossible. Therefore, after
(H_{\mathcal A}=N), a non-proper (H_{\mathcal B}) must be (1).

## Numerical cap and bit cost

Write (u=\log_2(n+1)). With fixed constants,

\[
K=O(u^c),
\qquad \log L=O(u^d),
\qquad
\log_2\widehat T=O(K+K^2\log L)=u^{O(1)}.
\]

Thus (widehat T) is an integer-valued numerical QP bound. Taking
(T=\widehat T), or taking the maximum with the earlier fixed P160 caps,
keeps the F181 construction uniformly QP. If (T) is allowed to be a
larger fixed QP, the last proof display should be read as
(log T=(\log n)^{O(1)}); the sharper
(O(K^2\log L)) estimate applies to the chosen threshold
(widehat T). This is a scope clarification, not a gap in the theorem.

For each shift,

\[
\log C_\delta
\le \left(1+\sum_{k=1}^K k\right)O(n+\log L)
=O\bigl(K^2(n+\log L)\bigr).
\]

Summing over the (L) shifts and multiplying by the outer power (n)
gives

\[
\log(P_{\mathcal S}^n)
=O\bigl(nLK^2(n+\log L)\bigr).
\]

At most two F182 exponents are built, so the factor two does not change this
bound. Product construction, modular powering, and gcd work are polynomial
in QP-size integers.

The displayed exponent bound is correctly the total for the conditional
F182 postprocessor. It does not include the already completed construction
of its input (w). If one counts the full P160/F181/F182 pipeline, F181 also
uses an exponent with (O(nT\log T)) bits (and P160 uses (O(n^2)) bits).
Those terms can be larger than the F182 display, but they are still QP and
do not change the claimed complexity class.

## Boundary

The proof certifies simultaneous long public action for every shift in one
returned menu. It does not identify which prime divisor of a surviving rough
local order carries that action, and it does not turn the conditional F181
branch into an all-input factoring algorithm. This matches the stated
boundary.
