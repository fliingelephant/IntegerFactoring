## 1. Consequences of the input certificates

Write \(g_j\) and \(d_j\) for the reductions modulo
\(R_j=p_j^{\alpha_j}\). Since \(g^M=1\pmod N\), each local order
\(r_j=\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(g_j)\) divides \(M\). If \(r_j<M\), choose a
prime \(\ell\mid M/r_j\). Then \(r_j\mid M/\ell\), so

\[
R_j\mid g^{M/\ell}-1.
\]

This contradicts \(\gcd(g^{M/\ell}-1,N)=1\). Hence every \(r_j=M\).

The quotient-order hypothesis says \(d_j^e\in H_j=\langle g_j\rangle\).
Every element of \(H_j\) has order dividing \(M\), so

\[
d_j^{eM}=1.
\]

CRT gives \(d^{eM}=1\pmod N\). Thus \(Q=eM\) is a known multiple of the
global order of \(d\).

## 2. Factor-first recovery of a common local order

Let \(r=\operatorname{ord}_N(d)\). During divisor stripping, maintain
\(r\mid m\), initially with \(m=Q\). A replacement \(m\leftarrow m/\ell\)
is made exactly when \(d^{m/\ell}=1\), so the invariant remains true. At
termination, suppose \(r<m\). A prime \(\ell\mid m/r\) would give
\(r\mid m/\ell\), contrary to the failed stripping test for \(\ell\).
Therefore the final \(m\) is the exact global order.

For each prime \(\ell\mid m\), put

\[
E_\ell=\gcd(d^{m/\ell}-1,N).
\]

The value \(E_\ell\) cannot equal \(N\), because that would make the global
order divide \(m/\ell\). Hence every \(E_\ell>1\) is a proper factor.

Assume all \(E_\ell=1\), and let \(m_j=\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(d_j)\).
Each \(m_j\mid m\). If \(m_j<m\), choose a prime
\(\ell\mid m/m_j\). Then

\[
R_j\mid d^{m/\ell}-1,
\]

which forces \(E_\ell>1\), a contradiction. Thus

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(d_j)=m
\quad\text{for every }j.
\]

This argument does not assume that \(N\) is squarefree. A gcd may contain a
whole CRT prime-power component and a partial power from another component;
any value strictly between one and \(N\) is still a valid factor.

Also, because \(m\mid eM\) and both \(e\) and \(M\) are coprime to \(N\),
\(\gcd(m,N)=1\). The separate gcd test on \(m\) is therefore harmless but
vacuous in this exact formulation.

## 3. The exact lcm identity

Fix a component and let

\[
K_j=\langle g_j,d_j\rangle.
\]

The unit group modulo an odd prime power is cyclic. In particular, its
subgroups are cyclic and its subgroup of each possible order is unique.

First, \(H_j\subseteq K_j\), and the image of \(K_j\) in the quotient by
\(H_j\) is generated by \(d_jH_j\). That image has order \(e\). Therefore

\[
|K_j|=|H_j|e=Me.
\]

Second, in a cyclic group the join of subgroups of orders \(M\) and \(m\)
has order \(\operatorname{lcm}(M,m)\). Consequently

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{lcm}(M,m)=Me}.
\]

No coprimality between \(M\) and \(m\) is used. Their shared prime powers
are handled by the lcm.

It follows immediately that the new order \(L=Me\) is strictly larger than
\(M\) exactly when \(e\ge2\). For \(e=1\), \(L=M\).

## 4. The public prime-primary word

For a prime \(\ell\mid L\), write

\[
a=v_\ell(M),\qquad b=v_\ell(m),\qquad t=\max(a,b).
\]

If \(a\ge b\), the order formula for a power gives, in every component,

\[
\operatorname{ord}\!\left(g_j^{M/\ell^a}\right)
=\frac{M}{\gcd(M,M/\ell^a)}=\ell^a=\ell^t.
\]

If \(b>a\), the same calculation for \(d_j\) gives exact order
\(\ell^b=\ell^t\). Thus the stated public choice \(y_\ell\) has exact local
order \(\ell^t\).

The elements \(y_\ell\) commute, and their orders are pairwise coprime. The
order of their product is therefore the product of their orders. Hence the
public word

\[
h=\prod_{\ell\mid L}y_\ell
\]

satisfies

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(h)=\prod_{\ell\mid L}\ell^{v_\ell(L)}=L
\]

for every \(j\). CRT then gives \(\operatorname{ord}_N(h)=L\). The empty
product convention also covers \(L=1\). The construction uses only known
positive exponents and needs no local logarithm.

## 5. Why the public gcd checks really equal one

The equality \(h^L=1\pmod N\) follows from the local orders. For the stronger
gcd checks, it is not enough in general to know only that \(h\) has order
\(L\) modulo \(p_j^{\alpha_j}\): a nonidentity modulo a prime power can still
be one modulo its underlying prime.

Here the missing fact is supplied by the input coprimality. Since

\[
L=Me,\qquad \gcd(M,N)=\gcd(e,N)=1,
\]

we have \(p_j\nmid L\). The kernel of

\[
(\mathbb Z/p_j^{\alpha_j}\mathbb Z)^\times
\longrightarrow (\mathbb Z/p_j\mathbb Z)^\times
\]

is a \(p_j\)-group. Its intersection with the order-\(L\) group
\(\langle h\rangle\) is trivial. Reduction modulo \(p_j\) therefore preserves
the exact order \(L\). For each prime \(\ell\mid L\),

\[
h^{L/\ell}\not\equiv1\pmod {p_j}.
\]

No prime divisor of \(N\) divides \(h^{L/\ell}-1\), and hence

\[
\gcd(h^{L/\ell}-1,N)=1.
\]

Together with the known complete factorization of \(L\), these checks give
the next certified common-order state.

## 6. Local equality and the global subgroup boundary

Locally, \(h_j\in K_j\) and both \(\langle h_j\rangle\) and \(K_j\) have
order \(L\). Therefore

\[
\langle h_j\rangle=\langle g_j,d_j\rangle.
\]

Globally, only \(\langle h\rangle\subseteq\langle g,d\rangle\) follows.
CRT embeds the latter group as a subdirect product of the local cyclic
groups. Different relations in different components can make this subdirect
product larger and noncyclic. A single element of order \(L\) then cannot
represent the full named subgroup.

This also explains the \(e=1\) branch. The identity gives
\(\operatorname{lcm}(M,m)=M\), so \(m\mid M\), and \(d_j\in\langle g_j\rangle\)
for every \(j\). It does not supply one exponent \(a\) for which
\(d=g^a\pmod N\). The local exponents can differ.

## 7. Independent checks of the separating certificates

### 7.1 The \(N=91\) order-screen separator

Here \(91=7\cdot13\), and \(g=-1\) has common order two. Modulo seven,
\(d=30\) reduces to \(2\), which has order three. Modulo thirteen it reduces
to \(4\), with \(4^3=-1\) and order six. Relative to \(\langle-1\rangle\),
the exact quotient order is three in both components, while the global order
is \(m=\operatorname{lcm}(3,6)=6\).

Moreover, \(d^3=1\pmod7\) and \(d^3=-1\pmod{13}\). Thus

\[
\gcd(d^3-1,91)=7.
\]

The factor-first screen stops before construction of \(h\). This example is
therefore not a counterexample to the no-factor branch.

### 7.2 The \(N=341\) global-subgroup separator

The CRT descriptions are correct:

\[
202=(2^2,27^2),\qquad 277=(2,27^3)
\]

modulo \(11\) and \(31\), respectively. The element \(2\) has order ten
modulo \(11\). For \(27\) modulo \(31\), one has \(27^5=-1\), so it also
has order ten. Therefore \(g\) has common order five, \(d\) has common
order ten, and the local quotient order is \(e=2\).

The local alignment exponents differ:

\[
d^2=g\pmod {11},\qquad d^2=g^3\pmod {31}.
\]

Directly, \(d^2=4\pmod{341}\), and then \(d^5=-1\pmod{341}\). The only
order screens for \(m=10\) are consequently

\[
\gcd(d^5-1,341)=\gcd(-2,341)=1,
\qquad
\gcd(d^2-1,341)=\gcd(3,341)=1.
\]

For \(L=10\), the primary construction selects \(d^5\) for the 2-part and
\(g\) for the 5-part. Hence

\[
h=gd^5=-g=139\pmod{341}.
\]

Locally this is \(t_i^7\), which has order ten in both components.

To determine the full generated group, express powers in
\(C_{10}\times C_{10}\). Powers of \(d\) have exponent pairs
\((k,3k)\), while powers of \(g\) have pairs \((2r,2r)\). Equality would
give \(2k=0\pmod {10}\). The case \(k=5\) cannot equal an even first
coordinate, so only the identity lies in
\(\langle d\rangle\cap\langle g\rangle\). Thus

\[
|\langle g,d\rangle|=10\cdot5=50,
\qquad
|\langle h\rangle|=10.
\]

Finally,

\[
\gcd(d^2-g,341)=\gcd(4-202,341)=11.
\]

This proves both strict global inclusion and the extra factor opportunity
available to alignment after all order screens pass.

### 7.3 The \(e=1\) separator

With the same \(N\) and \(g\), take \(d=4\). Modulo \(11\), this is
\(g^1\). Modulo \(31\), it is \(27^6=(27^2)^3=g^3\). Thus \(d\) has
common local order five and relative order one, but no common global
exponent relative to \(g\). The lcm remains five and the public primary
choice can simply return the order-five state generated by \(g\). Yet

\[
\gcd(d-g,341)=\gcd(4-202,341)=11.
\]

So lack of order growth neither proves global membership nor removes the
possible factor from alignment.

## 8. Exact relations and F164-style rank accounting

An integer-relation row asserting \(d^e=g^a\pmod N\) is valid only if the
same \(a\) works in every CRT component. The \(N=341\) separator has local
values \(a=1\) and \(a=3\), so the quotient-order statement alone cannot
produce such a row.

Likewise, replacing the coordinates for \(g\) and \(d\) by one coordinate
for \(h\) would assert that the discarded generators are global powers of
\(h\). The same separator disproves this: the named group has order 50,
while \(\langle h\rangle\) has order 10. Therefore rank or volume accounting
that concerns the full global named subgroup still requires alignment, or
an independent proof of global power relations.

Keeping all old words and using \((h,L)\) only as the base for later local
relative-order work is sound, because that discards no global information.
Later alignment can distinguish a common global exponent from mismatched
local exponents and can expose a factor in the latter case.

## 9. Cost and remaining scope

Every successful stripping step divides the current multiple by at least
two, so there are at most \(\log_2 Q\) successes. After a test for a prime
\(\ell\) fails, later divisions only make the tested exponent a divisor of
its old value; that prime need not be tested unsuccessfully again. There is
at most one final failure per distinct prime, also at most \(\log_2 Q\).
Thus divisor stripping uses \(O(\log Q)\) modular-power tests.

The order screens use one test per distinct prime of \(m\). Constructing and
certifying \(h\) uses one modular power per distinct prime of \(L\), plus
modular multiplications and gcds.

Because \(M\) is a local unit order, \(M\le\varphi(R_j)<R_j\le N\). Also
\(m\mid eM\) and \(L=eM\). Every exponent therefore has

\[
O(\log N+\log e)
\]

bits. Modular powering and gcd are deterministic polynomial-bit-cost
operations in these lengths. Given the complete factorizations and the
stated quasipolynomial cap on \(e\), the full update has deterministic
quasipolynomial bit cost.

The result begins only after a public \(d\) and a certified common relative
order \(e\) are already available. It neither constructs \(d\), forces that
first common return, nor ensures progress when \(e=1\). It also retains no
exact global relation unless alignment is performed. Therefore it proves
the stated factor-or-next-common-order update, but not a released block or
an all-input factoring algorithm.
