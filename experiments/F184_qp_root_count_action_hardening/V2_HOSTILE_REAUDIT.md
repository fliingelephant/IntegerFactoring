## Hostile checks

### 1. Complete primary deletion

Fix `\ell^e\parallel g_{t,j}`. The order-of-a-power identity gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(w_t^{C_a^n})
=\frac{g_{t,j}}{\gcd(g_{t,j},C_a^n)}.
\]

The proof's claim that `\ell\mid C_a` deletes the complete `\ell^e` part is
valid. Indeed,

\[
\ell^e\le g_{t,j}\le g_{0,j}<R_j\le N<2^n,
\]

so `e<n`. If `\ell\mid C_a`, then
`v_\ell(C_a^n)\ge n>e`; if `\ell\nmid C_a`, that valuation is zero. Thus the
whole primary part is deleted exactly in the first case. No factorization of
`C_a` or knowledge of `\ell` is used.

For

\[
F_K(X)=X\prod_{k=1}^K(X^k-1),
\]

the equivalence

\[
\ell\mid F_K(a)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\equiv0\pmod\ell
\ \text{or}\ 
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a)\le K
\]

is exact. In the unit case, an exponent `k\le K` with `a^k=1` exists exactly
when the multiplicative order is at most `K`.

### 2. Block cardinality and root count

Let `B=D+1`. The endpoints in V2 are

\[
2+tB\quad\text{and}\quad1+(t+1)B.
\]

Their inclusive cardinality is exactly `B=D+1`. Consecutive blocks are
disjoint, and their union is the integer interval

\[
\{2,\ldots,1+M(D+1)\}.
\]

Every prime in a current local order exceeds `T`, while every candidate is
strictly below `T`. Hence the candidates in one block give `D+1` distinct
residue classes modulo each such prime.

The polynomial `F_K` is monic and has degree

\[
1+\sum_{k=1}^K k=D.
\]

It remains a nonzero degree-`D` polynomial over every `\mathbb F_\ell`, even
if some factors have repeated roots. Therefore it cannot vanish at all
`D+1` candidates.

If no proper gcd occurs and no candidate has `H_{t,a}=1`, every candidate
must have `H_{t,a}=N`. For any component with nontrivial current order, choose
a prime `\ell` in that order. Global identity then implies
`g_{t,j}\mid C_a^n`, hence `F_K(a)=0\pmod\ell`, for every candidate. This is
the forbidden set of `D+1` roots. Thus a factor or an `H=1` candidate exists
at every stage.

### 3. Arbitrary prime powers

Write `R_j=p_j^{\nu_j}`. Every later local order divides `g_{0,j}` and is
coprime to `p_j`. The kernel of

\[
(\mathbb Z/R_j\mathbb Z)^\times
\longrightarrow\mathbb F_{p_j}^\times
\]

is a `p_j`-group. It intersects the cyclic subgroup generated by every
descendant only in the identity. Reduction is therefore injective on that
subgroup, and

\[
z\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z\equiv1\pmod {R_j}.
\]

Consequently, `H=1` leaves every local order nontrivial. This remains true
for a pure prime power and for composites with repeated prime factors.
Grouping equal rational primes into maximal prime powers makes the `R_j`
pairwise coprime, as required by the gcd argument.

### 4. Preservation of all selected actions

Orders only decrease. Hence a prime `\ell\mid g_{M,j}` survived every
earlier selected exponent. At stage `t`, survival gives
`\ell\nmid C_{a_t}`. Therefore

\[
\ell\nmid a_t,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a_t)>K.
\]

This holds for every selected stage. It also implies
`\gcd(a_t,g_{M,j})=1`, so exponentiation by `a_t` is genuinely an
automorphism on the final cyclic local subgroup. Disjoint blocks make the
selected bases distinct as public integers. Since all bases are below every
surviving `\ell`, they are also distinct modulo each surviving order prime.

### 5. Uniform QP accounting

Fixed numerical QP bounds are closed under the sums and products used here.
Thus

\[
D=1+K(K+1)/2,
\qquad
M(D+1)
\]

are numerical QP. The largest base is numerical QP, and

\[
\operatorname{bitlen}(C_a)
\le D\log_2 a+O(1),
\qquad
\operatorname{bitlen}(C_a^n)
\le nD\log_2 a+O(1),
\]

which are also numerical QP. There are exactly `M(D+1)` candidate tests.
Constructing the `K` powers and their product, binary modular exponentiation,
and an `n`-bit gcd all use QP bit time and QP-size intermediate integers.
The product of these QP operation counts remains QP.

The postprocessor itself does not iterate up to `T`. For the combined
F181--F184 use, one may choose the canonical cap

\[
T=2+M(D+1),
\]

which is numerical QP and satisfies the strict inequality. Hence the theorem
does not hide a super-QP roughness call.

### 6. Scope and attempted overreadings

The proof never factors `C_a`, a resultant, or an annihilator. It uses only
public integer construction, modular powering, and gcds. The selected bases
can all generate the same cyclic action direction. The proof supplies no
rank or independence statement, no full factorization of a local order, and
no guarantee of a proper gcd after the `M` stages. Its surviving output is
only the rough descendant with the stated simultaneous long-action
properties. The exact-boundary paragraph states these limitations correctly.
