# Hostile audit of F170 dual ordinary/torus order closure

## Verdict

**PASS, in the narrow stated scope.**

F170 proves a conditional decoder and terminal rule for a distinct odd
semiprime. It does not prove a public source, repeated progress, or a
quasipolynomial factoring algorithm.

## 1. Scope attack

The theorem assumes

\[
N=pq,\qquad 3\le p<q,
\]

with distinct odd primes. The arbitrary-semiprime CRT search does not use a
balance assumption. Only the sharper sum search uses `q<2p`.

The proof does not extend its torus argument to prime powers or to more than
two hidden prime components. It does not claim that it does. It also does
not claim complete factorization from an arbitrary composite input.

This scope is internally consistent.

## 2. Local torus structure

Fix an odd prime `r` not dividing `D`.

- If `D` is a square modulo `r`, then

  \[
  \mathbf F_r[w]/(w^2-D)\cong \mathbf F_r\times\mathbf F_r.
  \]

  The norm-one subgroup is isomorphic to
  \(\mathbf F_r^\times\), so it is cyclic of order `r-1`.

- If `D` is a nonsquare modulo `r`, the algebra is
  \(\mathbf F_{r^2}\). Its norm-one subgroup is the kernel of the norm
  from the cyclic group \(\mathbf F_{r^2}^\times\) to
  \(\mathbf F_r^\times\). It is cyclic of order `r+1`.

Thus

\[
|T_D(\mathbf F_r)|=r-\left(\frac Dr\right)
\]

in both cases. This validates the cyclic-group dependency in the quotient
updater. The split algebra has zero divisors, but this causes no defect:
`1,w` remains a vector-space basis, and every norm-one point is a unit with
inverse given by conjugation.

Since the Jacobi symbol of `D` is minus one,

\[
\left(\frac Dp\right)=\epsilon,
\qquad
\left(\frac Dq\right)=-\epsilon.
\]

The two local torus orders are therefore exactly `p-epsilon` and
`q+epsilon`, as claimed.

## 3. Two-coordinate equality attack

Let

\[
Y=y_0+y_1w,\qquad Z=z_0+z_1w.
\]

Their reductions are equal in the local quadratic algebra modulo a hidden
prime `r` if and only if both coefficient differences vanish modulo `r`.
For `N=pq`, it follows that

\[
\gcd(N,y_0-z_0,y_1-z_1)
\]

is `N`, `p`, `q`, or `1` according to whether equality holds in both, only
the `p` component, only the `q` component, or neither component. Hence a
proper value is a factor. On a no-factor run, every pair of globally
distinct stored points is distinct in both local tori.

A one-coordinate gcd can sometimes factor even when the two points are not
equal in either local algebra. F170 does not use that extra opportunity.
Omitting it does not weaken the injectivity argument.

The same joint-coordinate test must be used against `(1,0)` in every
factor-first order screen. This is the exact torus replacement for the
scalar gcd in P150/P154.

## 4. Quotient fingerprint attack

Fix one hidden prime `r`. Let

\[
H_r=\langle G\rangle,\qquad |H_r|=B,
\]

and let `K_r` be generated by `H_r` and the supplied torus words. Because
the ambient local torus is cyclic, `K_r` is cyclic and has a unique subgroup
of order `B`, namely `H_r`.

The map

\[
K_r/H_r\longrightarrow T_D(\mathbf F_r),
\qquad XH_r\longmapsto X^B
\]

is well-defined. Since `B` divides `|K_r|`, the kernel of the `B`-power map
on `K_r` has exactly `B` elements. It is `H_r`. The map is therefore
injective, and its image is precisely the group generated by the local
fingerprints `U_i^B`.

If the global breadth-first table closes without a factor, its projection
to each local fingerprint group is injective by Section 3 and surjective by
generation. Thus both projections are isomorphisms. In particular,

\[
Q_p\cong F\cong Q_q,
\qquad |Q_p|=|Q_q|=\kappa.
\]

Although a subgroup of a product of two cyclic groups need not be cyclic,
the closed global group `F` is cyclic here because it injects into either
cyclic local fingerprint group. This resolves the most direct cyclicity
attack.

A generator `Y=U^B` of `F` has order `kappa` in each local fingerprint
group. Hence the coset of `U` has exact order `kappa` in both local
quotients.

## 5. Alignment-free common-order lift

The P150 lift remains valid for the torus. Here is the complete reduction.

From the quotient order,

\[
U^{B\kappa}=1
\]

in both local tori. The factorization of `B*kappa` is known. Divisor
stripping computes the exact global order `m` of `U`. For every prime
`ell` dividing `m`, compare `U^(m/ell)` with `(1,0)` by the joint-coordinate
gcd from Section 3. A proper result factors `N`. If every result is one,
then `U` has exact order `m` in both local tori.

Inside either cyclic local torus,

\[
|\langle G,U\rangle|=B\kappa
\]

by the exact quotient order. The same group has order
`lcm(B,m)`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(B,m)=B\kappa.
\]

For each prime dividing this lcm, take the corresponding primary component
from whichever of `G` or `U` has the larger primary order. The product of
these public components has exact order `B*kappa` in both local tori. This
uses only public multiplication, powering, known factorizations, and the
joint-coordinate factor screens. It does not compare the hidden logarithms
in a relation of the form `U^kappa=G^a`.

Thus F170 correctly separates two contracts:

1. hidden-log alignment is not needed to construct the next exact
   common-order state; and
2. alignment is still needed for its extra factor channel, an explicit
   global relation, or compression of the complete named subgroup.

This matches the narrow P150/P154 interface.

## 6. Capacity branch attack

If the table stores `C+1` points without a proper equality gcd, those points
remain distinct in both local fingerprint groups. Therefore

\[
|Q_p|,|Q_q|\ge C+1,
\qquad
|K_p|,|K_q|\ge B(C+1).
\]

Nothing in this argument gives an exact common divisor of the two quotient
orders. A lower bound cannot enter the later CRT congruences.

I independently recomputed the public example

\[
N=143=11\cdot13,\quad D=5,\quad t=40.
\]

The Cayley denominator is `9 mod 143`, and its gcd with `143` is one. The
point is

\[
U=31+136w,
\qquad 31^2-5\cdot136^2\equiv1\pmod {143}.
\]

Its local orders are five modulo `11` and seven modulo `13`. The four
global points `1,U,U^2,U^3` are pairwise unequal in both local components,
and every joint-coordinate comparison has gcd one. Thus the cap `C=3`
returns capacity although the two local generated orders have no common
nontrivial divisor.

The example is written with the identity state `B=1`. This is sufficient
for the general capacity claim. It also persists after the pipeline's
order-two initialization: with `G=-1` and `B=2`, the fingerprints are
powers of `U^2`, which still have local orders five and seven. Therefore the
capacity obstruction is not an artifact of the order-one state.

The general Cayley obstruction is also sound. An odd-order local point
cannot equal `-1`. For a norm-one point `(a,b)` other than `-1`, the inverse
Cayley parameter is `t=b/(a+1)`. CRT combines the two local parameters, and
the resulting global Cayley denominator is a unit. Thus a one-parameter
Cayley source does not prohibit synchronized large cyclic quotients.

## 7. Exact divisibility and orthogonality

An ordinary state of exact common order `A` satisfies

\[
A\mid p-1,\qquad A\mid q-1.
\]

Hence `p` and `q` are both `1 mod A`, and `A` divides `N-1`.

A torus state of exact common order `B` satisfies

\[
B\mid p-\epsilon,\qquad B\mid q+\epsilon.
\]

Hence

\[
p\equiv\epsilon\pmod B,
\qquad q\equiv-\epsilon\pmod B,
\]

so `N` is `-1 mod B` and `B` divides `N+1`.

It follows that

\[
\gcd(A,B)\mid\gcd(N-1,N+1)=2.
\]

The public element `-1` has exact order two in every ordinary local unit
group and in every local torus over an odd field. If both channels start at
`-1`, all later exact orders remain even. Consequently

\[
\gcd(A,B)=2,
\qquad L=\operatorname{lcm}(A,B)=AB/2
\]

on every no-factor branch.

Suppose an exact update changes `A` to `A*kappa`. The new valid exact state
still divides `N-1`, while `B` divides `N+1`, so its gcd with `B` is again
exactly two. Therefore

\[
L'=\frac{A\kappa B}{2}=\kappa L.
\]

The torus update is identical. Thus every strict update multiplies `L` by
its exact update factor. The order-two initialization is essential: an
order-one channel can grow from one to two without increasing an lcm that
already contains two.

## 8. Generalized CRT attack

Let `h=gcd(A,B)` and `L=lcm(A,B)`. Since `h` divides two, each system

\[
x\equiv1\pmod A,
\qquad x\equiv e\pmod B,
\qquad e\in\{1,-1\},
\]

is consistent. If `h=2`, all three residues are odd. Each system gives one
public class modulo `L`. The smaller prime `p` belongs to the class with
`e=epsilon`.

One class has at most `sqrt(N)/L+1` representatives in
`3 <= x < sqrt(N)`. Enumerating both signs therefore uses at most

\[
2\left(\frac{\sqrt N}{L}+1\right)
\]

candidates. Duplicate sign classes when `B=1` or `B=2` only reduce this
count. If `L >= sqrt(N)/Q(n)`, the search has at most `2(Q(n)+1)` gcd
tests and contains `p`. No balance promise is used.

For the balanced search, put `S=p+q` and `x=q/p`. Under `1<x<2`,

\[
\frac{S^2}{N}=x+x^{-1}+2
\]

lies strictly between `4` and `9/2`. Hence

\[
2\sqrt N<S<\frac3{\sqrt2}\sqrt N.
\]

Also

\[
S\equiv2\pmod A,
\qquad S\equiv0\pmod B.
\]

These residues agree modulo `h`, so they define one public class modulo
`L`. The interval width is

\[
c_0\sqrt N,
\qquad c_0=\frac3{\sqrt2}-2.
\]

The constant satisfies `c_0 < 1/8`; equivalently,
`9/2 < (17/8)^2`. Thus the exact count is at most

\[
\frac{c_0\sqrt N}{L}+1.
\]

The condition `L >= c_0*sqrt(N)/Q(n)` gives at most `Q(n)+1`
candidates. The rational condition `L >= sqrt(N)/(8Q(n))` is slightly
stronger numerically and is therefore sufficient.

For each sum candidate, an exact integer-square test on `S^2-4N` and direct
multiplication verify the factors. Irrational interval endpoints and `c_0`
do not create an implementation issue; equivalent integer inequalities can
be tested with integer squares.

The orientation-free sum class is valid only for the balanced count. The
two-sign smaller-prime search is the valid arbitrary-semiprime terminal
rule.

## 9. Monotone event count

The initialized potential is `L=2`. Each strict exact update has multiplier
at least two. After `k` strict updates,

\[
L\ge2^{k+1}.
\]

The arbitrary terminal threshold has logarithm at most `n/2` plus a
quasipolynomial correction in the favorable direction. Thus `O(n)` strict
growth events suffice to reach it. A factor event terminates immediately.

This count does not bound inert or capacity rounds. F170 correctly makes the
progress law a missing source theorem.

## 10. Bit complexity attack

All state orders and coefficient residues have `O(n)` bits. The complete
factorizations of `A` and `B` are part of the state. A closed table has at
most `C` points. It considers `O(tC)` generator edges and performs
`O(tC^2)` joint-coordinate gcd comparisons. Its predecessor words have
length at most `C` in the direct representation.

Factoring `kappa <= C` by trial division is quasipolynomial when `C` is
quasipolynomial. Divisor stripping, primary-component selection, modular
torus multiplication, powering, generalized CRT, gcd tests, and integer
square tests also have quasipolynomial bit cost under the stated numerical
and encoding caps.

Multiplying this work by `O(n)` strict updates preserves quasipolynomial
cost. A numerical cap on `C` alone would not suffice. The candidate
explicitly also caps source records, coefficients, tables, predecessor
words, provenance, and the full retained transcript.

This is a conditional cost statement. It does not bound an unlimited
sequence of inert or capacity rounds.

## 11. Nonclaims and surviving gap

The proof establishes the following implication only:

\[
\text{QP-bounded exact growth transcript}
\Longrightarrow
\text{eventual CRT factor search in QP time}.
\]

It does not establish the antecedent. In particular, it proves none of the
following:

- a public torus-native block source;
- one strict closure per round;
- exclusion of synchronized capacity;
- hidden-log alignment for retained generators;
- a useful relation lattice or non-global square root;
- an all-input semiprime splitter;
- complete factorization of arbitrary composites.

The exact remaining theorem is source-side: force a factor or enough exact
common-order growth events while keeping the complete transcript
quasipolynomial.

## 12. Nonfatal wording notes

1. In `PROOF.md`, the phrase "the stronger balanced threshold (14)" can be
   read backwards. The balanced condition is numerically lower than the
   arbitrary-semiprime threshold and is therefore an easier threshold under
   the stronger balance promise. The displayed inequalities themselves are
   correct.
2. The torus form of the P150 certification is invoked abstractly. For an
   implementation, every scalar equality gcd in that interface must be
   replaced by the joint-coordinate gcd against `(1,0)`. Section 5 above
   reconstructs the required operation. This is an exposition note, not a
   mathematical gap.

No source or success theorem should be inferred from this PASS verdict.
