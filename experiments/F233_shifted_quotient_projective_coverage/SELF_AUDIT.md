# F233 self-audit

## Verdict

The candidate survives the author's first-principles audit.  Its exact
scope is a projective-incidence theorem and a residue-level obstruction.  It
does not claim that every avoiding residue is realized by an infinite family
of balanced zero-defect semiprimes.

## Checks

1. Even `u` remains a valid zero-defect quotient multiplier because
   `uN=(uH)B+u` and only `0<u<B` is needed.
2. The gap signs in (8) were checked separately at `p` and `q`.  Symmetric
   shifts make the two ratio covers equal.
3. The product in (10) is squarefree.  No unsupported higher-valuation
   divisibility of the linear gap integer is claimed.
4. The residual formula uses `ell^e`, because one exposed occurrence is
   deliberately raised to exponent `n` in the word.
5. The progress formula is quoted only after direct gcd screening.  A
   deterministic direct factor is not conflated with projected-stage
   probability.
6. The pigeonhole proof uses all multipliers `1,...,U`, not only odd ones.
   This is a valid enlargement of the zero-defect quotient bank.
7. The coverage implication uses the sufficient condition
   `ell<=C(U+1)`.  The avoidance implication uses the independent counting
   threshold `ell-1>2CU`; no false converse is asserted in the gap between.
8. Projective uniqueness needs `ell` not dividing `H` and `ell>2UC`; both
   are explicit.
9. The comparison with a smooth cutoff concerns residue-independent
   guaranteed prime support only.  It does not say that actual
   factor-correlated quotient incidences never help.
10. The recursive cost remains conditional on the missing all-input
    dispatcher and is not presented as a completed recurrence for factoring.

## Exact remaining risk for hostile audit

The main points to attack are the circular pigeonhole constant, the sign
identification in the two hidden orientations, and whether slope
deduplication can lose a large prime through a scalar coefficient.  A fresh
hostile audit and then a statement-only reconstruction are required before
promotion.
