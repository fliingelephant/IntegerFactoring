# F245 self-audit

## Mathematical checks

1. The zero inverse quotient occurs at (u=1). It is replaced by one before
   entering a multiplicative word. This merges two atoms at most and explains
   the factor two in (1).
2. The difference word replaces exact equality by one. Therefore a modular
   collision is necessary, not equivalent, and the upper bound has the safe
   direction.
3. The adaptive statement requires every new seed to remain conditionally
   uniform after the full past. Marginal uniformity alone is insufficient.
4. The complete-bank union bound covers arbitrary later selection and
   positive powers. It does not cover sums, polynomial combinations, or
   coefficients carrying a marker independently.
5. The F244 family supplies the same four markers and the pathwise
   signed-power avoidance. No new prime-family assumption is introduced.
6. The divisor maximum is taken below (N^2), exactly the range of
   (Nk+1). Its maximal-order estimate is subexponential in the input bit
   length, not polynomial.
7. The clean P208 upper bound uses only that one exponential marker survives
   on each side. Incidental sampler gcd exits are part of F244's scoped
   exponential bound and are not re-proved here.
8. In the Hilbert--90 formulas, (g) is the full signed integer norm, not
   only its canonical residue. Thus \(\widetilde d=(gv-1)/N\) is integral
   even when (g\notin[0,N)).
9. Equations (5)--(6) do not imply that nonlinear coordinate carries inherit
   the uniform inverse-quotient upper bound. The statement explicitly leaves
   that source open.

## Scope

- Distinct odd semiprimes only.
- Proof-only; no computation.
- Fresh conditionally uniform inverse seeds only.
- Multiplicative words generated by quotient values, pairwise differences,
  signed powers, the square baseline, and common-order lcm data.
- Biased feedback, descent trajectories, nonlinear carry transforms, full
  relation decoders, and arbitrary integer words remain open.
