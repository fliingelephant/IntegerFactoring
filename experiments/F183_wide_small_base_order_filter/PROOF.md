# Proof of the F183 wide small-base order filter

## 1. Exact primary filter

Fix a hidden component \(R_j\) and a prime power
\(\ell^e\parallel g_j\). Since

\[
\ell^e\le g_j<N<2^n,
\]

one has \(e<n\).

For a public base \(a\), the prime \(\ell\) divides \(C_a\) exactly when
either

\[
\ell\mid a,
\tag{1}
\]

or \(a\) is a unit modulo \(\ell\) and

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a)\le K.
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, (2) is equivalent to \(\ell\mid a^k-1\) for at least one
\(1\le k\le K\). Therefore \(\ell\mid P\) exactly when (1) or (2) holds
for at least one menu base.

Whenever \(\ell\mid P\), the outer \(n\)-th power gives

\[
v_\ell(P^n)\ge n>e,
\tag{3}
\]

so the complete \(\ell^e\) part is deleted. If \(\ell\nmid P\), that
primary part is preserved. The order-of-a-power formula now gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z)=h_j,
\tag{4}
\]

with \(h_j\) exactly as in (8) of the statement.

Because every \(\ell\mid g_j\) satisfies \(\ell>T\ge L+1\), the nonunit
alternative (1) cannot actually occur on the F181 rough input. Its explicit
inclusion in \(C_a\) makes the filter formula valid without using that
simplification.

## 2. The gcd branches for arbitrary prime powers

Every \(h_j\) divides \(g_j\), so it is coprime to the rational prime below
\(R_j\). Reduction from \((\mathbb Z/R_j\mathbb Z)^\times\) to the
corresponding prime field has a prime-power kernel. It is injective on the
subgroup generated by \(z\). Consequently,

\[
z\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z\equiv1\pmod {R_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
h_j=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus an intermediate \(H\) is a proper factor even when \(N\) has repeated
prime factors. If \(H=1\), every \(h_j\) is nontrivial. Its prime support
is a subset of the support of \(g_j\), so

\[
P^-(h_j)>T
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
h_j>T.
\tag{6}
\]

Equation (4) then gives every assertion in the hard descendant branch.

## 3. Global identity gives a factored exact-order interface

Suppose \(H=N\). Equation (5) gives \(h_j=1\) for every hidden component,
so

\[
g_j\mid P^n
\qquad(1\le j\le s).
\tag{7}
\]

The complete prime factorization of \(P^n\) is public. Start with
\(M=P^n\). For every prime \(r\mid M\), repeatedly compute

\[
D_r=\gcd(w^{M/r}-1,N).
\tag{8}
\]

If \(D_r=N\), replace \(M\) by \(M/r\). If \(1<D_r<N\), return the
factor. If \(D_r=1\), retain that copy of \(r\) and continue with the other
prime divisors.

Assume no proper gcd occurs. For every prime \(r\mid M\), the final
one-valued test says that no hidden local order divides \(M/r\). Since all
local orders divide \(M\), every \(g_j\) has the full \(r\)-adic valuation
of \(M\). Hence

\[
g_j=M
\qquad(1\le j\le s).
\tag{9}
\]

This is one exact common local order. F181 roughness makes it large:

\[
M=g_j\ge P^-(g_j)>T.
\tag{10}
\]

The returned state is \((w,M)\), not the identity descendant \(z\).

## 4. Complete factorization and total QP cost

For \(2\le a\le L+1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log_2C_a
&=\log_2a+\sum_{k=1}^K\log_2(a^k-1)\\
&=O(K^2\log L).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Trial division through \(\sqrt{C_a}\) therefore costs

\[
2^{O(K^2\log L)}
\tag{12}
\]

bit operations, including polynomial factors for integer divisions. There
are exactly \(L\) values \(C_a\), so all their complete factorizations cost

\[
L\,2^{O(K^2\log L)}
=2^{(\log n)^{O(1)}}.
\tag{13}
\]

Repeated rational primes can occur inside one \(C_a\) and across different
\(C_a\). Summing all publicly obtained valuations gives the exact
factorization of \(P\); no coprimality assumption is used.

The product has

\[
\log_2P=O(LK^2\log L)
\tag{14}
\]

bits, and the exponent \(P^n\) has

\[
O(nLK^2\log L)
\tag{15}
\]

bits. It can be built explicitly with a product tree and integer powering.
Binary modular powering is polynomial in (15) and in \(n\).

The number of prime-division attempts in factor-first stripping is at most
the total prime multiplicity of \(P^n\), which is bounded by
\(\log_2(P^n)\). Every tested exponent has at most the bit length in (15).
Thus the complete stripping stage is also QP.

Since \(K\) is polylogarithmic and \(L\) is one fixed numerical
quasipolynomial in \(n\), all displayed costs are uniformly deterministic
quasipolynomial.
