# Proof of the F182 wide-shift long-action filter

## 1. Exact menu filter

Fix a hidden component \(R_j\) and a prime power
\(\ell^a\parallel g_j\). Since \(g_j<N<2^n\), one has \(a<n\).

For one shift \(\delta\), the prime \(\ell\) divides \(C_\delta\) exactly
when at least one of the following holds:

\[
\ell\mid N+\delta,
\tag{1}
\]

or

\[
\ell\nmid N+\delta
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(N+\delta)\le K.
\tag{2}
\]

Therefore \(\ell\mid P_\mathcal S\) exactly when (1) or (2) holds for
at least one \(\delta\in\mathcal S\). The outer \(n\)-th power supplies at
least \(n>a\) copies of every such prime. The order-of-a-power formula gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z_\mathcal S)
=
\prod_{\substack{\ell^a\parallel g_j\\
\forall\delta\in\mathcal S:\ \,\ell\nmid N+\delta\\
\forall\delta\in\mathcal S:\ \,\operatorname{ord}_\ell(N+\delta)>K}}
\ell^a.
\tag{3}
\]

Every order in (3) divides \(g_j\), so it is coprime to the rational prime
below \(R_j\). Reduction from the unit group modulo \(R_j\) to the
corresponding prime field has a prime-power kernel. It is injective on the
subgroup generated by \(z_\mathcal S\). Hence

\[
z_\mathcal S\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z_\mathcal S\equiv1\pmod {R_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z_\mathcal S)=1.
\tag{4}
\]

An intermediate \(H_\mathcal S\) is therefore a proper factor, including
for repeated prime factors. If \(H_\mathcal S=1\), equation (3) is
nontrivial in every hidden component and proves (10). Its prime support is
a subset of the support of \(g_j\), so it remains \(T\)-rough.

## 2. Every cross-menu easy/easy resultant is small and nonzero

For a shift \(\delta\), define the integer polynomials

\[
F_{\delta,0}(X)=X+\delta,
\qquad
F_{\delta,k}(X)=(X+\delta)^k-1
\quad(1\le k\le K).
\tag{5}
\]

Fix \(\delta\in\mathcal A\), \(\epsilon\in\mathcal B\), and let

\[
D=\epsilon-\delta.
\]

The menu choice gives

\[
3\le D\le2L+1.
\tag{6}
\]

For \(0\le k,l\le K\), with index zero interpreted through (5), put

\[
R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
=\left|\operatorname{Res}_X
\bigl(F_{\delta,k},F_{\epsilon,l}\bigr)\right|.
\tag{7}
\]

All these resultants are nonzero.

- For \(k=l=0\), the resultant is \(D\).
- For \(k=0<l\), it is \(D^l-1\).
- For \(l=0<k\), its absolute value is \(|(-D)^k-1|\).
- For \(k,l>0\), a common complex root would lie on two unit circles
  whose centers have distance \(D\ge3\), which is impossible.

For \(k,l>0\), use the roots \(-\delta+u\), where \(u^k=1\), to obtain

\[
R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
=\prod_{u^k=1}|(D+u)^l-1|
<\bigl(2(D+1)^l\bigr)^k.
\tag{8}
\]

The three linear cases obey the same coarse upper bound. Equations (6)--(8)
give

\[
0<R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
<2^{K(1+K\lceil\log_2(2L+2)\rceil)}
<\widehat T\le T.
\tag{9}
\]

This includes \(L=1\): the two menus are \(\{0\}\) and \(\{3\}\), so
the unique cross-menu gap is still three.

## 3. Double global identity is impossible

Assume for contradiction that

\[
H_\mathcal A=H_\mathcal B=N.
\tag{10}
\]

Both filters in (10) act on the same original unit \(w\). Fix any
\(\ell^a\parallel g_j\). The first identity and the exact order formula
(3) imply that

\[
\ell\mid F_{\delta,k}(N)
\]

for some \(\delta\in\mathcal A\) and some \(0\le k\le K\). The second
identity similarly gives

\[
\ell\mid F_{\epsilon,l}(N)
\]

for some \(\epsilon\in\mathcal B\) and some \(0\le l\le K\).

The Sylvester-matrix Bezout identity evaluated at \(X=N\) now gives

\[
\ell\mid R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}.
\tag{11}
\]

But F181 roughness gives \(\ell>T\), while (9) gives

\[
0<R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}<T.
\]

This contradicts (11). Thus the two global identities cannot both occur.
The stated algorithm must return a proper factor or one of the two
wide-shift-hard descendants.

## 4. Encoded exponent cost

For every used shift,

\[
\log_2(N+\delta)=O(n+\log L).
\]

Summing the bit lengths of the factors in (6) of the statement gives

\[
\log_2 C_\delta=O\bigl(K^2(n+\log L)\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

Each menu contains exactly \(L\) shifts, so

\[
\log_2P_\mathcal S
=O\bigl(LK^2(n+\log L)\bigr),
\tag{13}
\]

and

\[
\log_2(P_\mathcal S^n)
=O\bigl(nLK^2(n+\log L)\bigr).
\tag{14}
\]

The products can be built with a product tree. Binary modular powering is
polynomial in the exponent bit length and in \(n\). There are at most two
such powers and two gcds. Since \(K\) is polylogarithmic and \(L\) is one
fixed numerical quasipolynomial, (14) and the total bit cost are uniformly
quasipolynomial.

The preceding F181 construction at the cap (5) is also QP because

\[
\log_2T=O(K^2\log L)=(\log n)^{O(1)}.
\]

No resultant needs to be factored or even constructed by the algorithm;
the resultants occur only in the contradiction proof.
