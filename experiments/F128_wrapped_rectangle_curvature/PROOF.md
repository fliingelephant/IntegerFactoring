# F128 proof — wrapped multiplicative-rectangle curvature

## 1. Exact decomposition and local rank

Let

\[
m_{00}=u,
\quad m_{10}=ua,
\quad m_{01}=ub,
\quad m_{11}=uab,
\]

and

\[
n_{00}=zab,
\quad n_{10}=zb,
\quad n_{01}=za,
\quad n_{11}=z.
\]

Every raw product is the same:

\[
m_{ij}n_{ij}=uabz=1+NK.
\]

The six quotient definitions give

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{00}&=u,&y_{00}&=zab-R_0N,\\
x_{10}&=ua-AN,&y_{10}&=zb-R_aN,\\
x_{01}&=ub-BN,&y_{01}&=za-R_bN,\\
x_{11}&=uab-CN,&y_{11}&=z.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(x=m-qN\), \(y=n-rN\), and \(mn=1+NK\), then

\[
\frac{xy-1}{N}=K-mr-nq+Nqr.
\]

Apply this identity at the four corners. It gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{00}&=K-uR_0,\\
\kappa_{10}&=K-uaR_a-zbA+NAR_a,\\
\kappa_{01}&=K-ubR_b-zaB+NBR_b,\\
\kappa_{11}&=K-zC.
\end{aligned}
\]

Substitute these four equations into

\[
\Omega_{\square}
=d(\kappa_{11}-\kappa_{00})
+e(\kappa_{10}-\kappa_{01}).
\]

Collect the coefficient of \(N\), the coefficient of \(u\), and the
coefficient of \(z\). This gives

\[
\Omega_{\square}
=Ne(AR_a-BR_b)
+u\left[dR_0+e(bR_b-aR_a)\right]
+z\left[e(aB-bA)-dC\right].
\]

This is the claimed decomposition. Removing the explicit multiple of \(N\)
does not change a gcd with \(N\).

For the local-rank statement, order the corners as \(00,10,01,11\), and put

\[
\lambda=(-d,e,-e,d).
\]

The vector \(\lambda\) annihilates the columns \(1,m,n\):

\[
\sum\lambda_{ij}=0,
\qquad
\sum\lambda_{ij}m_{ij}=0,
\qquad
\sum\lambda_{ij}n_{ij}=0.
\]

It also annihilates the constant raw carry \(K\). Modulo every prime
\(\ell\mid N\), reduction by multiples of \(N\) does not change the first
three columns. Under the P114 unit conditions, these columns have rank three.
Their left kernel is therefore generated by \(\lambda\). The fourth column
raises the rank to four exactly when

\[
\lambda\mathbin{\cdot}(\kappa_{ij})
=\Omega_{\square}
\equiv u\Phi_y+z\Phi_x
\not\equiv0\pmod\ell.
\]

## 2. Uniform-\(u\) density for fixed \(a,b\)

The quotient ranges are

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le A<a,&0&\le B<b,&0&\le C<ab,\\
0&\le R_0<ab,&0&\le R_a<b,&0&\le R_b<a.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \((A,B,C)\) has at most \(a^2b^2\) values, and
\((R_0,R_a,R_b)\) has at most \(a^2b^2\) values. There are at most
\(a^4b^4\) joint quotient patterns.

For each pattern, \(\Phi_x\) and \(\Phi_y\) are fixed integers. They obey

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\Phi_x|
&\le (ab-1)\bigl(a(b-1)+b(a-1)\bigr)
+|b-a|(ab-1),\\
|\Phi_y|
&\le |b-a|(ab-1)
+(ab-1)\bigl(b(a-1)+a(b-1)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence both are at most

\[
H=(ab-1)\bigl(a(b-1)+b(a-1)+|b-a|\bigr).
\]

For \(a,b\ge2\), the stated coefficient condition is safe because

\[
H<2(ab)^2
<(ab)^2\bigl(ab+\max\{a,b\}\bigr)<p.
\]

Multiplication by the unit \(uab\) gives

\[
uab(u\Phi_y+z\Phi_x)
\equiv ab\Phi_yu^2+\Phi_x
\pmod N,
\]

because \(uabz\equiv1\pmod N\).

Fix one joint quotient pattern. If \(\Phi_x=\Phi_y=0\), the exact
decomposition shows that \(N\mid\Omega_{\square}\). This pattern gives no
proper factor. Otherwise,

\[
ab\Phi_yX^2+\Phi_x
\]

is a nonzero polynomial modulo \(p\). It has at most two roots in
\(\mathbb F_p\). Every residue modulo \(p\) has at most \(q\) lifts in
\(\{1,\ldots,N-1\}\). Therefore

\[
\#\{u:p\mid\Omega_{\square},\ N\nmid\Omega_{\square}\}
\le2q\,a^4b^4.
\]

The same argument with \(p,q\) exchanged gives

\[
\#\{u:q\mid\Omega_{\square},\ N\nmid\Omega_{\square}\}
\le2p\,a^4b^4.
\]

The proper-factor event is contained in the union of these two events.
There are \(\varphi(N)=(p-1)(q-1)\) units, so

\[
\Pr_u\!\left(1<\gcd(\Omega_{\square},N)<N\right)
\le
\frac{2a^4b^4(p+q)}{(p-1)(q-1)}.
\]

For \(p<q\), the last expression is \(O(a^4b^4/p)\). The proof partitions
uniform \(u\)-values after the fact. It does not constrain an algorithm that
selects \(u\) from the observed quotient pattern or from earlier data.

## 3. Signed-small full-channel obstruction

For one corner, write

\[
x=[\eta r]_N,
\qquad
\eta\in\{-1,+1\},
\qquad
\bar r=[r^{-1}]_N,
\qquad
h_r=\frac{r\bar r-1}{N}.
\]

If \(\eta=+1\), then \(y=\bar r\) and \(\kappa=h_r\). If
\(\eta=-1\), then \(x=N-r\), \(y=N-\bar r\), and

\[
\kappa=N-r-\bar r+h_r
\equiv h_r-r-\bar r\pmod N.
\]

The original \(d,e\) are congruent modulo \(N\) to
\(\tilde d,\tilde e\). Therefore

\[
\Omega_{\square}
\equiv
\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}
\left(
h_{r_{ij}}
-\mathbf1_{\eta_{ij}=-1}(r_{ij}+\bar r_{ij})
\right)
\pmod N.
\]

Each \(r_{ij}\) divides \(L=sa_0b_0\). Since

\[
L\bar r_{ij}\equiv\frac{L}{r_{ij}}\pmod N,
\]

multiplication by \(L\) gives

\[
L\Omega_{\square}\equiv B_{\rm sign}\pmod N.
\]

If \(L\) is a unit, multiplication by \(L\) preserves the gcd with \(N\).
This proves the gcd identity.

For every corner, \(0\le h_r<r\le L\). Hence

\[
\left|
Lh_r-\mathbf1_{\eta=-1}
\left(Lr+\frac{L}{r}\right)
\right|<3L^2.
\]

Also,

\[
\sum_{i,j}|\lambda_{ij}|
=2|\tilde d|+2|\tilde e|
\le2(a_0+b_0+a_0b_0+1).
\]

Therefore

\[
|B_{\rm sign}|
<6L^2(a_0+b_0+a_0b_0+1).
\]

It remains to check the other named channels. For a corner of signed
magnitude \(r\), multiplication by the unit \(r\) gives

\[
\gcd(x-y,N)=\gcd(r^2-1,N),
\qquad
\gcd(x+y,N)=\gcd(r^2+1,N).
\]

Both integers on the right have magnitude at most \(L^2+1<T\), apart from
the zero case \(r=1\), which gives the global gcd \(N\). The P114 prefactors
reduce to members of

\[
\{s,a_0,b_0,a_0-\epsilon_a,b_0-\epsilon_b,
\epsilon_b b_0-\epsilon_a a_0\}
\]

up to signs and products. A proper prefactor gcd is therefore also
impossible when both prime factors exceed \(T\). Finally, the curvature gcd
is one if \(B_{\rm sign}\ne0\), and is \(N\) if \(B_{\rm sign}=0\).
This proves the full named-channel obstruction.

## 4. Fully wrapped arithmetic-progression trap

Assume first that \(s>1\). The four corners have the form

\[
x_{ij}=N-r_{ij},
\qquad
(r_{00},r_{10},r_{01},r_{11})=(s,sa,sb,sab).
\]

Every \(r_{ij}>1\) divides \(L=sab\). Since \(N\equiv-1\pmod L\),

\[
\bar r_{ij}=\frac{N+1}{r_{ij}},
\qquad
h_{r_{ij}}=1.
\]

Thus

\[
\kappa_{ij}=N+1-r_{ij}-\frac{N+1}{r_{ij}}.
\]

For \(\lambda=(-d,e,-e,d)\), the three exact identities

\[
\sum\lambda_{ij}=0,
\qquad
\sum\lambda_{ij}r_{ij}=0,
\qquad
\sum\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{r_{ij}}=0
\]

follow by direct substitution of
\((r_{00},r_{10},r_{01},r_{11})=s(1,a,b,ab)\). They give

\[
\Omega_{\square}=0.
\]

The lower bound on \(p,q\), together with \(a\ne b\), makes every nonzero
P114 prefactor smaller than the factors. It also makes every endpoint scalar
\(r_{ij}^2\pm1\) smaller than the factors. Thus the rectangle is eligible,
and every named direct screen is null or global.

For infinitely many balanced examples, use the prime number theorem in
arithmetic progressions. For arbitrarily large \(X\), choose

\[
p\equiv1\pmod L,
\qquad
q\equiv-1\pmod L
\]

in comparable intervals such as \([X,2X]\). Then \(pq\equiv-1\pmod L\),
and the factors eventually exceed every fixed eligibility bound.

Now take \(s=1\). The \(00\) corner is \(N-1\), with

\[
\kappa_{00}=N-2.
\]

At the other three corners, the preceding formula still applies.
Equivalently, their positive inverse carries are one, while the positive
inverse carry of \(1\) is zero. The same three weighted identities, with this
one correction, give

\[
\Omega_{\square}=(b-a)(1-N).
\]

Therefore

\[
\gcd(\Omega_{\square},N)=\gcd(b-a,N)=1
\]

under the stated large-factor eligibility. The equality
\(x_{00}=y_{00}=N-1\) gives a global difference gcd only. The other endpoint
scalars have magnitude at most \((ab)^2+1\), and every nonzero prefactor has
magnitude at most \(ab+a+b+1\). The boundary-case factor bound makes all of
their gcds one.
