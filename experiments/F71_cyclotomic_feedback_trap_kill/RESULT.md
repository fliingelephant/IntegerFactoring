# F71 — cyclotomic order-three feedback trap

**Status:** proved narrow obstruction, self-audited. No research computation
was run.

## 1. Disposition

The proposed trap is correct after two scope corrections.

1. Reducing an arbitrary power of the sole block can also give the trivial
   state $1$. Under the usual legal-state rule $1<g<N$, this state is
   discarded. If it is retained, it gives only the trivial relation $1$.
2. If an oversized raw power is retained as an endpoint instead of being
   replaced by its canonical residue, the resulting relation value can be
   $c^{3t}$, not literally only $c^3$. This larger rule still creates no
   new block, no proper direct gcd, and no non-global square-decoder root.

With canonical reduction before inversion, every nontrivial feedback step
has endpoints $c,c^2$ and repeats the value $c^3$. With the larger raw
power rule, every endpoint and relation value remains a power of $c$, and
the complete square-decoder image remains $\{1\}$.

There is also an infinite family with at least two distinct prime factors,
least prime factor tending to infinity, and no perfect-power escape. The
construction uses only CRT, Dirichlet's theorem, and a one-line simple-root
lifting argument.

This is a source-specific obstruction. It rules out escape by occurrence
amplification inside the one-block source. It does not rule out adding an
independent seed relation or using arithmetic outside that source.

## 2. Setup

Put

\[
F(X)=X^2+X+1.
\]

Let $c$ be an odd prime and assume

\[
N=F(c)=c^2+c+1
\]

is composite and $3\nmid N$. Then

\[
c^3-1=(c-1)N,
\]

so the initial canonical inverse relation is

\[
c\cdot c^2=c^3=1+(c-1)N. \tag{1}
\]

Since $c^2<N$, both endpoints are canonical positive residues. Complete
gcd-free refinement of these endpoints has the sole atomic block $c$, with
endpoint exponents $1$ and $2$.

## 3. Every local order is exactly three

### Lemma 1

For every prime power $p^a\Vert N$, the residue of $c$ modulo $p^a$
has exact multiplicative order $3$.

### Proof

Equation (1) gives

\[
c^3\equiv1\pmod {p^a}.
\]

Thus the order divides $3$. If it were $1$, then $c\equiv1\pmod p$.
Substitution into $F(c)\equiv0\pmod p$ would give $3\equiv0\pmod p$,
so $p=3$. This is impossible because $3\nmid N$. Therefore the order is
exactly $3$. □

### Corollary 2

The subgroup generated by $c$ is

\[
H=\langle c\rangle=\{1,c,c^2\}\pmod N, \tag{2}
\]

and the displayed integers are their canonical representatives. Neither
$c$ nor $c^2$ is $+1$ or $-1$ on any prime-power component of $N$.
Consequently

\[
\gcd(c\pm1,N)=\gcd(c^2\pm1,N)=1. \tag{3}
\]

In particular, $H$ contains no direct sign separator, even without a
finite exponent bound.

This is stronger than a statement that the useful power is outside a current
finite box. There is no useful sign power anywhere in this subgroup.

## 4. Canonical feedback is a fixed point

Consider any finite number of authorized occurrences of (1). Every legal
whole-block product is $c^e$ for some $e\ge1$.

First use the original legal-divisor rule, which requires the selected
integer to be less than $N$. Since $c\ge3$ and

\[
c^3>N,
\]

the only legal powers are $c$ and $c^2$. They are canonical inverses of
one another, and either orientation appends the same relation (1).

Now allow arbitrary occurrence multiplicity and reduce the selected power
modulo $N$ before canonical inversion. Lemma 1 gives

\[
c^e\bmod N=
\begin{cases}
1,&e\equiv0\pmod3,\\
c,&e\equiv1\pmod3,\\
c^2,&e\equiv2\pmod3.
\end{cases} \tag{4}
\]

The first case is not a legal nontrivial state. If it is nevertheless kept,
its canonical inverse is $1$ and it adds only the value $1$. The other
two cases have endpoints $c,c^2$ and add $c^3$. Therefore arbitrary
occurrence amplification plus canonical reduction cannot leave the initial
state.

This proves the literal fixed-point claim under the canonical-reduction rule:

\[
\boxed{\text{nontrivial endpoint set }\{c,c^2\},
\quad\text{relation-value set }\{c^3\}.} \tag{5}
\]

Exact gcd refinement cannot change this conclusion. Every known positive
integer is a power of the prime $c$, and

\[
\gcd(c^u,c^v)=c^{\min(u,v)}.
\]

Thus the only nontrivial gcd-free block remains $c$. There is no endpoint
overlap that can expose a second block generator.

## 5. Even an oversized raw-power variant remains trapped

The literal duplicate-value statement in (5) needs canonical reduction. A
larger operation could retain the raw integer $G=c^e$, even when $G\ge N$,
and multiply it by the canonical inverse $w\in\{1,c,c^2\}$ of its residue.
Choose $j\in\{0,1,2\}$ such that

\[
w=c^j,
\qquad e+j\equiv0\pmod3.
\]

Then the appended relation value is

\[
Gw=c^{e+j}=c^{3t} \tag{6}
\]

for some $t\ge1$. It need not equal $c^3$, but it remains a pure power of
the old block. Hence raw-power retention also cannot create a new gcd-free
block.

The direct screens still give no proper factor. If $e\not\equiv0\pmod3$,
the residue is $c$ or $c^2$, so (3) applies. If $e\equiv0\pmod3$, the
residue is the global identity: the minus screen returns $N$, and the plus
screen returns $1$. Neither result is a proper factor.

For the inverse-pair discriminant, let $d=G-w$. Since $Gw\equiv1\pmod N$,

\[
d^2+4\equiv(G+w)^2\pmod N. \tag{7}
\]

For the two nontrivial orientations,

\[
G+w\equiv c+c^2\equiv-1\pmod N.
\]

For the identity orientation, $G+w\equiv2\pmod N$. Since $N$ is odd,
equation (7) gives

\[
\gcd(d^2+4,N)=1
\]

in all cases. The related difference screen is also only global or trivial,
because

\[
\gcd(d,N)=\gcd(G^2-1,N).
\]

Thus the larger raw-power rule changes relation multiplicities but does not
create a proper screen.

## 6. The complete square decoder gives only the global root

Under canonical reduction, every nontrivial indexed relation value is
$c^3$. The parity matrix has one row and one entry $1$ in every such
column. Its kernel consists exactly of even-cardinality subsets. A dependency
using $2m$ copies has exact positive root

\[
\sqrt{(c^3)^{2m}}=c^{3m}\equiv1\pmod N. \tag{8}
\]

Therefore duplicate relations can pass the square-class closure gate, but
their induced root always fails the non-global root gate.

The same conclusion holds for the larger raw-power rule. Suppose its indexed
relation values are $c^{3t_i}$. A selected product is a rational square
exactly when

\[
\sum_i x_i t_i\equiv0\pmod2.
\]

For every such dependency, the exact positive root is

\[
c^{\frac32\sum_i x_it_i}
=(c^3)^{\frac12\sum_i x_it_i}
\equiv1\pmod N. \tag{9}
\]

This proves that the full rational-square relation space, not only one chosen
kernel basis, has root image

\[
\boxed{\{1\}.} \tag{10}
\]

Allowing integer relation coefficients or complete $2$-saturation does not
change (9). The parity condition is still that the total exponent of $c$ is
even, and the resulting root exponent is still a multiple of $3$.

## 7. Perfect-power and integer-square checks do not escape

Several exact roots are public:

\[
c^2=(c)^2,
\qquad
c^{3t}\text{ can itself be a perfect power},
\qquad
4N-3=(2c+1)^2. \tag{11}
\]

Each extraction returns $c$, a power of $c$, or the already known
integer $2c+1$. It does not split $N$. In particular, the square identity
in (11) explains how the source block $c$ can be recovered from bare $N$;
it does not give a factor of $N$.

The infinite construction below additionally forces two prime divisors of
$N$ to have valuation one. Hence $N$ itself is not a perfect power, so
standard perfect-power preprocessing does not remove the constructed family.

## 8. An infinite family with growing least prime factor

### Theorem 3

For every integer $B\ge3$, there are infinitely many odd primes $c$ such
that

1. $N=c^2+c+1$ is composite and $3\nmid N$;
2. $N$ has at least two distinct prime divisors;
3. every prime divisor of $N$ is greater than $B$;
4. two prescribed prime divisors occur to exact exponent one, so $N$ is not
   a perfect power.

Consequently there is a sequence of such moduli whose least prime divisor
tends to infinity.

### Proof

Choose distinct primes

\[
\ell_1,\ell_2>B,
\qquad
\ell_i\equiv1\pmod3.
\]

Such primes can be chosen arbitrarily large by Dirichlet's theorem. Since
$\mathbb F_{\ell_i}^{\times}$ has an element of order $3$, choose a
nontrivial cube root $r_i$ modulo $\ell_i$. Then

\[
F(r_i)=r_i^2+r_i+1\equiv0\pmod {\ell_i}. \tag{12}
\]

The derivative $F'(r_i)=2r_i+1$ is nonzero modulo $\ell_i$. Otherwise
$r_i\equiv-1/2$, and (12) would give $3/4\equiv0\pmod{\ell_i}$, forcing
$\ell_i=3$. Therefore exactly one of the $\ell_i$ lifts of $r_i$ modulo
$\ell_i^2$ is a root modulo $\ell_i^2$. Choose a different lift $a_i$.
It satisfies

\[
F(a_i)\equiv0\pmod {\ell_i},
\qquad
F(a_i)\not\equiv0\pmod {\ell_i^2}. \tag{13}
\]

Now impose the following finite congruence system on $c$:

\[
\begin{aligned}
c&\equiv a_i &&\pmod {\ell_i^2},\quad i=1,2,\\
c&\equiv1 &&\pmod2,\\
c&\equiv2 &&\pmod3,\\
c&\equiv1 &&\pmod q
\quad\text{for every prime }5\le q\le B.
\end{aligned} \tag{14}
\]

All moduli are pairwise coprime. The prescribed class is coprime to their
product: the $a_i$ are nonzero modulo $\ell_i$, and all other prescribed
residues are units. CRT gives one reduced residue class, and Dirichlet's
theorem gives infinitely many primes $c$ in it. Choose any such prime large
enough to avoid the finitely many fixed primes.

Equation (13) gives

\[
v_{\ell_1}(N)=v_{\ell_2}(N)=1. \tag{15}
\]

Thus $N$ is composite, has at least the two distinct prime divisors
$\ell_1,\ell_2$, and is not a perfect power.

No prime at most $B$ divides $N$. The modulus is odd because $c$ is odd.
For $q=3$, the residue $c\equiv2$ gives $F(c)\equiv1\pmod3$. For each
prime $5\le q\le B$, the residue $c\equiv1$ gives

\[
F(c)\equiv F(1)=3\not\equiv0\pmod q.
\]

This proves all four claims. Taking $B\to\infty$ and choosing a new larger
prime $c$ at each stage gives the required sequence. □

The phrase “avoid cube-root residues modulo every small prime” must mean every
prime below a finite threshold $B$ at one construction stage. No infinite
CRT condition is needed or claimed.

## 9. Small exact witness

The first simple example is

\[
c=11,
\qquad
N=11^2+11+1=133=7\cdot19.
\]

The seed relation is

\[
11\cdot121=1331=1+10\cdot133.
\]

The subgroup $\{1,11,121\}$ has order three modulo both $7$ and $19$.
Neither nontrivial endpoint passes a sign screen. Two copies close the square
class, but their exact root is $1331\equiv1\pmod {133}$. This example is
only illustrative; Theorem 3 removes its small factor $7$.

## 10. Relation to P69--P77

### P69: one-block saturation

P69 already proves a fixed point for feeding one whole current block after a
quotient-bounded saturation scan. Here the quotient in (1) is $c-1$, which
is of order $\sqrt N$. Scanning through that quotient takes exponentially
many states in the bit length. The present result gives a different exact
fact: even unlimited occurrence amplification of the sole block cannot
escape, because its modular order is three.

### P70: power escape

P70's infinite family uses repeated occurrences to grow a tiny block power
to a useful value near $\sqrt N$. The present family is a direct obstruction
to treating that mechanism as universal. Raw legality permits only $c,c^2$,
and modular reduction makes all higher powers cycle through (2). Occurrence
multiplicity alone supplies no new state.

### P71: finite-box boundary

P71 gives examples in which the finite legal box misses a useful element that
exists in the full generated subgroup. Here the stronger subgroup-level
obstruction holds: $H$ has odd order three and contains no direct sign
separator at all. Recovering its complete order or exponent lattice only
returns the public fact that the order is three.

### P73 and P74: closure and root label

Two duplicate $c^3$ columns pass P73's square-class closure gate. Equation
(8) shows that P74's induced root is the global identity. Every further
closure has the same root label. This is an exact infinite failure of the
second gate inside the declared source.

### P76: endpoint refinement

P76 shows that integer overlaps can split old composite blocks and enlarge the
block-generated subgroup even when endpoint residues remain old-subgroup
elements. That mechanism is absent here. Every endpoint is a power of the
prime $c$, so gcd refinement has no second atomic block to expose.

### P77: raw balance

The split $c\cdot c^2=c^3$ is an exact re-presentation of the old value.
Balancing or reversing it adds neither a new integer value nor a new root.
This agrees with P77's one-relation redundancy boundary.

## 11. Exact scope and conclusion

The proved invariant is:

> Start from the sole gcd-free block $c$ and allow only products of its
> authorized occurrences, canonical modular reduction, canonical inversion,
> exact gcd refinement, perfect-power extraction, direct sign and inverse-pair
> discriminant screens, and the complete rational-square decoder. Every finite
> transcript remains in the monoid of powers of $c$; every proper screen
> fails; and every decoded square root is the global identity.

Theorem 3 shows that this is not a small-factor or perfect-power artifact.
It supplies infinitely many composite inputs with at least two distinct prime
factors and growing least prime factor.

The result does **not** obstruct general factoring or general cross-relation
feedback. A procedure can add an inverse relation from a state outside
$\langle c\rangle$, use the public quotient $c-1$, or apply unrelated
arithmetic to $N$. Any such action introduces data outside the one-block
hypothesis, so this invariant no longer applies. In particular, the theorem
does not prove that every polynomial-size seed family is trapped.

**Classification:** proved approach-family obstruction for sole-block
occurrence amplification; not an all-input source obstruction and not a
factoring algorithm.
