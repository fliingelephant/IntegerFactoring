# Hostile audit of F184 — QP root-count action hardening

## Verdict

**PASS.** The stated transition is correct on the imported F181 rough-order
branch. The proof uses no hidden factorization, local order, or order oracle.
The total deterministic bit cost is quasipolynomial for arbitrary fixed
integer-valued numerical-QP bounds \(K(n)\) and \(M(n)\).

I found two editorial defects, neither of which changes the theorem or its
algorithm:

1. Statement (5) has a stray comma immediately after the opening brace. The
   prose, endpoints, cardinality, and all later bounds uniquely specify

   \[
   \mathcal I_t=
   \{2+t(D+1),\ldots,1+(t+1)(D+1)\}.
   \]
2. In the cost paragraph, “each stage uses binary modular powering and one
   gcd” means “each candidate evaluation uses” those operations. Also,
   \(M(D+1)\) is an upper bound if evaluation stops at the first survivor or
   factor; evaluating each full block makes it exact. Either reading gives the
   claimed cost.

## Attack 1: exact primary deletion

The proof's immediate claim that \(C_a^n\) deletes the complete
\(\ell^e\)-part exactly when \(\ell\mid C_a\) has the required size margin.
Indeed,

\[
\ell^e\mid g_{t,j}<R_j\le N<2^n,
\]

so \(e<n\). If \(\ell\mid C_a\), then
\(v_\ell(C_a^n)\ge n>e\), and the full part is deleted. If
\(\ell\nmid C_a\), none of it is deleted. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(w_t^{C_a^n})
=\frac{g_{t,j}}{\gcd(g_{t,j},C_a^n)}
\]

has exactly the asserted prime support. This also closes the implication used
later: if \(\ell\) survives, then \(\ell\nmid C_a\).

## Attack 2: degree and root counting

The polynomial

\[
F_K(X)=X\prod_{k=1}^K(X^k-1)
\]

is monic of degree

\[
1+\sum_{k=1}^K k=D.
\]

For a prime \(\ell\), its roots are exactly \(a=0\) and the units whose
multiplicative order modulo \(\ell\) is at most \(K\). The converse is valid:
if a unit has order \(d\le K\), the factor \(X^d-1\) occurs in \(F_K\).

Put \(q=D+1\). The intended block is

\[
\mathcal I_t=\{2+tq,\ldots,1+(t+1)q\}.
\]

It has exactly \(q=D+1\) integers. Its maximum over all stages is
\(1+Mq<T\). Every prime in every current local order exceeds \(T\), so the
members of a block are \(D+1\) distinct residues modulo each such prime.
The reduction of \(F_K\) modulo \(\ell\) remains a nonzero monic polynomial
of degree \(D\). It cannot vanish at all those residues.

If every candidate at a stage had \(H_{t,a}=N\), then every \(z_{t,a}\)
would be the identity modulo every \(R_j\). Fixing one nontrivial current
local order and one prime \(\ell\) in it gives

\[
g_{t,j}\mid C_a^n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell\mid C_a=F_K(a)
\]

for all \(D+1\) candidates, contradicting the root bound. Thus some
candidate has \(H_{t,a}\ne N\). In the no-factor branch, every gcd is either
\(1\) or \(N\), so that candidate has \(H_{t,a}=1\). No simultaneous-root or
resultant assumption is hidden here.

## Attack 3: repeated prime factors and the gcd screen

Write \(R_j=p_j^{r_j}\). Every later local order divides \(g_{0,j}\), hence
is coprime to \(p_j\). The kernel of

\[
(\mathbb Z/R_j\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb F_{p_j}^\times
\]

is a \(p_j\)-group. Its intersection with the cyclic subgroup generated by
any \(z_{t,a}\) is therefore trivial. Consequently

\[
z_{t,a}\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z_{t,a}\equiv1\pmod {R_j}.
\]

Thus \(H_{t,a}=1\) makes the next local order nontrivial on every hidden
prime-power component. A partial \(p_j\)-adic gcd would in any event be a
proper factor, but the injectivity argument shows that it cannot occur on
the surviving branch. This validates the claim for nonsquarefree \(N\),
including the case that \(N\) itself is a prime power.

## Attack 4: survival of every earlier selected-base condition

For the selected \(a_t\), a prime \(\ell\) in \(g_{t+1,j}\) satisfies
\(\ell\nmid C_{a_t}\). The exact description of the roots of \(F_K\) then
gives

\[
\ell\nmid a_t,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a_t)>K.
\]

The order sequence is divisibility-monotone:
\(g_{t+1,j}\mid g_{t,j}\). Hence a prime in \(g_{M,j}\) survived every
earlier filter and satisfies both conditions for every selected stage.
Nontriviality is renewed by each \(H=1\) screen. Coprimality with \(N\) and
\(T\)-roughness persist because later orders only lose primary parts.

The blocks are consecutive and disjoint. Their union is
\(\{2,\ldots,1+M(D+1)\}\), which lies strictly below every surviving
\(\ell\). Thus the \(M\) selected bases are distinct as public integers and
as residues modulo every final order prime. Since each is a unit modulo each
prime dividing \(g_{M,j}\), exponentiation by that base is an automorphism
on the corresponding primary cyclic component. Its order is at least
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a_t)>K\).

## Attack 5: arbitrary QP parameters and total cost

For fixed numerical-QP \(K\) and \(M\),

\[
D=1+K(K+1)/2,
\qquad
M(D+1),
\qquad
T
\]

are numerical-QP. The largest base is numerical-QP. For \(a\ge2\),

\[
0<C_a
=a\prod_{k=1}^K(a^k-1)
<a^D,
\]

so \(C_a\) has at most \(D\log_2 a+1\) bits and the explicit integer
\(C_a^n\) has at most \(nD\log_2a+1\) bits. These are QP bounds, not bounds
on the numerical value of the exponent. Building the ordinary powers,
their product, and \(C_a^n\) therefore uses QP-size integers and QP bit
operations.

There are at most \(M(D+1)\) candidates. Each candidate needs public integer
arithmetic, binary modular exponentiation modulo the \(n\)-bit input, and
one gcd. Multiplying the operation count by the QP cost per operation stays
QP. Repeating stages does not form or manipulate the product of all prior
exponents: every \(w_t\) is reduced modulo \(N\) before the next stage.

The construction enumerates public bases, forms \(C_a\), powers modulo
\(N\), computes gcds, and chooses the first public gcd equal to one. The
hidden components, their primes, and their orders occur only in the proof.
No \(C_a\) is factored, and no test computes
\(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a_t)\); the root argument certifies it after the
fact. Therefore there is no hidden factoring or order oracle.

## Scope check

The conclusion gives \(M\) distinct long exponentiation actions on every
prime in the final local-order support. It does not establish independence
of those actions, a prescribed action menu, an exact common local order, or
factoring on the final surviving branch. The statement explicitly preserves
these boundaries.

audit was symbolic; the only computation was the requested SHA-256 input
