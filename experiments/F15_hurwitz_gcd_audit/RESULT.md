# F15 — focused hostile audit of the Hurwitz one-sided-gcd kill

**Audit verdict:** **survives only after explicit corrections and scope
qualification.**  The central odd, squarefree, two-prime theorem is correct:
conditional on iid exact-uniform samples from the norm-\(pq\) shell, the stated
one-sided-gcd law and the \(O(K^2/\sqrt N)\) balanced-semiprime obstruction hold.
This is evidence against that narrow collision mechanism, not a factoring result
and not a lower bound for nonuniform, adaptive, or nonlinear constructions.

The author directory has only `RESULT.md`.  There is no author run manifest; the
author explicitly says no computation was used.  Thus there was no missing
computational evidence on which the proof depended, but also no author artifact
to validate.

## Corrections required before using the result

1. **Normalize the order.**  The proof needs

   \[
   \mathcal H=\left\{\frac{a+bi+cj+dk}{2}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z,
   \ a\equiv b\equiv c\equiv d\pmod 2\right\},
   \]

   with \(\bar\alpha\) obtained by negating the three imaginary coordinates,
   \(\operatorname{trd}(\alpha)=\alpha+\bar\alpha\), and
   \(\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha)=\alpha\bar\alpha\).  In this normalization
   the reduced trace and norm are integers and the norm-one group has 24
   elements.

2. **Correct the relative right action.**  If \(L=\operatorname{row}(A)\), then

   \[
   LB_1=LB_2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   L(B_1B_2^{-1})=L.
   \]

   The author writes \(B_2^{-1}B_1\).  Those two matrices are conjugate, so they
   have the same number of fixed projective lines and are scalar modulo exactly
   the same primes.  The subsequent bound and trace-free-coordinate gcd survive,
   but the displayed acting element must be corrected.

3. **State transformed gcds modulo \(N\), not as the literal norm table.**  A
   transform \(a\alpha b\) generally no longer has norm \(N\).  For two
   independent source samples, the exact theorem remains true for the pattern
   of divisibility of the gcd norm by \(p\) and \(q\), equivalently after taking
   \(\gcd(\operatorname{nrd}(d),N)\).  The full norm of the transformed gcd may
   also contain primes from the fixed multiplier norms.

4. **Keep the ideal-lattice statement local.**  The equality-partition argument
   is a theorem about the lattice generated by the minimal ideals after reduction
   to each \(M_2(\mathbb F_r)\).  It covers local sums, intersections,
   inclusions, equalities, and ranks.  It does not by itself cover integral ideal
   intersections before reduction, coefficient-valued invariants, saturations,
   or arbitrary global normal forms; reduction need not commute with every such
   construction.

5. **Keep the probability quantifiers explicit.**  The exact laws use iid
   uniform orientations.  The row and image orientations of the *same* sample
   were not proved independent, which is why using both gcd handednesses gives
   only a union bound.  The product law uses \(I_r(\beta)\) and \(K_r(\alpha)\)
   from different independent samples.  A polynomial fixed menu of incidences is
   covered; sample-dependent nonlinear transforms are not.

6. **Use \(\Omega(N^{1/4})\) for the necessary sample lower scale.**  The union
   bound proves that \(K=o(N^{1/4})\) has vanishing success on balanced
   semiprimes.  The exact occupancy formulas also show that
   \(K=\Theta(N^{1/4})\) is the birthday scale and gives constant collision
   probability.  The author's informal “needs \(\Theta(N^{1/4})\)” is sound as a
   scale statement, but the proved necessary statement is \(\Omega(N^{1/4})\).

7. **Do not promote the cited sampler sentence into a proved lemma.**  The cited
   Rabin--Shallit and Pollack--Treviño results do distinguish unconditional
   find-one algorithms from an ERH-dependent faster variant and work in an
   arithmetic-operation model with polynomial-bit operands.  That supports the
   ancillary existence of an expected-polynomial find-one algorithm.  The author
   did not reconstruct its random-bit and Las Vegas proof, and no citation proves
   exact-uniform sampling.  The F15 obstruction should therefore be stated
   conditionally on uniform sampling supplied for free; the standard find-one fact
   is logically separate and unnecessary to the obstruction.

## Corrected theorem that survives the audit

Let \(p\ne q\) be odd primes, \(N=pq\), and

\[
S_N=\{\alpha\in\mathcal H:\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha)=N\}.
\]

For arbitrary algebra splittings
\(\iota_r:\mathcal H/r\mathcal H\simeq M_2(\mathbb F_r)\), \(r=p,q\), define
\(R_r(\alpha)\) and \(I_r(\alpha)\) to be the row and image lines of
\(\iota_r(\alpha)\).  Then:

1. \(|S_N|=24(p+1)(q+1)\).
2. \(\mathcal H^\times\backslash S_N\) for the free left-unit action maps bijectively to
   \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_p)\times\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_q)\) through
   \((R_p,R_q)\).  Right-unit orbits give the analogous image-line bijection.
3. If \(\alpha,\beta\) are iid uniform on \(S_N\), a greatest common right
   divisor \(d_R\), normalized only up to a left unit, satisfies

   \[
   \bigl(\Pr[\operatorname{nrd}d_R=1],
   \Pr[\operatorname{nrd}d_R=p],
   \Pr[\operatorname{nrd}d_R=q],
   \Pr[\operatorname{nrd}d_R=N]\bigr)
   =\frac{(pq,q,p,1)}{(p+1)(q+1)}.
   \]

   A greatest common left divisor has the same norm law.  There are no other norm
   values and no associate multiplicity correction.
4. For two independent samples, \(\alpha\beta\in r\mathcal H\) exactly when
   \(I_r(\beta)=K_r(\alpha)\), so being divisible by exactly one of \(p,q\) has
   the same probability \((p+q)/((p+1)(q+1))\).
5. For \(K\) iid uniform samples, every-pair testing with one handedness has

   \[
   \Pr(\text{some proper gcd})\le
   \binom K2\frac{p+q}{(p+1)(q+1)}.
   \]

   On \(p<q<2p\) this is \(O(K^2/\sqrt N)\).  Thus every fixed
   \(K=\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\) has exponentially small success along an
   infinite balanced family.

### Proof from the exact normalization

For odd \(m\), the integral-coordinate part of \(S_m\) has
\(r_4(m)=8\sigma(m)\) elements.  Doubling a half-integral element gives an
all-odd representation of \(4m\).  A representation of \(4m\) has either zero
or four odd coordinates; the all-even representations are counted by \(r_4(m)\),
while

\[
r_4(4m)=8\sum_{d\mid4m,\ 4\nmid d}d=24\sigma(m).
\]

Hence the half-integral part has \(16\sigma(m)\) elements and
\(|S_m|=24\sigma(m)\).  This gives the first claim.

The odd-prime splitting does not hide a congruence condition.  Over
\(\mathbb F_r\), choose \(u,v\) with \(u^2+v^2=-1\), which exists because the
number of such pairs is \(r-\left(\frac{-1}{r}\right)>0\), and put

\[
I=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\v&-u\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then \(I^2=J^2=-1\), \(IJ=-JI\), and \(1,I,J,IJ\) span
\(M_2(\mathbb F_r)\).  Since 2 is invertible, this realizes
\(\mathcal H/r\mathcal H\simeq M_2(\mathbb F_r)\).  It works at the edge
\(r=3\), for example with \(u=v=1\).

For \(\alpha\in S_N\), its local determinant is zero.  Its local matrix cannot
be zero: that would put \(\alpha\) in \(r\mathcal H\), forcing
\(r^2\mid\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha)\).  Thus every local matrix has rank one.

The 24 units act freely on nonzero \(\alpha\), because the rational Hamilton
quaternion algebra is a division algebra, and left multiplication preserves row
space.  If rank-one reductions \(A,B\) have the same row line, then
\(B\operatorname{adj}(A)=0\).  Therefore equality of both local row lines gives

\[
\beta\bar\alpha\in p\mathcal H\cap q\mathcal H=N\mathcal H.
\]

The element \(u=\beta\bar\alpha/N\) lies in \(\mathcal H\), has norm one, and
satisfies \(\beta=u\alpha\).  Thus the row map is injective on left-unit orbits.
Both its source and target have \((p+1)(q+1)\) elements, so it is bijective.
For rank-one \(A\), the row of \(\operatorname{adj}(A)\) is obtained from
\(\operatorname{im}(A)\) by the fixed projective quarter-turn
\((x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)\).  Conjugation therefore gives the image/right-unit
version.  This proves exact uniformity and independence of the two local row
coordinates under a uniform sample.

For completeness, the one-sided gcd convention is essential.  If \(d_R\) is a
greatest common **right** divisor, then

\[
\mathcal H\alpha+\mathcal H\beta=\mathcal H d_R.
\]

The Hurwitz order is Euclidean on both sides: for every real quaternion one of the
two Hurwitz lattice cosets \(\mathbb Z^4\) and
\((\mathbb Z+\tfrac12)^4\) is at squared distance at most \(1/2<1\), giving
left and right division with smaller norm.  Thus the displayed principal-ideal
gcd identity is valid.

Modulo \(r\), the left ideal \(M_2(\mathbb F_r)A\) consists of matrices whose
rows lie in \(R_r(A)\).  Two such minimal left ideals agree when their row lines
agree and otherwise sum to the whole matrix algebra.  Consequently

\[
r\mid\operatorname{nrd}(d_R)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R_r(\alpha)=R_r(\beta).
\]

Moreover \(\alpha=a d_R\) implies
\(N=\operatorname{nrd}(a)\operatorname{nrd}(d_R)\), so squarefreeness forces
\(\operatorname{nrd}(d_R)\in\{1,p,q,N\}\).  The two local equality events are
independent with probabilities \(1/(p+1)\) and \(1/(q+1)\), giving the table.
Right ideals \(A M_2(\mathbb F_r)\), which are determined by image lines, give
the left-gcd law.  Units only change a right gcd by left association (and a left
gcd by right association), so the norm law has no multiplicity ambiguity.

Finally, rank-one matrices satisfy \(AB=0\) exactly when
\(\operatorname{im}(B)=\ker(A)\).  With different independent source samples,
these are independent uniform projective lines, independently in the two CRT
components.  This proves the product law.

## All-pairs and balanced asymptotics

The pairwise union bound above is exact enough.  The local collision probability
is

\[
C_r(K)=1-\frac{(r+1)_K}{(r+1)^K}
\]

for \(K\le r+1\), and is one afterward.  The complete local equality partitions
are independent, and a proper pair exists exactly when those two partitions
differ.  In particular, local ideal expressions built only from the listed
lattice operations cannot distinguish the two components unless a pair collides.

If \(p<q<2p\), then \(p=\Theta(\sqrt N)\).  Hence the union bound is
\(O(K^2/\sqrt N)\), and a polynomial in the input bit length gives exponentially
small probability.  Conversely, the occupancy formula puts constant collision
mass at \(K=\Theta(\sqrt p)=\Theta(N^{1/4})\).  There are infinitely many such
pairs: choose arbitrarily large primes \(p\) and apply Bertrand's postulate to
obtain a prime \(q\) with \(p<q<2p\).

## Fixed transforms and conjugation

All assertions below are local at \(r=p,q\); known denominators coprime to \(N\)
may be cleared before taking an ordinary integer gcd.

* A fixed \(a\alpha b\) or \(a\bar\alpha b\) with multiplier norms coprime to
  \(N\) applies invertible projective maps locally.  Different independent source
  samples therefore remain independent and uniform in orientation.
* For two unbarred right transforms of one source, the corrected relative action
  is \(B_1B_2^{-1}\).  A nonidentity element of
  \(PGL_2(\mathbb F_r)\) has at most two fixed lines.  If it is scalar in exactly
  one CRT component, all three coordinates of a cleared trace-free quaternion are
  divisible by exactly that factor, and their joint gcd with \(N\) already finds
  it.  If it is scalar in both, the collision is simultaneous.
* For rank-one \(A\),

  \[
  \operatorname{row}(A)=\operatorname{row}(\operatorname{adj}A)
  \Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{tr}(A)=0.
  \]

  If the row spaces agree, \(A+\operatorname{adj}A=\operatorname{tr}(A)I\)
  has rank at most one, impossible for nonzero trace; zero trace gives
  \(\operatorname{adj}A=-A\).  Thus self-conjugation gives a proper right gcd
  exactly when \(\gcd(|\operatorname{trd}\alpha|,N)\) is proper.  The image-line
  statement is identical.
* For a mixed pair \(X=AB\), \(Y=\operatorname{adj}(A)C\),

  \[
  X\operatorname{adj}(Y)
  =AB\operatorname{adj}(C)A
  =\operatorname{tr}(B\operatorname{adj}(C)A)A.
  \]

  Since \(A\ne0\), row equality is exactly vanishing of the displayed scalar.
  Globally it is the explicit integer linear form
  \(\operatorname{trd}(b\bar c\alpha)\).  Vanishing in exactly one component is
  therefore already detected by its ordinary gcd with \(N\).  This is a
  reduction of that fixed mixed collision, not a zero-density theorem for a
  family of forms.

Left-handed versions follow by reversing multiplication.  None of these arguments
controls a transform selected from hidden sample-dependent information.

## Exact nonuniform boundary

For iid draws from an arbitrary joint orientation distribution \(\mu\), let
\(E_p\) and \(E_q\) be equality of the corresponding local lines.  Then

\[
\Pr(E_p)=\sum_L\mu_p(L)^2,\quad
\Pr(E_q)=\sum_M\mu_q(M)^2,\quad
\Pr(E_p\cap E_q)=\sum_{L,M}\mu(L,M)^2.
\]

The proper-gcd event is their exclusive-or, so its exact probability is

\[
\sum_L\mu_p(L)^2+\sum_M\mu_q(M)^2
-2\sum_{L,M}\mu(L,M)^2.
\]

This confirms the author's boundary: an asymmetrically concentrated nonuniform
sampler can evade the obstruction.  Constructing such a factor-free sampler is
not ruled out.

## Smallest edge checks and non-extension counterexamples

Run F15-A01 independently enumerated the normalized Hurwitz order.

* **Smallest odd distinct-prime edge \(N=15=3\cdot5\).**  It found 576 elements,
  24 row-orientation pairs and 24 image-orientation pairs, each with fibre 24.
  Among all \(576^2\) ordered pairs, the right- and left-handed categories were

  \[
  (1,3,5,15)=(207360,69120,41472,13824),
  \]

  giving probabilities \((5/8,5/24,1/8,1/24)\) and proper probability \(1/3\).
  Direct right- and left-divisor enumeration matched row and image lines,
  respectively.  Direct products produced the same table.  The run also checked
  73,728 mixed barred/unbarred local equivalences and 12,288 asymmetric scalar
  gcds.
* **Ramification at 2.**  The theorem must not be extended to \(p=2\).
  \(\mathcal H/2\mathcal H\) has 12 units, whereas \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\) has 6,
  so these rings are not isomorphic.  At the smallest affected squarefree
  semiprime, \(|S_6|=96\), while the false product formula would give
  \(24(2+1)(3+1)=288\).
* **Repeated primes.**  The theorem must not be extended to \(N=p^2\).
  \(|S_9|=24(1+3+9)=312\), not \(24(3+1)^2=384\), and elements such as
  \(3u\) reduce to rank zero modulo 3.  The rank-one orientation and
  \(\{1,p,q,N\}\) squarefree argument therefore genuinely require distinct
  primes.

These two failures also have short symbolic certificates.  With
\(\omega=(1+i+j+k)/2\), every residue modulo \(2\mathcal H\) is uniquely
\(x_0+x_1i+x_2j+x_3\omega\), \(x_i\in\mathbb F_2\), and its norm parity is

\[
x_0+x_1+x_2+x_3(x_0+x_1+x_2)+x_3.
\]

For \(x_3=0\), four of the eight residues have odd norm; for \(x_3=1\), all
eight do.  Odd norm is equivalent to invertibility (use
\(x\bar x=\operatorname{nrd}(x)\)), proving the count 12.  By contrast
\(|GL_2(\mathbb F_2)|=(4-1)(4-2)=6\).  For even \(m\), a half-integral norm-
\(m\) element would give four odd squares summing to \(4m\), impossible modulo
8; hence \(|S_6|=r_4(6)=8(1+2+3+6)=96\).  The already-proved odd formula gives
\(|S_9|=24\sigma(9)=312\).

The finite run is a check, not the proof of the unbounded theorem.  Its source,
hard timeout, log, JSON certificate, and hashes are recorded in `RUN_MANIFEST.md`.

## Final classification

After the corrections above, F15 is valid **evidence against the exact auxiliary
mechanism** of polynomially many iid uniform-orientation samples followed by raw
one-sided gcds, fixed-menu projective incidences, or the explicitly analyzed fixed
transforms.  It establishes no obstruction to:

* an efficiently sampled nonuniform distribution with asymmetric collision
  energy;
* adaptive or nonlinear sample-dependent transforms;
* global integral ideal invariants outside the local equality/rank lattice; or
* non-collision quaternion invariants.

It also supplies no exact-uniform sampler.  Therefore it neither solves nor
refutes the top-level factoring statement.
