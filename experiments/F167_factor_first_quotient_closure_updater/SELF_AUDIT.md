# F167 self-audit

## Verdict

The candidate is internally consistent. The result is conditional and does
not close the source problem. It is ready for an independent hostile audit,
not for promotion.

## 1. Main identities checked

### Absolute-order screen

For every local order \(r_j\),

\[
d^{\Lambda_B}=1\pmod {R_j}
\iff r_j\mid\Lambda_B
\iff \sigma(r_j)\le B.
\]

Thus the one/proper/global gcd trichotomy is exact. On a global return,
prime-by-prime divisor stripping either separates local valuations by a
proper gcd or proves that all exact local orders are equal.

### Common-order merge

If \(d\) has common local order \(m\), local cyclicity gives

\[
|\langle g,d\rangle|=\operatorname{lcm}(M,m).
\]

The relative order is the quotient by \(M\). This includes shared prime
factors. F166 therefore applies.

### Fingerprint kernel

In the cyclic local group \(K_j\), the kernel of the \(M\)-power map has
size \(M\) because \(M\mid|K_j|\). It is the unique subgroup of order
\(M\), namely \(H_j\). Hence

\[
K_j/H_j\cong K_j^M=F_j.
\]

This is the central point of the BFS theorem.

### Closed BFS

Every new fingerprint is compared with every stored value. On a no-factor
run, globally distinct stored values remain distinct in each hidden
component. A closed table therefore projects bijectively to each \(F_j\).
The closed group and every generated local quotient have the same exact
cyclic order \(\kappa\).

### Lifted generator

The stored word \(a\) satisfies \(a^M=y\). If \(y\) generates the closed
fingerprint group, then \(aH_j\) has exact order \(\kappa\) in every local
quotient. F166 gives common order \(M\kappa\), subject to its factor-first
screens.

## 2. Edge cases checked

- **\(\kappa=1\).** There is no strict order growth. Local membership is not
  global membership. The statement requires alignment for the stronger
  conclusion.
- **Exact BFS duplicate.** The algorithm still compares the candidate with
  all other stored values. It does not stop at the exact duplicate.
- **More than two CRT components.** A gcd of a difference is proper whenever
  equality holds in a nonempty proper subset. The injection proof applies to
  every component.
- **Odd prime powers.** Their unit groups are cyclic, so the local kernel
  proof applies. Even prime powers are outside the statement.
- **Shared factors of \(M\), \(m\), and \(\kappa\).** The proof uses lcm and
  quotient orders, not a coprimality assumption. The normal F166 gcd screens
  remain required.
- **Positive-generator BFS.** In a finite group, the monoid generated by
  units equals the subgroup they generate. Explicit inverse edges are
  optional.
- **Changing common-order state.** All fingerprint tables and capacity
  counts reset. Only exact global relations and provenance persist.
- **Changing integer basis.** A persistent endpoint basis records every
  split and retains historical generators. No equality is inferred from a
  recomputed parity basis alone.

## 3. Claims deliberately not made

- A capacity lower bound is not a factor.
- Many hard blocks need not give independent quotient directions.
- Closure below a quasipolynomial cap is not guaranteed.
- Strict common-order growth is not yet factor correlation.
- F166 state construction need not preserve the full global named subgroup.
- P98 is only a related stable-capacity warning. It is not asserted to be an
  instance of the F167 state.
- The operational corollary proves cost, not success.

## 4. Closest-result check

- The single absolute screen plus exact order reduction strictly changes the
  P87 operational decoder: the punctured bank is not needed once a global
  return is reduced factor-first.
- The BFS enumeration operation resembles P92/P93, but its object and output
  differ. It enumerates the quotient fingerprint group and lifts a generator
  to a new common-order state.
- The one-block BFS is a form of the F161 relative-order scan.
- The finite-bank capacity inequality is F164. The new conditional step is
  to make the bank the complete generated fingerprint group when closure
  fits under the cap.
- The final no-alignment state lift is F166.

The novelty is therefore the exact composition and state machine. No claim
is made that the individual algebraic tools are new.

## 5. Required independent audit attacks

An independent audit should try to break these points.

1. Is the kernel of the local \(M\)-power map always exactly \(H_j\) under
   the stated common-order certificate?
2. Does comparison against all stored fingerprints suffice to certify every
   projection as injective?
3. Does a lifted fingerprint generator always have relative order
   \(\kappa\), including shared prime factors with \(M\)?
4. Are the F166 gcd screens sufficient when \(\kappa\) shares a factor with
   \(N\)?
5. Does optional alignment of \(a^\kappa\) and the residual words really
   compress the full global named subgroup?
6. Is the full provenance representation quasipolynomial under the stated
   cap, not only the number of residues?
7. Does any wording accidentally turn the P98 analogy into an asserted
   reduction?

No computation supports this candidate. Every claim must stand on the proof.
