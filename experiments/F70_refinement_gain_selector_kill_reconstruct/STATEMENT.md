# Proof-blind reconstruction target: refinement-created subgroup expansion

Do not read any other F70 artifact, audit, registry entry, or proof. Reconstruct
the claims below independently from this statement. Check every numerical
identity and every scope boundary. Report PASS only if all claims follow.

## Model

Let a transcript contain pairwise-coprime gcd-free integer blocks \(Q\).
Append a canonical inverse relation \(gw=1+kN\), and jointly gcd-refine all
old and new endpoints.

For this one refinement step:

- \(\sigma\) is the sum, over old block types, of “number of descendants
  minus one”;
- \(\nu\) is the number of new refined block types that divide no old block;
- \(r\) and \(r'\) are the old and new numbers of distinct blocks.

Reconstruct:

\[
r'=r+\sigma+\nu.
\]

For a fixed finite transcript, let \(R\) be its final number of distinct
nontrivial blocks, and let

\[
L=\sum_x\left\lceil\log_2(x+1)\right\rceil
\]

over all endpoint integers in that transcript. Prove \(R\le L\) and the
telescoping identity

\[
\sum_t(\sigma_t+\nu_t)=R-r_0\le L-r_0.
\]

Explain why this is not a polynomial stopping bound for an adaptive run.

## Old subgroup at \(N=4033\)

Verify

\[
N=4033=37\cdot109=\Phi_{36}(2).
\]

Prove that \(2\) has order \(36\) modulo both \(37\) and \(109\), using

\[
2^{18}=-1\pmod {37},\quad 2^{12}=26\pmod {37},
\]

\[
2^{18}=-1\pmod {109},\quad 2^{12}=63\pmod {109}.
\]

Deduce that \(H_0=\langle2\rangle\) contains no element that is \(+1\) or
\(-1\) in exactly one CRT component. Thus no \(h\in H_0\) gives a proper
factor through \(\gcd(h\pm1,N)\).

## Immediate-square family

Let \(s>1\) be odd and \(s\equiv1\pmod3\), and define

\[
N_s=\frac{4s^2-1}{3}
=(2s-1)\frac{2s+1}{3}.
\]

Prove:

1. the two displayed factors are coprime odd integers greater than one;
2. \(N_s+1=2(2s^2+1)/3\), with the second factor odd;
3. two indexed copies of the old relation value \(N_s+1\) have only the
   duplicate square-class dependency, whose positive root is
   \(N_s+1\equiv1\pmod {N_s}\);
4. the two copies authorize \(g=4\), whose canonical inverse is \(w=s^2\);
5. \(gw=4s^2=1+3N_s=(2s)^2\), and the root \(2s\) splits \(N_s\).

For the subfamily \(s\equiv55\pmod {1530}\), verify that the direct sign,
inverse-pair, and discriminant screens are trivial before the exact-square
screen succeeds.

For \(s=55\), verify \(N=4033\), \(g=4\), \(w=3025\), and that joint
refinement has \(\sigma=0\) although the appended exact square has a
non-global root and exposes both factors. State precisely that old-block
splitting is not necessary for immediate useful feedback; do not claim zero
total block gain.

## Strict refinement-created subgroup expansion at \(N=4033\)

Start from the three relations

\[
2\cdot2017=1+N,
\]

\[
64\cdot3970=1+63N,
\]

\[
8\cdot3529=1+7N.
\]

Verify that complete refinement gives

\[
Q_0=\{2,2017,1985,3529\},
\qquad H(Q_0)=H_0=\langle2\rangle.
\]

The authorized product \(g=2^{11}=2048<N\) has canonical inverse \(w=3905\).
Verify

\[
gw=1+1983N.
\]

Show that all immediate sign, difference, discriminant, and exact-square
screens fail. Joint refinement has the sole old/new overlap

\[
\gcd(1985,3905)=5,
\]

and gives

\[
1985=5\cdot397,\qquad 3905=5\cdot781,
\]

\[
Q_1=\{2,2017,5,397,3529,781\}.
\]

Prove \(\sigma=1\), \(\nu=1\). Show that private block \(781\) prevents a
new square-class dependency at this step.

Prove the strict subgroup expansion

\[
H(Q_1)=\langle H_0,5\rangle\supsetneq H_0
\]

using

\[
5=2^{23}\pmod {37},
\qquad
2^{23}=77\ne5\pmod {109}.
\]

Then verify that the enlarged subgroup contains

\[
x=5\cdot2^{-23}=5\cdot2^{13}=630\pmod {4033},
\]

with

\[
\gcd(x-1,4033)=37.
\]

Finally, prove that the exact positive whole-block occurrence box generated by
\(Q_1\) cannot contain the representative \(630\): blocks larger than \(630\)
cannot occur, while products of \(2\) and \(5\) cannot supply its prime
factors \(3\) and \(7\). Explain why
\(5\cdot2^{13}\equiv630\pmod {4033}\) uses modular reduction and is a
different operation.

## Required classification

Decide whether the claims prove the following narrow result:

> Endpoint gcd refinement can expose a generator outside a separator-free old
> subgroup and enlarge it to a subgroup that contains a factor-bearing
> element, even though both feedback endpoint residues lie in the old
> subgroup.

Also state what is not proved:

- no uniform selector or sampler;
- no polynomial stopping bound;
- no all-input success theorem;
- no claim that \(\sigma\) alone ranks candidates;
- no factoring algorithm.
