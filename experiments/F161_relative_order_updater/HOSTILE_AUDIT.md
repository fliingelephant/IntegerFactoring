## Verdict

**PASS.** The stated updater is correct for every odd prime-power CRT
decomposition under its explicit input hypotheses. I found no failed
quantifier, missing gcd outcome, false common-order certificate, incorrect
Pohlig--Hellman step, noncyclic aligned presentation, bad Smith word,
incorrect probability bound, or hidden super-quasipolynomial operation.

The result is a conditional decoder and monotone progress theorem. It does
not supply the released unit `d`. It also gives no bound on inert blocks or
blocks whose relative orders exceed `B`. Thus it is not a factoring
algorithm. This nonclaim is stated correctly.

## 1. Common-order input and prime-power scope

For each hidden component

\[
R_j=p_j^{\alpha_j},
\]

equation (2) makes the local order of `g` divide `M`. If that order is a
proper divisor, it divides `M/ell` for some prime `ell|M`. Then `p_j`
divides `g^(M/ell)-1`, which contradicts the certificate gcd being one.
Therefore every local order is exactly `M`.

This argument needs only divisibility by `p_j`. It remains valid when a
difference is divisible by part of `R_j` but not by all of it. Such partial
divisibility would already give a proper factor.

The screen `gcd(M,N)` is also correct. Exact local order gives

\[
M\le\varphi(R_j)<R_j\le N,
\]

so `M<N`. Hence this gcd is either one or a proper factor. On the surviving
branch, no local residue characteristic divides `M`. This fact is used
correctly in the digit alignment and final order certificate.

The proof uses that the unit group modulo an odd prime power is cyclic. It
does not silently replace `R_j` by a prime field. It covers one component,
several components, repeated prime powers, and more than two distinct
primes.

## 2. The bounded scan is exact

In the cyclic unit group modulo `R_j`, the solutions of `y^M=1` form the
unique order-`M` subgroup `H_j`. Thus

\[
R_j\mid d^{eM}-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^e\in H_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e_j(d)\mid e.
\]

If a scan gcd contains only part of a prime power, or only a proper subset
of the CRT components, it is a proper factor. On a no-factor run, each gcd
is therefore one or `N`.

If the first non-one result is `N` at exponent `e`, every local relative
order divides `e`. A smaller local relative order `f<e` would have made the
earlier gcd at `f` non-one. Therefore all local relative orders equal `e`.
If all first `B` gcds are one, no local relative order can be at most `B`.
This proves both exact scan outcomes.

The additional `gcd(e,N)` has safe priority. It either gives a proper
factor or proves that `e` is coprime to every residue characteristic. It
cannot equal `N`, because a local relative order is smaller than `R_j` and
hence smaller than `N`. The later proof uses only the surviving coprime
branch.

## 3. Factor-first digit alignment

At a common return, `x=d^e` satisfies `x^M=1`. In each component it has one
local logarithm `a_j modulo M` with respect to `g`, because the local
`M`-torsion is exactly `H_j`.

For a prime power `ell^c|M`, the displayed digit formula is the standard
one:

\[
Y_t=(XG^{-A_t})^{\ell^{c-1-t}}=H^{b_t}.
\]

The screen `gcd(M,N)=1` makes `ell` different from every `p_j`. Reduction
modulo `p_j` is injective on the order-`ell` group generated by `H`.
Therefore a wrong digit candidate has gcd one in that component. A correct
candidate contains the full component `R_j`.

If the hidden digits disagree, a candidate used by a nonempty proper set of
components gives a proper gcd. If they agree, exactly one candidate gives
`N`; all others give one. Repeating this for every digit and then applying
integer CRT either factors `N` or returns one global exponent `a modulo M`.

The cost is digit-wise. It is

\[
\sum_{\ell^c\parallel M}c\ell
\le B\log_2M.
\]

It does not enumerate `ell^c`. Thus a large prime power in `M` does not
break the stated quasipolynomial bound.

## 4. Alignment forces a cyclic extension

After alignment, the public relations are

\[
G^M=1,
\qquad
D^e=G^a.
\]

Their integer relation matrix has determinant `Me`, so the presented group
`P` has order `Me`. In each component, `H_j` has order `M`, and the coset of
`d_j` has order `e`. Hence the image group `K_j` also has order `Me`. The
natural surjection from `P` to `K_j` is therefore an isomorphism.

Each `K_j` lies in a cyclic odd-prime-power unit group. Hence `P` is cyclic,
which is equivalent to

\[
\gcd(M,a,e)=1.
\]

The global map is injective because every component map is injective.
Therefore the global named subgroup and every local image have the same
exact cyclic order `Me`. This step correctly explains why equal relative
indices without aligned logarithms are insufficient.

## 5. The deterministic Smith word is correct

Let `E_0` be the product of primes dividing `e` but not `M`. The CRT choice

\[
s=a\pmod M,
\qquad
s=1\pmod {E_0}
\]

makes `s` coprime to `e`. For a prime shared by `M` and `e`, this follows
from `gcd(M,a,e)=1`. For every other prime of `e`, it follows from the
second congruence.

Choose `u,v` with `ue+vs=1`. The homomorphism

\[
P\longrightarrow \mathbf Z/(Me),
\qquad
G\mapsto e,
\quad
D\mapsto s
\]

respects both relations and is onto. Since both groups have order `Me`, it
is an isomorphism. The word `h=g^u d^v` maps to one, so it has exact order
`Me` globally and in every component.

The screens for `(h,Me)` are exactly one. The earlier `gcd(M,N)` and
`gcd(e,N)` screens make `Me` coprime to each `p_j`. Reduction modulo
`p_j` is then injective on the order-`Me` subgroup. Thus
`h^(Me/ell)` is nonidentity even modulo `p_j` for every prime `ell|Me`.

This verifies the orientation, sign, shared-prime case, negative exponent
case, and the edge case `M=1`.

## 6. Optional sampler

The map

\[
(\mathbf Z/(Me))^2\to\langle g,d\rangle
\]

is onto. Uniform independent exponents therefore give a uniform group
element. The cyclic group has `phi(Me)` generators, so the success
probability is exactly `phi(Me)/(Me)`.

For distinct prime factors `q_1<...<q_w`, one has `q_i>=i+1`. Therefore

\[
\frac{Me}{\varphi(Me)}
\le\prod_{i=1}^w\frac{i+1}{i}
=w+1
\le\log_2(Me)+1.
\]

The stated expected-trial bound and almost-sure termination follow. The
deterministic Smith word is stronger, so the proof does not depend on this
random route.

## 7. Complexity, smoothness, and iteration

The scan makes `B` modular exponentiations and gcds. Each exponent has
`O(n+log B)` bits. The alignment uses at most `B log_2 M` digit tests.
Trial division factors `e<=B` in quasipolynomial time. All other arithmetic
has polynomial bit complexity in the stated operands.

Every prime factor of the old `M` is at most `B`, and every prime factor of
`e` is at most `e<=B`. Hence the new order remains supplied `B`-smooth.

Each successful growth has `e>=2`, so after `t` such calls,

\[
2^tM_0\le M_t< N.
\]

Thus `t<n`. A polynomial number of calls, each with a fixed
quasipolynomial bound in `n`, still has quasipolynomial total cost. The
statement correctly excludes inert and beyond-cap calls from this bound.

## 8. Independent finite attack

I ran an independent scratch enumeration with elementary integer
arithmetic. It used no factor information inside a claimed public step. It
enumerated every odd `N` from 3 through 299, all CRT prime-power
components, every unit `g` with a valid common-order state surviving
`gcd(M,N)=1`, and every unit `d`. The cap was `min(12,N)`.

The enumeration covered:

- 8,496 common-order states;
- 1,515,276 common scan returns;
- 1,513,876 aligned returns;
- 1,400 hidden-log mismatches;
- 69,572 beyond-cap returns; and
- 33,832 proper-factor returns.

For every case, it checked the local relative orders against the first scan
return. Every hidden-log mismatch produced a proper factor in the displayed
digit algorithm. Every aligned case satisfied `gcd(M,a,e)=1`; the stated
CRT and extended-Euclid word had exact local order `Me`; and every new
certificate screen was one. The range includes prime powers and inputs
with more than two distinct primes.

The four displayed examples also recompute exactly:

- `N=91,d=30` returns first at `e=3`, and the two hidden logs mismatch;
- `N=91,d=17` gives aligned order growth from 2 to 6;
- `N=35,d=2` gives the proper factor 5 at `e=2`; and
- `N=77,d=2,B=2` has local relative orders 3 and 5, both above the cap.

## 9. Material difference and exact boundary

P144/F158 handles a supplied quadratic lift. P146/F159 V2 tests only
`gcd(d^M-1,N)` for one released block and does not align hidden exponents or
construct a new common-order state. F161 adds three real decoder steps:

1. it scans every relative index up to a quasipolynomial cap;
2. it turns hidden-log disagreement into a factor; and
3. it compresses an aligned two-generator extension to one certified
   common-order generator.

These are a material project-level extension of the two promoted results.
They use standard cyclic-group, Smith-form, and Pohlig--Hellman facts. The
frozen files make no unsupported publication-level novelty claim.

The open problem is unchanged on the source side. No result forces a
feedback refinement to release a useful unit, forces its relative order to
be at most `B`, or bounds how many inert and beyond-cap candidates occur.
A statement-only blind reconstruction is still required before promotion.
