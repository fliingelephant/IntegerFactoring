## Candidate and scope difference

The candidate applies one finite-theta reciprocity to the **whole family**
of sharp prefixes generated by the exact F294 Euclidean window step. It
does not evaluate each F292 scalar Cauchy kernel separately. The goal is
to shorten the common prefix range while retaining both the cut positions
and the complex endpoint weights. The cached Hiary/Kuznetsov papers were
read before any new literature search; no additional paper was needed.

An actual phase discrepancy in the cached Kuznetsov equation(7) was found
and retained. `SOURCE_FORMULA.md` gives the printed version, the derivation
of the coefficient used here from equations(16),(17), and an exact
nonzero difference witness. The identity below uses the derived-i/2
coefficient, not the printed-1/2 coefficient.

## Start with the exact sharp-window moment

Let0<a<m,0<=b<m and

    H=floor((a*(m-1)+b)/m),
    n_j=ceil((m*j-b)/a)-1=floor((m*j-b-1)/a), 1<=j<=H.

For F_n(alpha,tau)=sum_{k=0}^n e(alpha*k+tau*k^2), the weighted floor sum

    Q=sum_{i=0}^{m-1} e(alpha*i+tau*i^2)*Z^floor((a*i+b)/m)

satisfies exactly

    Q=Z^H*F_(m-1)(alpha,tau)-(Z-1)*T,
    T=sum_{j=1}^H Z^(j-1)*F_(n_j)(alpha,tau).            (1)

This is finite summation by floor levels. It keeps the inequality endpoint
and the complete outer power of Z. Normalize the quadratic phase using
the exact integer/half-integer shifts and conjugation from Hiary. If the
phase is conjugated, conjugate Z as well and conjugate the complete result
back afterward. For the dyadic charts write the normalized parameters as

    alpha=B/m in[0,1), tau=A/m in(0,1/4], p=2A<=m/2.

All parameters here are public chart data. The outer root Z is retained.

## A full two-parameter reciprocity with centered endpoints

For each n=n_j define

    X_j=n_j+1/2,
    r_j=floor(alpha+2tau*X_j),
    z_j=alpha+2tau*X_j-r_j-1/2 in[-1/2,1/2),
    E_j=(-1)^r_j*e(X_j*(alpha+tau*X_j)).

The centered r_j choice is the root's improvement. The underlying identity
permits arbitrary nonnegative r_j, so no h-argument normalization is needed
for this choice. In particular r_j<=floor(n_j/2)+1. Define

    alpha'=alpha/(2tau), tau'=-1/(4tau),
    lambda=exp(pi*i/4-pi*i*alpha^2/(2tau))/sqrt(2tau),
    C0=-(i/2)*exp(-pi*i*(alpha-tau/2))
                     *h(alpha-tau+1/2,-2tau).

The corrected exact identity gives

    T=lambda*sum_j Z^(j-1)*F_(r_j)(alpha',tau')
       +C0*sum_j Z^(j-1)
       -(i/2)*sum_j Z^(j-1)*E_j*h(z_j,-p/m).            (2)

There is one common h value and one centered variable-argument h family.
Every Gauss and endpoint phase is present. Nothing has been changed into
an absolute-square moment. The common geometric sum is immediate; the last
weighted h family is an explicit remaining boundary operation.

## Remove the nested floor from the bulk, with one exact cut correction

Put c=-b-1 and define integers

    k=floor((p*c+a*(B+A))/m),
    zeta=(p*c+a*(B+A)) mod m,
    R_j=floor((p*j+k)/a),
    delta_j=1_{m*((p*j+k) mod a)+zeta
                         <p*((m*j+c) mod a)}.

Then

    r_j=R_j-delta_j.                                   (3)

To verify it, replace n_j by(m*j+c)/a minus its fractional part. Before
the last floor the omitted quantity lies in[0,p/m), hence below1. The
fractional numerator of the unrounded linear expression is exactly
m*((p*j+k) mod a)+zeta, with common denominator a*m. This proves both the
strict comparison and the possible one-unit correction in(3).

Thus the dual moment in(2) equals

    sum_j Z^(j-1)*F_(R_j)(alpha',tau')
      -sum_{j:delta_j=1}Z^(j-1)*e(alpha'*R_j+tau'*R_j^2). (4)

The first term has a simple linear-floor cut with numerator p instead of m.
The correction is one quadratic endpoint family with a stated residue
inequality modulo a. It is not an unrecorded rounding error.

Interchange the finite sums in the first term of(4). With

    Rmax=max_j R_j,
    J_h=max(1,ceil((a*h-k)/p)),

it becomes

    sum_{h=0}^{Rmax} e(alpha'*h+tau'*h^2)
                      *sum_{j=J_h}^H Z^(j-1).          (5)

The inner sum is zero for J_h>H, and otherwise is
(Z^(J_h-1)-Z^H)/(1-Z). At Z=1 use H-J_h+1. This is a new quadratic-floor
sum and a complete prefix term. Since H<=a and k<5a/4, Rmax<=p+1. The
bulk therefore has at most p+2<=m/2+2 terms, compared with m originally.
Its quadratic coefficient is-m/(2p), so its phase denominator divides2p.
The old root Z, whose order can still be m, remains in the cut weight.

Equations(2)--(5) are the concrete two-modulus transformation. They contract
the bulk length while producing two explicit boundary families: delta_j
and the centered Mordell endpoints. A total bit-complexity contraction
requires aggregating those families; it does not follow from the shorter
bulk alone. Within this fixed outer-frequency frame, the step does not
enumerate M/4 unrelated moduli.

This entire prefix family belongs to **one fixed F294 frequency b0**.
The coefficients A0=b0*beta mod m, B0=-b0*d1 mod m and Z0=e_m(-b0),
and hence the normalized coefficients and child parameter p, depend on b0.
Even a fast completed-endpoint evaluator for this family would still leave
all original odd b0 indices to aggregate. No simultaneous contraction of
those indices, or bound on their b0-dependent child-modulus family, is
established here.

## Endpoint phases do simplify

The root supplied, and this pilot checked exactly, the identities

    E_j*exp(-pi*i*(z_j+1/2)^2/(2tau))
        =exp(-pi*i*(alpha-r_j)^2/(2tau)),
    E_j*exp(-pi*i*(1/2-z_j)^2/(2tau))
        =-exp(-pi*i*(alpha-r_j-1)^2/(2tau)).             (6)

Their remaining factors are1 and-1 because2X_j=2n_j+1 is odd. Thus the
Fresnel parts of the h decomposition carry dual quadratic phases in r_j
or r_j+1. The overall-i/2 in(2) must still be retained. This is useful
structure, rather than a list of arbitrary independent h evaluations.

The root separately investigates the smooth J remainder and its uniform
series in F297. F296 makes no conclusion from the number of distinct z_j:
that count alone cannot obstruct a uniform analytic approximation or a
small moment representation.
