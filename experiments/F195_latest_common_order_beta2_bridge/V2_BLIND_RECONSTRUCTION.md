## 1. Exact common order descends from prime powers to primes

Let

\[
N=\prod_i p_i^{a_i}
\]

and suppose the image of the public unit (g) has exact order (M) modulo
every (p_i^{a_i}). Fix one component. Reduction gives a homomorphism

\[
(\mathbb Z/p_i^{a_i}\mathbb Z)^*
\longrightarrow
(\mathbb Z/p_i\mathbb Z)^*.
\]

Its kernel is a (p_i)-group: for (a_i>1), it consists of the classes
congruent to one modulo (p_i), and for (a_i=1) it is trivial. Since
(gcd(M,N)=1), one has (p_i\nmid M). The cyclic subgroup generated by
(g), whose order is (M), therefore intersects the reduction kernel
trivially. Reduction is injective on that subgroup, so

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{p_i}(g)=M.
\]

Fermat's theorem, or Lagrange's theorem in
((\mathbb Z/p_i\mathbb Z)^*), now gives

\[
M\mid p_i-1,
\qquad p_i\equiv1\pmod M.
\]

The remaining hypotheses of the first published interface also hold.
The assumption already gives (gcd(M,N)=1), so (M) is a unit modulo
(N). Moreover, (M\le\varphi(p_i^{a_i})<p_i^{a_i}\le N), hence (M<N),
and (s=1<N). Applying Gao--Feng--Hu--Pan with (m=M,s=1) yields

\[
O\!\left(
\left\lceil\frac{N^{1/4}}M\right\rceil
\log^{7+3\epsilon}N
\right)
\]

bit operations.

If (N^{1/4}/M) has a numerical-QP upper bound in (n), its ceiling also
does, and the logarithmic factor is polynomial in (n). The total is QP.

Put (x=N^{1/4}/M>0). The balanced enumeration expression is exactly

\[
\frac{\sqrt N}{M^2}=x^2.
\]

For (x\ge1), (x\le x^2). For (x<1), both actual search counts are
bounded below by one and hence are constant. Also, (x) is numerical-QP if
and only if (x^2) is: QP bounds are closed under fixed powers and square
roots. This proves both the terminal and the unchanged QP threshold class.

The prime-power descent is essential. Without (gcd(M,N)=1), the order
modulo (p^a) can contain a (p)-power that disappears modulo (p), so the
congruence modulo the full (M) would not follow.

## 2. The beta-two branch exits before the smooth stage

Assume (N) is odd and

\[
\operatorname{ord}_N(2)>C(n),
\qquad 1\le D\le C(n).
\]

Then (2) is a unit and

\[
D<\operatorname{ord}_N(2).
\]

The order of any unit divides (arphi(N)), and for (N>1),
(arphi(N)\le N-1). Hence

\[
D<N-1,
\]

which verifies the published algorithm's stated range hypothesis.

The two control-flow cases are exhaustive:

- If (2^D<N), then no exponent (1\le e\le D) can satisfy
  (2^e\equiv1\pmod N), because (0<2^e-1<N). Returning (2) as an
  element of order above (D) is sound.
- If (2^D\ge N), the algorithm enters its loop with (M=1), and its first
  tested integer is (eta=2). The granted bounded-order interface reports
  order above (D), because that follows from the certificate. Thus the
  algorithm exits at that test. If a factor-first implementation exposes a
  proper factor even earlier, that is a stronger terminal and does not reach
  the later smooth-number stage either.

The published early-path cost is

\[
O\!\left(
\frac{D^{1/2}\log D}{\sqrt{\log\log D}}\log N
\right).
\]

For numerical-QP (D), its square root, logarithms, and the factor
(log N=O(n)) remain numerical-QP. In the theorem's declared setting this
condition is automatic from (D\le C(n)) and the numerical-QP cap (C).

If one instead chooses (D=N^\delta) for a fixed (delta>0), the main
factor is

\[
D^{1/2}=N^{\delta/2},
\]

while all displayed logarithmic terms contribute (N^{o(1)}). Thus the
same-node cost is (N^{\delta/2+o(1)}), exponential in the bit length. This
choice is an illustrative departure from the theorem's QP cap, not another
case under (D\le C(n)).

A recurrence such as

\[

can amortize work only after a smaller child exists. Here the bounded-order
work is completed before any such child is produced. Writing a later

### 3.1 The P160 direct order-bound certificate

Suppose a public unit has local orders greater than (C). A list of powers
whose relevant exponent differences all lie between (1) and (C) cannot
collide in either local cyclic group: a collision would make a nonzero
difference divisible by a local order greater than (C). For the published
prefix with scale (m^2) and length (k), the direct sufficient range
certificate is

\[
m^2k\le C.
\]

But substitution of the prescribed (k) gives

\[
m^2k
=\Theta\!\left(
m^2\frac{N^{1/2}}{m^{3/2}}
\right)
=\Theta(N^{1/2}m^{1/2}).
\]

Since (m>72), this is already (Omega(N^{1/2})), hence exponential in
(n), throughout the legal interval. A numerical-QP lower-order cap (C)
cannot certify that full published prefix through this elementary exponent
range argument.

This is a boundary on what follows from the order lower bound alone. It does
not prove that the powers collide, and it does not exclude a certificate
using exact orders or other structure.

### 3.2 The P161 rough-order smoothness certificate

Call an integer (T)-smooth if all its prime factors are at most (T), and
call an order (T)-rough if all its prime factors exceed (T). If (m) is
(T)-smooth and (P(k)\le T), then every (i\le k) is (T)-smooth:
every prime factor of (i) is a prime at most (k), and hence is at most
the largest such prime (P(k)\le T). Consequently every

\[
m^2i,
\qquad1\le i\le k,
\]

is (T)-smooth and is coprime to a (T)-rough local order.

Conversely, this particular argument cannot cover every (i\le k) if
(P(k)>T), because the integer (i=P(k)) itself has a prime factor above
(T). For sufficiently large (k), Bertrand's postulate supplies a prime
strictly between (k/2) and (k). Hence

\[
T\ge P(k)>k/2=\Omega(N^{1/8}).

\]

Such a required numerical threshold is exponential in (n), not QP. Thus
the direct smooth-exponent certificate in the published rank-three
interface cannot cross the numerical-QP beta-two gate. This is not a lower
bound against another use of rough order.

\[

\qquad

work because there are at most (B) candidates. For each (eta), the
granted factor-first bounded-order and synchronization interfaces give the

- a gcd or local mismatch exposes a proper factor;
- both hidden components have the same recovered exact order at most (D);
- the tested small prime has order above (D).

Each bounded search is numerical-QP because (D) is. Multiplying a
numerical-QP number of numerical-QP costs remains numerical-QP.

Assume every scan reaches the synchronized-low case. Let (t_\beta) be the

\[

Each (t_\beta) divides the order of each hidden multiplicative group, so

\[

\qquad

\]

The lcm remains an (O(n))-bit integer because it divides both (p-1) and
(q-1); iterative gcd/lcm arithmetic therefore adds only QP work.

### 4.1 Reconstructing the smooth-number root count

Let (Psi(p,B)) be the number of positive (B)-smooth integers at most
(p), as in the granted smooth-number premise. Because (B<p), the prime
(p) itself is not counted as (B)-smooth. Every counted integer (a) is
a product of the scanned ordinary primes. Since every scanned prime has
order dividing (M) modulo (p),

\[
a^M\equiv1\pmod p.
\]

The counted integers lie in (1,\ldots,p-1), so they give distinct elements
of (mathbb F_p). A nonzero polynomial of degree (M) over a field has at
most (M) roots. Applied to (X^M-1), this proves

\[
\Psi(p,B)\le M.
\]

The stated Harvey--Hittmeir smooth-number lower bound applies in the regime

\[
\log\log p=o(\log B),
\qquad
\log B=o(\log p).
\]

Balance gives (log p=\Theta(n)) and
(log\log p=\Theta(\log n)), so these are exactly the two assumptions on
(B). The granted bound yields

\[
M\ge\Psi(p,B)\ge p^{1-o(1)}.
\]

Since (p<\sqrt N<\sqrt2,p), this is equivalently

\[
M=N^{1/2-o(1)}.
\]

### 4.2 Applying the common-congruence terminal

The congruences needed by Gao--Feng--Hu--Pan are now known directly:

\[
p\equiv q\equiv1\pmod M.
\]

Also (M<N). Since (M\mid p-1), it is not divisible by (p), and since
(M\mid q-1), it is not divisible by (q). Hence

\[
\gcd(M,N)=1.
\]

The external interface applies with (m=M,s=1). Its variable search factor
is

\[
\frac{N^{1/4}}M
\le N^{-1/4+o(1)},
\]

which is below one for all sufficiently large inputs; the ceiling is one.
The remaining logarithmic cost is polynomial. Including the prime scan,
bounds searches, lcm arithmetic, and verification, the all-low branch is
numerical-QP.

Notice that this argument does not need to manufacture one public element
of order (M). It invokes the common-congruence theorem directly from
(M\mid p-1,q-1). This avoids an invalid inference that an lcm of orders of
different public elements is automatically the order of one element.

If the scan is nonterminal and not all-low, the exhaustive trichotomy leaves
at least one ordinary prime (eta\le B) in the high-order case. In the P159
state, (operatorname{ord}_N(2)>C); for (D\le C), the first scanned prime
(2) is already such a witness, subject only to the stronger possibility of
an earlier factor terminal. This proves the claimed low/high fork and no
more.

It does not show that different high-order witnesses are independent, that
their exact orders are accessible, or that their generated subgroup has
large rank. It therefore does not solve the mixed branch.

## 5. Canonical carries do not supply the missing hypotheses

For the canonical residue (x_e=[2^e]_N\in[0,N)), one has

\[
0\le2x_e<2N.
\]

There is consequently a unique (c_e\in\{0,1\}) such that

\[
x_{e+1}=2x_e-c_eN.
\]

Modulo every prime factor of (N),

\[
x_e\equiv2^e.
\]

Thus the residue sequence in each hidden field is the orbit of the single
generator (2); adding more exponents does not turn it into a bank of
independent small ordinary bases. It also does not furnish a separately
specified residue modulo one hidden factor, which is the factor-local input
needed by the rank-three interface.

This observation is purely structural. The integer carry bits themselves
can contain information absent from a field-only orbit, and the statement
does not prove that no succinct carry-sensitive construction can exploit
them.

- a lower bound against other rank-three, restricted-smoothness,

These limitations agree with the stated scope. Conditional on the granted
published interfaces, the complete F195 V2 statement passes independent
