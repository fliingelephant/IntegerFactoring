## 1. Separator-free subgroups are graphs

Let \(H\leq G_p\times G_q\) contain no positive separator. The kernel of
\(H\to H_p\) consists of elements \((1,y)\). If \(y\neq1\), this is a
positive separator; if \(y=1\), it is the identity. The kernel is therefore
trivial. The same proof applies to \(H\to H_q\).

Each projection is surjective onto its defined image. Both projections are
therefore isomorphisms and

\[
|H|=|H_p|=|H_q|=h.
\]

Thus \(H\) is exactly the graph of the induced isomorphism
\(H_p\to H_q\). This uses absence of all positive separators in \(H\), not
merely failure of a finite menu of sign tests. The candidate states the
strong hypothesis correctly.

## 2. Quotient maps and the exact transition count

Because \(G\) is abelian, \(H\) is normal in every \(K\) with
\(H\leq K\leq G\). Projection induces the well-defined maps

\[
K/H\longrightarrow K_p/H_p,\qquad kH\longmapsto k_pH_p
\]

and its \(q\)-analogue. They are surjective because \(K\to K_p\) and
\(K\to K_q\) are surjective onto their image definitions. Hence
\(a\mid c\) and \(b\mid c\).

The three group sizes are

\[
|K|=ch,\qquad |K_p|=ah,\qquad |K_q|=bh.
\]

It follows that the two projection kernels inside \(K\) have sizes
\(c/a\) and \(c/b\). Their intersection is only the global identity, by
CRT injectivity. After that identity is removed, the first kernel contains
exactly the positive separators that are \(1\) modulo \(p\), and the second
contains exactly those that are \(1\) modulo \(q\). These two remaining sets
are disjoint. Therefore the exact count is

\[
\left(\frac ca-1\right)+\left(\frac cb-1\right)
=\frac ca+\frac cb-2.
\]

Division by \(|K|=ch\) gives the claimed density. Since \(a,b\mid c\), a
nonidentity kernel exists exactly when \(c>a\) or \(c>b\). A strict extension
with \(c=a=b>1\) is possible: it is simply a larger graph subgroup. Thus
strict subgroup growth alone is correctly shown to be insufficient.

## 3. One actual unit-block split

Suppose the exact integer unit block \(B\) splits as \(B=uv\). Since
\(\gcd(B,N)=1\), both \(u\) and \(v\) are units modulo \(N\). If the residue
of \(B\) is in \(H\), then

\[
v=Bu^{-1}\pmod N.
\]

Consequently

\[
\langle H,u,v\rangle=\langle H,u\rangle,
\]

and \(K/H\) is cyclic, generated by \(uH\). This is exactly the group change
caused by replacing one old block by two descendants while preserving the
old product.

If \(c\) is the order of \(uH\), every element of \(K\) has a representation
\(u^tz\), with \(0\leq t<c\) and \(z\in H\). It is unique: equality of two
such representations puts \(u^{t-t'}\) in \(H\), so \(c\mid t-t'\);
the chosen range forces \(t=t'\), then \(z=z'\).

The order of \(u_pH_p\) is \(a\). Thus a \(p\)-kernel element can occur only
when \(a\mid t\), and every such \(t\) permits exactly one cancelling
\(z\): projection \(H\to H_p\) is bijective. There are \(c/a\) choices of
\(t\) in the range, including the identity case. The \(q\)-side argument is
identical. The candidate's cancellation-word description is therefore
exact.

This is an existence and counting statement. It does not compute the hidden
orders or the cancelling old word. The candidate does not claim otherwise.

## 4. The permanent \(H\)-coset ceiling

Fix \(yH\). For a fixed local component and a fixed sign \(s\), the equation

\[
(yx)_p=s,\qquad x\in H
\]

has at most one solution because \(H\to H_p\) is injective. The same is true
at \(q\). Therefore:

- the two local \(+1\) equations contain at most two coset points;
- the two local \(-1\) equations contain at most two coset points; and
- their union contains at most four coset points.

A point that is the same sign in both components gives a global gcd and is
not a proper success. Coincidences can therefore only decrease the count.
Uniform sampling inside one \(H\)-coset has direct two-sign success
probability at most \(4/h\).

A law that is constant within each \(H\)-coset is exactly a mixture of the
uniform coset laws. Averaging preserves the \(4/h\) ceiling. Uniform law on
any supergroup \(K\geq H\) is such a mixture.

The sharper positive bound also follows directly from the exact formula:

\[
\delta_+(K)
=\frac1{ah}+\frac1{bh}-\frac2{ch}
\leq\frac2h.
\]

For the negative sign, the exact local-coset formula gives

\[
\delta_-(K)
=\frac{\epsilon_p}{ah}
+\frac{\epsilon_q}{bh}
-\frac{2\epsilon}{ch}
\leq\frac2h.
\]

Thus the ceiling is independent of \([K:H]\). If \(h=2^{\Omega(\log N)}\),
the direct-sign density of every \(H\)-invariant law is exponentially small
in the input bit length. This remains only a statement about that class of
laws and tests.

## 5. Adaptive total variation

Let \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) be the prior transcript. Under the candidate's
hypothesis, for every realized history the conditional law \(\mu_t\) is
within \(\varepsilon_t\) of an \(H\)-invariant law \(\nu_t\). The comparison
law may depend on the history; Theorem 3 holds for all such laws. For the
direct-sign event \(S\),

\[
\Pr_{\mu_t}(S\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})
\leq \Pr_{\nu_t}(S)+d_{\rm TV}(\mu_t,\nu_t)
\leq\frac4h+\varepsilon_t.
\]

Conditional expectation and a union bound give

\[
\Pr\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^T S_t\right)
\leq\frac{4T}{h}+\sum_{t=1}^T\varepsilon_t.
\]

No independence between trials is needed. Here each \(\varepsilon_t\) is a
deterministic bound valid for all relevant histories. If one instead records
a history-dependent random error, the last term must be replaced by the sum
of its expectations or by uniform suprema. This is the standard and natural
reading of the candidate's displayed hypothesis.

## 6. Pair products and ratios

If \(X\) is uniform on \(H\), then \(X^{-1}\) is also uniform on \(H\).
Conditional on an independent value \(Y=y\), each variable

\[
X^{\pm1}Y^{\pm1}
\]

is uniform on one \(H\)-coset. Mixing over \(Y\) makes its unconditional law
\(H\)-invariant. This proves all four sign choices. Commutativity is
available because \(G=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\).

If \(Y\) is uniform on \(K\geq H\), the coset mixture weights are uniform, so
the product is uniform on \(K\). Similarly, the ratio of two independent
uniform \(K\)-samples is uniform on \(K\).

A union bound therefore controls any fixed polynomial list of such pair
tests, even if different pairs share samples. It also controls a selector
from all pairs of distinct samples in a polynomial-size independent pool, by
unioning over that polynomial-size pair universe. A pair chosen through value-dependent
conditioning need not retain the displayed marginal law; the candidate's
phrase “such pairs” must not be extended to that different setting.

## 7. Abstract family

For diagonal

\[
H=\{(t,t):t\in C_L\},
\]

the only point with a zero component is \((0,0)\), so \(H\) is
separator-free and has size \(L\). If nonzero \(u,v\) are chosen with
\(v-u\) a generator, then

\[
(u,v)+(-u,-u)=(0,v-u).
\]

The right side generates the second coordinate, and the diagonal supplies
the first. Hence \(K=C_L\times C_L\). Its indices are
\(c=L\) and \(a=b=1\), and

\[
\delta_+(K)=\frac{2L-2}{L^2}=\Theta(1/L).
\]

The construction is available for every \(L>2\), for example by taking a
generator \(d\), \(u=d\), and \(v=2d\). The candidate only asks to choose
such \(u,v\); it does not assert their existence for \(L=2\). The family is
an exact asymptotic sharpness example.

It is explicitly abstract. No canonical-inverse split, public integer
realization, infinite semiprime family, or computational lower bound is
claimed.

## 8. Exact scope

The proof applies to monotone refinement of unit blocks. It does not apply if
the old subgroup is known only to have failed a finite sign-test menu; the
full separator-free hypothesis is essential. It gives a hidden group-theory
criterion, not a public way to compute \(a,b,c\) or the cancellation word.

Most importantly, a direct sign-gcd success is a sampled group element whose
canonical residue is \(+1\) or \(-1\) in exactly one CRT component. An exact
square-class decoder is a different operation: it combines integer
relations, takes an exact square root, and then tests the root signs. Its
output law need not be \(H\)-invariant, and Theorem 3 does not bound it.
Integer gcd refinement can also expose a factor before any subgroup sample.

The candidate states these exclusions. It proves no realizability theorem,
no all-input density theorem, no hardness result, and no factoring
algorithm. The historical references to P78, P81, and P83 are not used in
the proof and were not re-audited here.
