# Proof of the F185 fixed-contraction recursive closure theorem

## 1. Exact primary filtering, including nonunits

Fix a hidden component \(R_j=p_j^{e_j}\) and a prime power
\(\ell^a\parallel g_j\). The order-of-a-power formula gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z_\mathcal S)
=\frac{g_j}{\gcd(g_j,P_\mathcal S^n)}.
\tag{1}
\]

For one shift \(\delta\), the prime \(\ell\) divides \(C_\delta\) exactly
when

\[
\ell\mid N+\delta,
\tag{2}
\]

or, after excluding (2),

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(N+\delta)\le K.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed, (2) is the index-zero linear factor. If \(N+\delta\) is a unit
modulo \(\ell\), one of the factors \((N+\delta)^k-1\),
\(1\le k\le K\), vanishes exactly when its multiplicative order is at most
\(K\). Thus every nonunit case is included without assigning it an
undefined multiplicative order.

Since \(g_j<N<2^n\), the relation \(\ell^a\le g_j\) gives \(a<n\).
Therefore one occurrence of \(\ell\) in \(P_\mathcal S\) becomes at least
\(n>a\) occurrences in \(P_\mathcal S^n\). The complete \(\ell^a\)
primary part is deleted if (2) or (3) occurs for at least one menu shift,
and it is otherwise preserved. This proves the exact formula (12) in the
statement for arbitrary primary multiplicities.

Every filtered order divides \(g_j\), hence is coprime to \(p_j\). The
kernel of reduction

\[
(\mathbb Z/p_j^{e_j}\mathbb Z)^\times
\longrightarrow
(\mathbb Z/p_j\mathbb Z)^\times
\tag{4}
\]

is a \(p_j\)-group. Reduction is therefore injective on the cyclic subgroup
generated by \(z_\mathcal S\). Consequently,

\[
z_\mathcal S\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z_\mathcal S\equiv1\pmod {R_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z_\mathcal S)=1.
\tag{5}
\]

This proves that \(H_\mathcal S\) is a product of complete hidden prime
powers. Its only possibilities are one, a proper divisor, or \(N\), even
when \(N\) is itself a prime power. The one-valued branch has a nontrivial
order in every component and satisfies the long-unit conditions (21).

All evaluated factors in (10) are positive: \(N+\delta>1\), and
\((N+\delta)^k-1>0\). No zero exponent factor occurs.

## 2. Every cross resultant is nonzero and contracted

Fix \(\delta\in\mathcal A\), \(\epsilon\in\mathcal B\), and set

\[
D=\epsilon-\delta.
\tag{6}
\]

The menu separation gives

\[
3\le D\le2L+1.
\tag{7}
\]

The resultants with a linear polynomial are explicit:

\[
R_{\delta,\epsilon;0,0}=D,
\tag{8}
\]

\[
R_{\delta,\epsilon;0,l}=D^l-1
\quad(1\le l\le K),
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,0}=|(-D)^k-1|
\quad(1\le k\le K).
\tag{10}
\]

They are all positive because \(D\ge3\).

For \(k,l\ge1\), a common complex root would give two points on unit
circles whose centers have distance \(D\). Their difference has absolute
value at most two, contradicting (7). Hence the resultant is nonzero. Using
the roots \(-\delta+u\), where \(u^k=1\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
&=\prod_{u^k=1}|(D+u)^l-1|\\
&<\bigl(2(D+1)^l\bigr)^k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Since \(\log_2(D+1)\le\lambda\), equations (8)--(11) imply uniformly

\[
0<R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}
<2^{K(1+K\lambda)}.
\tag{12}
\]

Put \(E=K(1+K\lambda)\). A positive integer strictly below \(2^E\) has
bit length at most \(E\). The parameter condition gives

\[
\operatorname{bitlen}(R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l})
\le E\le m=\lfloor\rho n\rfloor.
\tag{13}
\]

Because \(\rho<1\), one has \(m\le n-1\). Thus every resultant is at most
\(2^{n-1}-1\), while an integer of bit length \(n\) is at least
\(2^{n-1}\). Therefore every resultant is strictly below \(N\). Values
equal to one need no recursive call; no value is zero.

Solving \(\lambda K^2+K\le m\) gives exactly the integer range (7) of the
statement. Numerical-QP \(L\) has \(\lambda=(\log n)^{O(1)}=o(n)\), so
the range is eventually nonempty and its upper endpoint has the asymptotic
size in (8).

## 3. Double global identity gives a recursively factored annihilator

Assume

\[
H_\mathcal A=H_\mathcal B=N.
\tag{14}
\]

Both filters act on the same original unit \(w\). Fix any
\(\ell^a\parallel g_j\). The first identity says
\(g_j\mid P_\mathcal A^n\), so \(\ell\mid P_\mathcal A\). Hence for some
\(\delta\in\mathcal A\) and some \(0\le k\le K\),

\[
F_{\delta,k}(N)=0\pmod\ell.
\tag{15}
\]

The second identity similarly gives \(\epsilon\in\mathcal B\) and
\(0\le l\le K\) with

\[
F_{\epsilon,l}(N)=0\pmod\ell.
\tag{16}
\]

The two polynomials are monic, so neither becomes the zero polynomial over
\(\mathbb F_\ell\). Equations (15)--(16), or equivalently the
Sylvester-matrix Bezout identity evaluated at \(N\), imply

\[
\ell\mid R_{\delta,\epsilon;k,l}.
\tag{17}
\]

Thus every rational prime in every \(g_j\) divides \(U\). Strong induction
completely factors each positive resultant greater than one, and merging
all returned valuations gives the exact factorization of \(U\).

For every \(\ell^a\parallel g_j\), equation (17) gives
\(v_\ell(U)\ge1\), while \(a<n\). Therefore

\[
v_\ell(U^n)\ge n>a,
\tag{18}
\]

which proves \(g_j\mid M=U^n\) for every hidden component. No
squarefreeness of \(N\), of a local order, or of a resultant is used.

## 4. Factor-first stripping synchronizes the local orders

Start with the completely factored common multiple \(M\). For every prime
\(r\mid M\), repeatedly compute

\[
D_r=\gcd(w^{M/r}-1,N).
\tag{19}
\]

If \(D_r=N\), replace \(M\) by \(M/r\) and test that prime again. If
\(1<D_r<N\), return the factor. If \(D_r=1\), retain the current copy of
\(r\) and continue with the next prime.

The invariant \(g_j\mid M\) for all \(j\) is preserved whenever a copy is
deleted. Suppose no proper gcd occurs. At termination, for each
\(r\mid M\), the one-valued test says that no local order divides \(M/r\).
Since every \(g_j\) divides \(M\), this is possible only if

\[
v_r(g_j)=v_r(M)
\qquad\text{for every }j.
\tag{20}
\]

Applying (20) to every prime divisor of \(M\) gives

\[
g_j=M
\qquad(1\le j\le s).
\tag{21}
\]

This is an exact common local order with a complete public factorization.
P161 roughness and \(g_j>1\) imply \(M=g_j>T\).

## 5. Local bit complexity

Let

\[
B(n)=L^2(K+1)^2
\tag{22}
\]

be the exact number of cross-resultant slots. Calls for values equal to one
are omitted, so the number of actual recursive calls is at most \(B(n)\).

For every used shift,

\[
\log_2 C_\delta
=O\bigl(K^2(n+\log_2(L+1))\bigr).
\tag{23}
\]

It follows that each explicitly represented menu exponent has bit length

\[
\operatorname{bitlen}(P_\mathcal S^n)
=O\bigl(nLK^2(n+\log_2(L+1))\bigr).
\tag{24}
\]

The polynomials in a resultant have degree at most \(K\) and coefficient
bit length polynomial in \(K\) and \(\log(L+1)\). Standard exact integer
resultant algorithms use polynomial bit complexity in those parameters and
the output length. Computing all \(B(n)\) resultants is therefore QP.

Equation (13) bounds each resultant by \(m\) bits, so

\[
\operatorname{bitlen}(U^n)=O(nB(n)m).
\tag{25}
\]

The number of factor-first prime-copy deletion attempts is at most the
total prime multiplicity of \(U^n\), which is at most
\(\log_2(U^n)\). Every modular exponent in the filters and stripping stage
has QP bit length. Since \(L\) is numerical QP and the contraction condition
gives \(K=O(\sqrt n)\), all construction, exact resultant arithmetic,
modular powering, gcd, merging, and stripping outside the recursive calls
have one uniform deterministic numerical-QP bound \(A(n)\).

## 6. The complete recursive cost

Strong induction supplies correctness only after every smaller factoring
call is discharged. It does not by itself supply a time bound. Let
\(\mathcal T(t)\) be the maximum expected cost of the enclosing recursive
procedure on inputs of bit length at most \(t\). If all recursive calls obey
the same fixed contraction and total call-count bound, then

\[
\mathcal T(t)
\le A(t)+B(t)\mathcal T(\lfloor\rho t\rfloor)
\tag{26}
\]

above a fixed finite base \(t_0\). This recurrence includes all sibling
calls; it is not a single-branch estimate.

Because \(A\) and \(B\) are numerical QP, fixed constants \(C,d>0\) can be
chosen so that the nondecreasing envelope

\[
W(t)=\max_{1\le u\le t}\{2,A(u),B(u)\}
\le2^{C(\log_2(t+1))^d}.
\tag{27}
\]

The recursion depth is at most

\[
h=\left\lceil\log_{1/\rho}(t/t_0)\right\rceil+1
=O(\log(t+1)).
\tag{28}
\]

Unrolling (26), bounding every level by \(W(t)\), and including all leaves
gives

\[
\mathcal T(t)
\le (h+1)\max\{1,\mathcal T(t_0)\}W(t)^{h+1}
=2^{O((\log_2(t+1))^{d+1})}.
\tag{29}
\]

For Las Vegas subcalls, linearity of expectation gives (26). The tree has
finite deterministic depth and finitely many nodes at every level, so
almost-sure termination of the local work and base calls gives almost-sure
termination of the whole tree. All returned prime factorizations are
verified before they are merged.

If one assumes only that every auxiliary is below the parent integer, its
bit length may be \(t-1\). The analogous depth can then be \(\Theta(t)\),
and multiplying a QP branching factor at every level need not remain QP.
The fixed \(\rho<1\) in (13) is therefore essential to the stated cost.

This recurrence accounts only for an enclosing procedure whose recursive
calls satisfy the stated conditions. F185 does not supply the missing
resolution of its wide-shift-hard branch and therefore does not, by itself,
instantiate such a complete factoring procedure.

## 7. Narrow canonical inverse-carry boundary

Let \(1\le x,y<N\) and suppose

\[
xy=1+cN
\tag{30}
\]

with integer \(c\ge0\). If \(c=0\), then \(xy=1\), so \(x=y=1\). If
\(c>0\), then

\[
c=\frac{xy-1}{N}<\frac{xy}{N}<x
\tag{31}
\]

because \(y<N\); symmetrically \(c<y\). Hence
\(0<c<\min(x,y)<N\).

If a positive integer divides both \(c\) and \(x\), it divides both
\(cN\) and \(xy\), hence it divides \(xy-cN=1\). Thus \(\gcd(c,x)=1\),
and the same argument gives \(\gcd(c,y)=1\). Therefore

\[
\gcd(c,xy)=1.
\tag{32}
\]

Factoring \(c\) recursively consequently supplies no prime factor of either
endpoint of this same inverse relation. This proves only the stated
direct-endpoint boundary; it does not exclude other nonlinear constructions
from several carry relations.
