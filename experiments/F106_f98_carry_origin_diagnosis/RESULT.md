# F106 — local carries do not explain the F98 circuit by themselves

**Status:** candidate factor-assisted diagnosis of one fixed circuit. It
requires hostile audit and proof-blind reconstruction before promotion.

## Exact local mechanism

For consecutive values in a trajectory with multiplier \(r\), write

\[
c_{e+1}=r c_e-a_eN,
\qquad
r w_{e+1}=w_e+b_eN.
\]

A zero \(a_e\) forces the two relations to share \(c_e\). A zero \(b_e\)
forces them to share \(w_{e+1}\). If both are zero, their exact relation
values are equal. The F98 first-occurrence rule removes that duplicate.

The selected circuit contains 31 consecutive selected pairs. Of these, 16
have only the first zero carry, 14 have only the second zero carry, and one
has neither. None has both. Allowing selected exponents to skip intermediate
columns gives 51 exact zero-carry chains.

## Circuit diagnosis

The circuit has 230 odd-prime rows and rank 165 on 166 columns. Exact carry
chains explain at least one adjacency in 74 rows. Those rows have rank 71 and
leave three column components of sizes 163, 2, and 1. Thus local carry
structure alone is far from the unique circuit.

The 156 rows with no carry-chain adjacency have rank 145. They leave 19
components, the largest of size 148. Thus accidental-looking overlaps alone
also do not give the unique circuit. Both types are needed.

The stronger finite signal is cross-trajectory coupling:

- 175 rows span more than one oriented trajectory;
- those rows already connect all 166 columns and have rank 163;
- 84 such rows use primes larger than the public bound \(n^2=784\); and
- the largest no-carry prime is 2,814,499.

Small primes at most 784 connect all columns but give only rank 95. Larger
primes give rank 132 but leave 29 components. The useful circuit therefore
mixes local carry links, small shared factors, and larger cross-trajectory
overlaps. It is not generated by the consecutive carry identities alone.

## Meaning and limit

This strengthens the algorithmic interpretation of F98: the useful event is
not one trajectory closing on itself. It is a global coupling among several
feedback trajectories. It also weakens the simple scalable explanation. The
verified carry identities supply only 71 of the required 165 row-rank units.

This finite diagnosis does not determine whether cross-trajectory overlaps
have inverse-polynomial density. At 28 bits, ordinary shared-factor
collisions can be much more common than they are asymptotically. No
all-input factoring law follows.
