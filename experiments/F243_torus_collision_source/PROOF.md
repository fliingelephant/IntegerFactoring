# Proof of the F243 shifted collision theorem

## 1. Universal shifted-support stripping

Fix a sign pair and abbreviate

\[
m_p=p-\epsilon_p,
\quad m_q=q-\epsilon_q,
\quad d=\gcd(m_p,m_q),
\quad s_i=m_i/d.
\]

F242 proves

\[
\gcd(N-\epsilon_p\epsilon_q,m_i)=d
\]

and hence, for every public word `W`, its local residual is

\[
r_i={s_i\over\gcd(s_i,W)}.                         \tag{1}
\]

Let `ell^e || s_i`.  Since `s_i<N<2^n`, one has `e<n`.  If
`ell | A=N^2-1`, then

\[
v_\ell(A^n)\ge n>e.
\]

If `ell` does not divide `A`, it is absent from `A^n`.  Substitution in
(1) proves that `W_0=A^n` leaves exactly `s_i^ext`.  Also

\[
\log_2 W_0=n\log_2(N^2-1)<2n^2.
\]

Now let `ell` be odd and divide `p-epsilon_p`.  Then
`p=epsilon_p mod ell`, so

\[
N^2-1=p^2q^2-1=q^2-1\pmod\ell.
\]

Therefore `ell | A` exactly when `q=+1` or `q=-1 mod ell`.  This is the
claimed cross-sign support equivalence.  The rational prime two always
divides `A` and never occurs in an exterior part.

## 2. Conditional collision bank

P206 applies to any positive integer `s<N`.  For completeness, if

\[
s=\prod_\ell\ell^{e_\ell},
\qquad
\Delta=|Z-Z'|\text{ for }Z\ne Z',
\]

with `Delta=1` on equality, then

\[
\gcd(s,\Delta^n)
=\prod_{\ell^{e_\ell}\parallel s}
\ell^{e_\ell 1_{\ell\mid\Delta}}.                 \tag{2}
\]

The exponent `n` saturates a whole primary because `e_ell<n`.  In
particular, if `kappa_ell>=1/Q` and `R` independent differences are used,
then the chance of missing this prime is at most

\[
(1-1/Q)^R\le e^{-R/Q}.
\]

There are fewer than `n` distinct primes in `s_i^ext`.  With
`R=ceil(Q log(2n))`, a union bound gives probability at least `1/2` that
every exterior prime occurs in some difference.  Equation (2), together
with `A^n`, then gives `s_i | W`.

The uniform-divisor formula is the P206 Parseval calculation with
`A=N^2-1`.  If `mu_ell` is the reduction law in `F_ell^*`, then

\[
C_\ell=\sum_x\mu_\ell(x)^2
= {1\over\ell-1}\sum_\chi
\prod_j\left|
{1\over a_j+1}\sum_{e=0}^{a_j}\chi(b_j)^e
\right|^2.                                          \tag{3}
\]

The divisors are distinct integers and uniform, so the exact-equality mass
is `1/T`.  Hence `kappa_ell=C_ell-1/T`.  The law is supported on the
subgroup generated by the `b_j`, and Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
C_\ell\ge1/h_\ell,
\qquad
\kappa_\ell\ge1/h_\ell-1/T.                        \tag{4}
\]

Assume the bank saturates one fixed side in one fixed orientation.  The
word samples are independent of the later discriminant.  A uniform unit
discriminant has each hidden sign pair with probability `1/4`.  In the
target orientation, (1) has `r_i=1`, and F242 gives clean powered success
at least `1/2`.  Multiplying the saturation, orientation, and powered
probabilities gives

\[
{1\over2}\cdot{1\over4}\cdot{1\over2}={1\over16}.
\]

Each divisor and difference is below `A<N^2`.  Every factor raised to `n`
has `O(n^2)` bits, so the total word length is `O((R+1)n^2)`.

## 3. Quadratic norm counts

Work over `k=F_ell`.  If `D` is nonsquare, `a^2-Db^2` is the norm from
`F_{ell^2}`.  Its zero fibre contains only `(0,0)`, and every nonzero fibre
has `ell+1` points.  If `D` is square, a linear change of variables makes
the form `xy`.  Its zero fibre has `2ell-1` points, and every nonzero fibre
has `ell-1` points.

In either case, the sum of squared fibre sizes is

\[
\ell^3+\ell^2-\ell.
\]

Division by `ell^4` proves

\[
\Pr(Q_D(V)=Q_D(V'))
={\ell^2+\ell-1\over\ell^3}.                       \tag{5}
\]

The same-vector atom is `ell^2/ell^4=1/ell^2`; subtracting it from (5)
gives `(ell^2-1)/ell^3`.

The difference `V-V'` is itself uniform on `k^2`.  The two zero-fibre
counts above therefore give

\[
\Pr(Q_D(V-V')=0)=
{2\ell-1\over\ell^2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
{1\over\ell^2},                                    \tag{6}
\]

in the split and nonsplit cases.  The same-vector atom is again
`1/ell^2`.  This proves every norm law in the statement.  An exact integer
zero is a subset of the modular zero event, so replacing it by one cannot
increase useful energy.

## 4. Hilbert--90 and trace resultants

Let

\[
K_D=k[w]/(w^2-D),
\qquad \phi(z)=z/\bar z.
\]

If `D` is square, `K_D=k x k`; if it is nonsquare, `K_D=F_{ell^2}`.
In both cases the kernel of `phi` is the scalar group `k^*`, of size
`ell-1`.  The image is the full norm-one torus, of size

\[
m=\ell-(D/\ell).
\]

Thus a uniform algebra unit maps to a uniform torus point.

The trace observable identifies `U` with `U^{-1}`.  The points `+1` and
`-1` are the two fixed points of inversion; every other orbit has size two.
Therefore

\[
\Pr(\operatorname{tr}U=\operatorname{tr}U')
={2+4(m-2)/2\over m^2}
={2m-2\over m^2}.                                  \tag{7}
\]

The atom `U=U'` has probability `1/m`.  Subtracting it from (7) gives
`(m-2)/m^2`.

Write `z=a+bw` and `z'=c+ew`.  Cross multiplication gives

\[
z\bar z'-z'\bar z=2(bc-ae)w.                       \tag{8}
\]

For algebra units, (8) vanishes exactly when `phi(z)=phi(z')`.  Direct
cross multiplication of the two trace fractions gives

\[
(a^2+Db^2)(c^2-De^2)
-(c^2+De^2)(a^2-Db^2)
=2D(bc-ae)(bc+ae).                                 \tag{9}
\]

This proves the same-discriminant identities and shows why the
discriminant cancels.

For two discriminants, the same calculation gives

\[
(a^2+Db^2)(c^2-Ee^2)
-(c^2+Ee^2)(a^2-Db^2)
=2(Db^2c^2-Ea^2e^2).                               \tag{10}
\]

If the four coefficient factors are units and `D,E` are independent
uniform elements of `k^*`, then (10) prescribes exactly one value of `D`
for every `E`.  Its zero probability is `1/(ell-1)`.  For a law `nu` on
`k^*`, the same probability is a multiplicative correlation

\[
\sum_{x\in k^*}\nu(x)\nu(\lambda x)
\]

for an explicit unit `lambda`.  The resultant transfers concentration in
`nu`; it does not create it.

## 5. Canonical-coordinate collision bound

Fix a unit discriminant modulo `N` and its hidden signs.  The accepted F242
point is uniform on a set of size

\[
M=(p-\epsilon_p)(q-\epsilon_q).
\]

For a fixed first coefficient `x mod N`, the norm-one equation is

\[
Dy^2=x^2-1.
\]

It has at most two roots modulo each hidden prime, hence at most four roots
modulo `N`.  Thus the canonical integer first-coordinate law has every atom
at most `4/M`.  For a fixed second coefficient, the equation

\[
x^2=1+Dy^2
\]

gives the same bound.

A residue class modulo `ell` contains at most `ceil(N/ell)` canonical
integers in `{0,...,N-1}`.  If `rho_a` is the mass in one residue class,
then

\[
\rho_a\le {4\lceil N/\ell\rceil\over M}.
\]

The modular collision probability is

\[
\sum_a\rho_a^2\le\max_a\rho_a.                    \tag{11}
\]

For distinct odd primes,

\[
M\ge(p-1)(q-1)
=N(1-1/p)(1-1/q)\ge {8N\over15}.                   \tag{12}
\]

Equations (11)--(12) give

\[
\Pr(X\equiv X'\pmod\ell)
\le {15\over2N}\left({N\over\ell}+1\right).
\]

Every exterior prime is below `N`, so this is less than `15/ell`.  Exact
integer equality is part of the event and its deletion only decreases the
useful probability.  A union bound gives `30/ell` for the two coordinates.

A Jacobi-sign class contains exactly `phi(N)/2` canonical unit
discriminants.  Repeating the same atom and residue-class argument, now with
atom `2/phi(N)` and `phi(N)>=(8/15)N`, gives

\[
\Pr(D\ne D',\ D=D'\bmod\ell)<{15\over2\ell}.       \tag{13}
\]

This proves the random-discriminant claim.  Mixtures over public
discriminants preserve the coordinate atom bound.

## 6. Exact gcd-divisor law and harmonic bound

Let `X` be uniform modulo `A` and `Z=gcd(X,A)`.  For every divisor `d|A`,
the residues with gcd exactly `d` are `d` times the units modulo `A/d`.
Hence

\[
\mu(d)=\Pr(Z=d)={\varphi(A/d)\over A}\le{1\over d}.\tag{14}
\]

For `ell` coprime to `A`, the useful collision probability is exactly

\[
\kappa_\ell=
\sum_{\substack{d,e\mid A\\d\ne e\\d=e\bmod\ell}}
\mu(d)\mu(e).                                       \tag{15}
\]

Use (14), order the pair so that `e>d`, and write `e=d+kell`.  Extending
the divisor sum to all positive integers gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_\ell
&\le2\sum_{1\le k\le A/\ell}
\sum_{d\ge1}{1\over d(d+k\ell)}\\
&=2\sum_{1\le k\le A/\ell}{H_{k\ell}\over k\ell}\\
&\le {2(1+\log A)^2\over\ell}.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{16}
\]

The middle identity follows by telescoping

\[
{1\over d(d+a)}={1\over a}\left({1\over d}-{1\over d+a}\right).
\]

This proves the universal obstruction.

For reference, the exact character law is also explicit.  If
`A=product b^a`, the exponent `V_b=v_b(Z)` has

\[
\Pr(V_b=v)=
\begin{cases}
(b-1)/b^{v+1},&0\le v<a,\\
b^{-a},&v=a.
\end{cases}
\]

The exponent variables are independent.  Parseval modulo `ell`, followed
by subtraction of the full integer-equality mass, gives the exact value in
(15).  If the divisor support is injective modulo `ell`, (15) is zero.

## 7. Pell meta-order law

Put `Delta=N^2-1` and work in

\[
R=\mathbb Z[w]/(w^2-\Delta).
\]

The element `eta=N+w` has norm one.  For an exterior prime `ell`, `Delta`
is a unit, and the reduction of `eta` lies in the cyclic norm-one torus of
order

\[
\ell-(\Delta/\ell).
\]

Let its exact order be `h`.  Then

\[
\eta^i=\eta^j\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i=j\pmod h.\tag{17}
\]

Counting ordered unequal pairs in the residue classes of
`{0,...,T-1}` modulo `h` gives, for `T=qh+r`,

\[
{h q(q-1)+2rq\over T^2}.                            \tag{18}
\]

Write `eta^k=X_k+Y_kw`.  Since `eta^{-k}=X_k-Y_kw`,

\[
2X_k=\eta^k+\eta^{-k}.
\]

Two such traces are equal over `F_ell` exactly when the two roots of their
common reciprocal quadratic agree, hence

\[
X_i=X_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\eta^i=\eta^j\text{ or }\eta^i=\eta^{-j}.           \tag{19}
\]

Equivalently, `i=plus_or_minus j mod h`.  If `0<=i,j<T`, `i!=j`, and
`h>=2T-2`, neither the nonzero difference `i-j` nor the sum `i+j` can be a
nonzero multiple of `h`.  At the endpoint `h=2T-2`, the only possible sum
is attained by `i=j=T-1` and is excluded.  Thus the useful trace collision
is zero.

Over the integers, `X_0=1`, `X_1=N`, and

\[
X_{k+1}=2NX_k-X_{k-1}.
\]

For `N>1` this sequence is strictly increasing.  Therefore distinct
exponents never create a discarded exact-integer trace equality.  Finally,
`eta^h=1 mod ell` implies `X_h=1 mod ell`.  The public product of
`X_k-1` over every QP-small `k` has QP bit length.  This proves that a
successful bounded Pell window is precisely a small-meta-order event, not a
new random source theorem.
