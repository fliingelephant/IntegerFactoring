## 1. Target and conditional laws

Let (R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z), and identify the multiplication graph with
(R^2) by

\[
(k,x)\longmapsto(k,x,kx).
\]

The unnormalized state mass is (w(kx)), so summing by residual gives

\[
Z_w=\sum_{k,x}w(kx)=\sum_dM_N(d)w(d).
\]

Because (w(0)>0), the event (kx=0) has positive mass, and every state in
that event has the same mass (w(0)). The conditional law on that event is
therefore uniform on (\Omega_N). This part is exact.

Holding (x=b), the refreshed value (a) has law

\[
Q_{b,w}(a)=\frac{w(ab)}{Z_b},
\qquad Z_b=\sum_yw(yb).
\]

Every denominator is positive, even when (w) has zeros, because the term
(y=0) contributes (w(0)>0). Thus sparse support causes no undefined
conditional.

If (b) is a unit, (a\mapsto ab) is a permutation. Hence (Z_b=W), and
the new residual (D=ab) has law

\[
\nu_w(d)=w(d)/W.
\]

Moreover, (a=Db^{-1}), and unit multiplication preserves the ideal and the
gcd generated by a residue. Therefore

\[
1<\gcd(a,N)<N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1<\gcd(D,N)<N.
\]

If (b=0), every candidate residual is zero and

\[
Q_{0,w}(a)=\frac{w(0)}{Nw(0)}=\frac1N.
\]

The hypothesis (w(0)>0) is essential. If (w(0)=0), the zero-held
conditional has zero denominator, and conditioning the target on (d=0) is
not defined. The artifact excludes this case correctly.

Weights with zeros can remove residual fibres from the target support. This
does not affect the proof. The artifact does not claim a mixing theorem. For
completeness, with a standard scan that gives both coordinates a positive
chance, the positive-weight support is in fact connected: refresh one
coordinate to zero, use the zero-held uniform conditional to set the other,
and then enter any desired positive-weight state. A scan that never updates
one coordinate can be reducible. The hazard theorem does not need
irreducibility.

## 2. Direct residual-sampler reduction

Put

\[
H_N=\{d:1<\gcd(d,N)<N\},
\qquad
\theta_w=\nu_w(H_N).
\]

At the public held value (b=1), one conditional call returns
(D\sim\nu_w). A gcd succeeds with exact probability (\theta_w). Fresh
independent calls therefore use (1/\theta_w) calls in expectation when
(\theta_w>0).

This is an exact oracle reduction. To turn it into a classical
expected-polynomial factoring algorithm, the conditional routine must be a
uniform public algorithm. It must take (N) and a public finite description
of (w_N), use no factor-dependent advice, sample exactly, terminate almost
surely, and have one fixed expected polynomial **bit and fair-bit** cost.
Under those hypotheses and a uniform bound

\[
\theta_{w_N}\ge1/P(\log N),
\]

fresh repetitions have expected bit cost at most

\[
\frac{T(N)+\operatorname{poly}(\log N)}{\theta_{w_N}}.
\]

This calculation remains valid if one call's runtime is correlated with its
own output, because the event that call (i) is reached depends only on the
earlier independent calls.

An arbitrary real-valued (w) does not supply such an implementation.
Point evaluation of (w) also does not by itself supply exact normalization
or exact sampling. Lines 73--76 and 249--252 recognize this, but lines
216--220 omit it and assert a direct expected-polynomial factor source for
“any exact residual-only Gibbs construction.” “Exact” alone is not a bit-cost
or uniformity guarantee. The conclusion must repeat the full implementation
hypothesis. Saying that no normalizer or factor is supplied separately does
not replace that hypothesis; an oracle or a nonuniform routine can still hide
either one internally.

## 3. Pathwise hazard and tail

Call an initial state safe when neither free coordinate nor its derived
residual has a proper gcd with (N). For any residue (a), failure of the
gcd screen means either (\gcd(a,N)=1), so (a) is a unit, or
(\gcd(a,N)=N), so (a=0) in (R). Hence, before the stopping time, both
free coordinates are in

\[
C_N=R^\times\cup\{0\}.
\]

Their product is again a unit or zero. Conditional on any full survival
history:

- if the held coordinate is a unit, the refreshed coordinate and residual
  expose a factor with exact probability (\theta_w);
- if the held coordinate is zero, the residual stays zero and the refreshed
  coordinate exposes a factor with exact probability (h_N/N).

Thus the next-step conditional hazard is at most

\[
\rho_w=\max\{\theta_w,h_N/N\}.
\]

This proof survives deterministic scan, random scan, laziness, and any choice
of coordinate made from the past before drawing the next conditional sample.
It does not cover choosing a move after seeing its proposed new value, because
that is a different transition kernel.

Iterating the conditional survival bound gives

\[
\Pr(\tau>t)\ge(1-\rho_w)^t.
\]

The union form and tail sum then give

\[
\Pr(\tau\le t)\le1-(1-\rho_w)^t\le t\rho_w,
\qquad
\mathbb E\tau\ge1/\rho_w
\]

when (\rho_w>0). No independence of successive chain states is used. If
(N) is prime, then (H_N) is empty, (h_N=\theta_w=0), and the stopping
time is infinite. This is consistent with the tail bound. For every composite
(N), (h_N>0).

Monitoring the residual adds no missed event to this proof. At a unit-held
update, coordinate and residual gcd success are equivalent. At a zero-held
update, the residual is zero and cannot give a proper gcd.

## 4. Counts and adversarial families

For (N=pq), with distinct primes (p<q), the proper nonunits are the
nonzero multiples of (p) or (q). The two sets are disjoint after removing
zero, so

\[
h_{pq}=(q-1)+(p-1)=p+q-2.
\]

If (q/p\le C), then (N\le Cp^2) and

\[
\frac{h_N}{N}\le\frac{(1+C)p}{N}
=O_C(N^{-1/2}).
\]

For (N=p^2), the proper nonunits are the (p-1) nonzero multiples of
(p), hence

\[
h_N=p-1,
\qquad h_N/N<N^{-1/2}.
\]

These formulas include (p=2). For example, (H_4=\{2\}). The distinct
semiprime formula also includes (p=2); no odd-prime step occurs in the
proof. Thus no hidden parity restriction is needed.

On either growing balanced family, (h_N/N) is negligible as a function of
the input length (n=\lceil\log_2N\rceil). If (\theta_{w_N}) is also
negligible, then every polynomial number of refreshes has negligible monitored
gcd success, and the expectation lower bound is superpolynomial. This
endpoint implication is correct.

If

\[
\theta_{w_N}=O\!\left(N^{-1/2}\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\right),
\]

the proved statement is only

\[
\mathbb E\tau
=\Omega\!\left(\frac{N^{1/2}}{\operatorname{poly}(\log N)}\right).
\]

Lines 224--225 say that the expected time “is” this scale. No matching upper
bound is proved, and an allowed lazy or one-sided scan can even make the
hitting time infinite. The text must say “is at least” or use (\Omega).

## 5. The claimed dichotomy is not exhaustive

Lines 216--228 first say that one of two cases holds and then say that no
binary exhaustion is intended. Those statements conflict. A family of
success probabilities need not have either a uniform inverse-polynomial lower
bound or be negligible.

For an explicit realizable example, take a growing balanced composite family,
write (n=\lceil\log_2N\rceil), choose one (d_N\in H_N), and define a
normalized weight supported on (0) and (d_N) by

\[
w_N(0)=1-\alpha_N,
\qquad w_N(d_N)=\alpha_N,
\]

where

\[
\alpha_N=
\begin{cases}
1/n,&n\text{ even},\\
2^{-\sqrt n},&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

Then (w_N(0)>0) and (\theta_{w_N}=\alpha_N). This sequence is not
negligible, because of the even subsequence, and it has no uniform
inverse-polynomial lower bound, because of the odd subsequence. The exact
pointwise bound (4.3) still applies.

The correction is simple. Replace “one of the following holds” with “the two
important endpoint regimes are,” state their uniform asymptotic quantifiers,
and leave all other or oscillating regimes to the exact pointwise bound.

## 6. Framework claim and relation to prior results

The exact theorem says that the Gibbs wrapper adds no per-step gcd success
beyond the larger of two sources:

1. factor mass already present in the one-dimensional residual sampler; and
2. the raw proper-nonunit density exposed by a zero-held uniform refresh.

It does **not** say that the chain can never create factor mass absent from
the residual law. For example, let (N=6) and let (w) be supported only at
zero. Then (\theta_w=0). A unit-held refresh goes to zero, but a subsequent
zero-held refresh is uniform and hits

\[
H_6=\{2,3,4\}
\]

with probability (1/2). Thus lines 13, 232--236, and the unqualified title
are false if “amplify” means increase relative to (\theta_w). The displayed
maximum bound and the final line 259--260 already contain the correct
qualification. The prose should say “cannot amplify beyond the residual mass
or raw zero-refresh density.”

The theorem also does not rule out an implementable residual law with useful
factor mass. Such a law would itself be a factoring sampler, and finding one
would be a major positive result. What this proof removes is the hope that a
one-free-coordinate Gibbs wrapper will amplify a residual law whose factor
mass is negligible. Therefore “residual reweighting alone is not the missing
sampler operation” is too broad. The justified statement is that the **local
Gibbs wrapper** is not an additional amplifier.

This boundary is consistent with P43--P45 and P59/X53:

- P43 treats the cold heat bath confined to (kx=0). F52 allows all residual
  fibres but conditions back to the same uniform zero-product target.
- P44 and P45 treat deterministic automorphisms or low-degree bijections of
  the zero-product locus. F52 uses stochastic, noninvertible updates on all of
  (R^2), so those results do not imply it.
- P59 treats one positive scalar weight on all nonzero residuals. F52 genuinely
  extends its local hazard argument to an arbitrary fixed nonnegative
  residual weight, including weights with zeros.

The proof remains restricted to one refreshed free coordinate, a fixed target
whose weight depends only on (kx), and individual coordinate or residual
gcd screens. Joint block moves, joint ((k,x))-dependent weights, interacting
residuals, valuation lifts, nonlocal proposals, and transcript decoders remain
open. The pathwise theorem actually covers every safe warm-start law for the
named gcd screens; a warm start becomes a genuine reopen condition only when
its construction or a different decoder already carries usable factor
information.
