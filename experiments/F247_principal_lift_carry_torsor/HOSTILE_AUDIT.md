## 1. Ordinary lift law

For \(a_t=a+Nt\), every binomial term of degree at least two in \(Nt\)
vanishes modulo \(N^2\). Hence

\[
(a+Nt)^E=a^E+NEa^{E-1}t\pmod {N^2}.
\]

The residue \(x=\langle a^E\rangle_N\) is independent of \(t\). Subtracting
\(x\) and dividing the resulting congruence by \(N\) gives

\[
K_t=K_0+Ea^{E-1}t\pmod N.
\]

The slope is a unit because both \(a\) and \(E\) are units modulo \(N\).
Thus the map is a permutation for every odd composite \(N\), including
non-squarefree \(N\). Squarefreeness is not used here.

The conditional-uniform statement has the correct quantifier. The conditioned
transcript must be invariant under \(t\), and the lift parameter must still be
uniform after that conditioning. It would be false if the conditioning exposed
\(t\), a modulo-\(N^2\) value, or the carry itself. The packet excludes those
cases.

For a negative return, the canonical residue section is \(N-1\), while the
signed section is \(-1\). Therefore

\[
(N-1)+NK_t=-1+N(K_t+1),
\]

so \(C_t=K_t+1\pmod N\). A fixed canonical \(K_t\) and signed \(C_t\) can
have different gcds. The packet does not conflate them.

## 2. Exact probability constants

Let \(N=pq\) for distinct primes. Among the canonical residues:

- \(q-1\) are divisible by \(p\) but not \(q\);
- \(p-1\) are divisible by \(q\) but not \(p\);
- one residue, zero, is divisible by both;
- \((p-1)(q-1)\) are units.

This gives exactly

\[
\Pr(\gcd(K,N)=1)=\frac{\varphi(N)}N,
\quad
\Pr(1<\gcd(K,N)<N)=\frac{p+q-2}{N},
\quad
\Pr(\gcd(K,N)=N)=\frac1N.
\]

For fixed \(L\), translation by \(-L\) is a permutation modulo \(N\). This
proves the same proper-gcd constant for \(K-L\bmod N\). Changing the zero
difference to one changes a gcd of \(N\) into a gcd of \(1\). It does not
change the proper-gcd event.

For a prime \(\ell\), the canonical interval contains
\(\lceil N/\ell\rceil\) multiples of \(\ell\), including zero. After zero
is replaced by one, it contains
\(\lfloor (N-1)/\ell\rfloor\) relevant multiples. Thus (8) and (9) are
exact, and the latter probability is strictly less than \(1/\ell\).

Write \(N=s\ell+r\), with \(0\le r<\ell\). There are \(r\) residue classes
of size \(s+1\) and \(\ell-r\) classes of size \(s\). For independent
uniform \(K,L\), subtraction of the \(N\) exact-equality pairs gives

\[
\Pr(K\ne L,\ K\equiv L\pmod\ell)
=\frac{\ell s(s-1)+2rs}{N^2}.
\]

The claimed upper bound follows because

\[
N^2-\ell\bigl(\ell s(s-1)+2rs\bigr)=\ell^2s+r^2\ge0.
\]

If a history fixes \(L\), exactly \(c_\ell(L)-1\) unequal values of a new
uniform \(K\) collide with it. Since
\(c_\ell(L)-1\le\lfloor N/\ell\rfloor\), the conditional probability is at
most \(1/\ell\). This is a conditional count. It does not assume that the
adaptive sequence is jointly independent.

Equations (4)--(7) are stated only for a distinct-prime semiprime. They are
not formulas for prime powers or general composites. The packet preserves
that boundary.

## 3. Signed carry and order modulo \(r^2\)

Here the distinct-prime assumption is essential. If \(N=rq\), then \(q\)
is a unit modulo \(r\), so

\[
r\mid C
\iff r^2\mid NC
\iff a^E=\sigma\pmod {r^2}.
\]

Let \(m=\operatorname{ord}_r(a)\) and
\(M=\operatorname{ord}_{r^2}(a)\). The reduction kernel has order \(r\).
Therefore \(M=m\) or \(M=rm\).

If \(M=m\), reduction is injective on the cyclic group generated by \(a\).
A positive return lifts to \(+1\). For a negative return, \(m\) is even and
\(E=m/2\pmod m\). The only element of order two modulo an odd prime square
is \(-1\), so the negative return also lifts.

If \(M=rm\), a lifted positive return forces \(rm\mid E\). A lifted
negative return forces \(E=rm/2\pmod{rm}\). In both cases \(r\mid E\).
The hypothesis \(r\nmid E\) excludes both cases. Hence

\[
r\mid C\iff M=m.
\]

The hostile edge cases confirm the stated scope:

- If \(r^2\mid N\), then \(NC=0\pmod {r^2}\) for every \(C\). The first
  equivalence can fail. The packet assumes \(N=pq\) with distinct primes.
- If \(r\mid E\), an order-\(rm\) lift can return to the sign. The packet
  excludes this case.
- At the prime \(2\), the two signs coincide modulo \(2\) and the negative
  argument is not the stated one. The packet assumes odd primes.

## 4. Canonical order-two witness

For odd \(N\) and even \(E\),

\[
(N-1)^E=(-1+N)^E=1-EN\pmod {N^2}.
\]

Thus \(K_0=-E\pmod N\). If \(E=(N-1)W\), then \(K_0=W\pmod N\). With
\(W=1\), the carry is exactly the useless unit \(1\).

Modulo every divisor of \(N\), the base is \(-1\). Every signed screen is
therefore global: one sign gives gcd \(N\), and the other gives gcd \(1\)
because \(N\) is odd. This calculation also survives repeated prime factors.
It is one null witness. It is not a claim that all canonical carries are
null or random.

## 5. Exact norm-one torus fibre

Let \(V\) be any exact norm-one lift of the same residue as \(U\). Since
\(U\) is a unit, uniquely

\[
VU^{-1}=1+N(c+dw),\qquad c,d\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z.
\]

The first-order norm is

\[
\operatorname{Nm}(1+N(c+dw))=1+2Nc\pmod {N^2}.
\]

Exact norm one therefore gives \(2c=0\pmod N\). Oddness of \(N\) gives
\(c=0\). Conversely, every \(1+Ntw\) has norm one modulo \(N^2\). Hence

\[
U_t=U(1+Ntw)
\]

is the complete fibre, with a unique \(t\pmod N\). This argument needs
neither squarefreeness nor a field quadratic algebra. It does need odd \(N\).
For even \(N\), a nonzero scalar tangent can satisfy \(2c=0\), so the stated
one-dimensional description need not hold.

For the normalized carry,

\[
U_t^EX^{-1}
=(U^EX^{-1})(1+NEtw)
=1+N(C_0+Etw)\pmod {N^2}.
\]

Thus \(C_t=C_0+Etw\). If
\(\operatorname{Nm}(X)=1+Nh\), taking norms gives

\[
h+\operatorname{Tr}(C_t)=0\pmod N,
\qquad 2c_{0,t}=-h\pmod N.
\]

Only \(c_{1,t}=c_{1,0}+Et\) is free. It is uniform because \(E\) is a
unit. The probability laws apply to this scalar free coordinate. They do
not apply to the fixed trace coordinate or to an arbitrary raw coordinate
of \(U_t^E-X\). The packet states the normalized-coordinate restriction.

Without the norm-one condition, the ratio of two lifts is
\(1+N(s+tw)\), and the carry changes by \(E(s+tw)\). Uniform independent
\(s,t\) make both coordinates uniform. This is also exact.

## 6. Same-chain laws

For the ordinary source, expansion of
\((x+NK_E)^m\) and the identity \(x^m=x_m+NQ_m\) give

\[
K_{mE}=Q_m+mx^{m-1}K_E\pmod N.
\]

No unit claim is made for the new slope. The identity proves dependence,
not fresh uniformity.

For the torus source, commutativity gives

\[
U^{mE}=X_E^m(1+NmC_E).
\]

Using \(X_E^m=X_{mE}(1+NQ_m)\) gives

\[
C_{mE}=Q_m+mC_E.
\]

Both formulas are section-aware affine identities. Neither licenses an
independence assumption along a square chain.

## 7. Adaptive bank and exact scope

Before stage \(i\), condition on the full allowed past. The next base and
exponent can be functions of that past. The new lift parameter must then be
conditionally uniform, and the exponent must be a unit modulo \(N\). The
affine law makes the new ordinary carry, or the free torus coordinate,
conditionally uniform.

There are \(T\) nonzero-replaced raw atoms and at most
\(\binom T2\) nonzero-replaced pair differences. For a fixed exterior prime
\(\ell\), (9) bounds each raw atom and (11) bounds each integer pair
difference after the older value is fixed. A union bound followed by
averaging over the past gives

\[
\Pr(\ell\mid W_{\rm lift})
\le \frac{T+\binom T2}{\ell}.
\]

No joint independence is used. If pair differences are instead represented
modulo \(N\), conditional translation makes each such residue uniform and
(9) gives the same upper bound.

For quasipolynomial \(T\), both \(T\) and \(T^2\) are \(2^{o(n)}\). Thus an
exterior marker of size \(2^{\Omega(n)}\) is hit with probability
\(2^{-\Omega(n)}\). On a balanced distinct-prime semiprime, (5) and (7)
give the same exponential scale for direct gcd exits. The four-marker union
changes only a constant factor.

The bound covers only positive products and positive powers of the fresh
uniform scalar coordinates and their pair differences. It does not cover
the fixed torus trace coordinate, canonical intercepts, raw torus
coordinates, same-gauge nonlinear maps, cross-base identities, sums,
determinants, Euclidean quotients, retained exact relations, or decoders
that use more than product-word prime support. It also does not claim a
factoring algorithm or a top-level factoring lower bound.

## Final hostile assessment

The assumptions that carry the proof are visible and necessary:
unit base, unit exponent, odd modulus, fresh conditional lift randomness,
normalization in the torus case, and a distinct-prime semiprime for the
semiprime counts and signed local-order statement. The authenticated packet
uses each assumption only within its valid scope. In particular, it never
transfers random-fibre uniformity to the canonical section.
