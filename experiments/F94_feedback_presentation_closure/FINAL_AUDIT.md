### Multiplicity map and dimension formula

The replacement map records the full prime multiplicity:

    Delta_alpha e_j = sum over r in S_j of m_jr e_r,
    m_jr = v_b_r(q_j) mod ell.

Thus E' = Delta_alpha E and

    ker E' = E inverse of ker Delta_alpha.

The restriction of E maps ker E' onto im E intersect ker Delta_alpha, with
kernel ker E. Rank-nullity therefore gives

    dim ker E' = dim ker E
                 + dim(im E intersect ker Delta_alpha).

Because the supports are disjoint, the nonzero columns of Delta_alpha are
independent. Its kernel is exactly the span of coordinates j for which every
descendant multiplicity is zero modulo ell. This handles powers such as
q_j = 4 = 2 squared; no multiplicity-free assumption remains.

### One-column closure

For an appended column u, the new kernel equation is

    E' x + a u = 0.

If u is outside im E', then a = 0 and the kernel is ker E' times zero. If u is
inside im E', one chosen preimage splits the new kernel as a direct sum with
one additional scalar parameter. The stated nullities and the references to
equations (9) and (10) are correct.

These are exactly the two algebraic gates. Passing a gate supplies a known
dependency; it does not by itself guarantee a useful factor.

### Fresh rows and private rows

At a fresh prime, all old columns have zero exponent and the new column has
exponent one. Its vector is therefore nonzero outside the old row space, so it
cannot close.

For several insertions, the private-row corollary is correctly scoped to one
fixed final presentation. Each private row is zero on M_0, nonzero on its own
column modulo ell, and zero on earlier inserted columns. Taking the largest
nonzero inserted coordinate proves that all inserted coefficients vanish.
The displayed kernel is consequently exact. A later presentation refinement
can invalidate the premise, and the text says to reapply the main theorem.

complete old decoder failure on the explicit known root image, and numerically

The P99 statement is conditional. Under its premises,

### Bit complexity and search basis

The polynomial bound is measured in the explicit presentation dimensions,
coefficient bit sizes, scanned-prime encodings, and log N. A bound solely in
log N is claimed only when the presentation and scan caps are themselves
polynomial.

The implementation rule processes a kernel basis and the explicit support-two
menu. It does not enumerate every vector or projective direction in a kernel.
The earlier phrase about every new direction is governed by this explicit
basis rule, so it does not introduce an exponential search.

the second [1] closes, and deleting the first column destroys that known
dependency. The example claims no factorization from the dependency.
