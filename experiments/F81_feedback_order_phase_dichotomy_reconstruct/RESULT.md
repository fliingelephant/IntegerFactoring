## 1. Existence of a positive separator

The order of \(u\) modulo \(N=pq\) is

\[
r=\operatorname{lcm}(r_p,r_q),
\]

so the elements of \(\langle u\rangle\) are uniquely \(u^t\) with
\(0\le t<r\). For every integer \(t\),

\[
u^t\equiv1\pmod p\iff r_p\mid t,
\qquad
u^t\equiv1\pmod q\iff r_q\mid t.
\]

If \(r_p=r_q\), the two conditions always agree, so no power is a positive
separator.

Conversely, suppose \(r_p\ne r_q\). If \(r_q\nmid r_p\), then
\(u^{r_p}\) is \(1\) modulo \(p\) but not modulo \(q\). If instead
\(r_q\mid r_p\), the divisibility is proper and \(u^{r_q}\) is \(1\)
modulo \(q\) but not modulo \(p\). Thus a positive separator exists exactly
when \(r_p\ne r_q\).

## 2. Exact positive-separator count

Among the \(r\) exponent classes modulo \(r\), exactly \(r/r_p\) are
multiples of \(r_p\). These give all powers equal to \(1\) modulo \(p\).
After the identity exponent is removed, they contribute \(r/r_p-1\)
positive separators. The \(q\)-side similarly contributes \(r/r_q-1\).

The two nonidentity sets are disjoint: an exponent divisible by both local
orders is divisible by their least common multiple \(r\), and is therefore
the identity class. The exact count is consequently

\[
\frac r{r_p}+\frac r{r_q}-2.
\]

Division by \(|\langle u\rangle|=r\) gives density

\[
\frac1{r_p}+\frac1{r_q}-\frac2r.
\]

## 3. The exact positive-sign gate

For every \(E\ge1\),

\[
p\mid u^E-1\iff r_p\mid E,
\qquad
q\mid u^E-1\iff r_q\mid E.
\]

Since \(N=pq\) is squarefree, the gcd with \(N\) is proper and nontrivial
exactly when precisely one of these divisibilities holds. Hence

\[
1<\gcd(u^E-1,N)<N
\iff
(r_p\mid E)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(r_q\mid E).
\]

## 4. Equal orders synchronize both signs

Assume \(r_p=r_q=s\). Positive-sign status is synchronized by the preceding
divisibility test.

For the negative sign, first suppose \(s\) is odd. The cyclic subgroups
generated by the two local images have odd order, so neither contains the
field element \(-1\), which has order \(2\).

Now suppose \(s\) is even. In a cyclic group generated by an element of
order \(s\), its unique element of order \(2\) is the power with exponent
congruent to \(s/2\pmod s\). In each prime field the unique element of order
\(2\) is \(-1\). Therefore

\[
u^E\equiv-1\pmod p
\iff E\equiv s/2\pmod s
\iff u^E\equiv-1\pmod q.
\]

Thus the two local negative-sign conditions also always agree. For every
pure power, each sign gcd is either \(1\) or \(N\), never a proper factor.

## 5. Exact completeness of the punctured-lcm bank

For \(a>1\), write

\[
\sigma(a)=\max_{\ell\mid a,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}\ell^{v_\ell(a)},
\]

and retain the stated convention \(\sigma(1)=1\). This records the largest
complete prime-power component of \(a\).

Let

\[
s_\ell=v_\ell(M_B)=\lfloor\log_\ell B\rfloor
\]

for every prime \(\ell\le B\). Then

\[
a\mid M_B
\iff
\ell^{v_\ell(a)}\le B\text{ for every }\ell\mid a
\iff
\sigma(a)\le B. \tag{1}
\]

This equivalence is the reason the theorem needs the full prime-power
condition.

Suppose first that some \(E\in\mathcal E_B\) gives a proper gcd. Every bank
exponent divides \(M_B\). By the gate in Claim 3, exactly one of
\(r_p,r_q\) divides \(E\). That order therefore divides \(M_B\), so (1)
gives

\[
\min\{\sigma(r_p),\sigma(r_q)\}\le B.
\]

The xor also makes \(r_p=r_q\) impossible. This proves necessity.

For sufficiency, assume

\[
r_p\ne r_q
\quad\text{and}\quad
\min\{\sigma(r_p),\sigma(r_q)\}\le B.
\]

By (1), at least one local order divides \(M_B\).

If exactly one local order divides \(M_B\), choose \(E=M_B\). The exact gate
immediately gives a proper factor.

It remains to consider the case in which both local orders divide \(M_B\).
Since they are unequal, there is a prime \(\ell\le B\) with

\[
\alpha=v_\ell(r_p)\ne v_\ell(r_q)=\beta.
\]

Exchange \(p,q\) if necessary so that \(\alpha<\beta\). Put
\(s=s_\ell=v_\ell(M_B)\). Since both orders divide \(M_B\),
\(\beta\le s\). Choose

\[
j=s-\alpha.
\]

Then \(1\le j\le s=\lfloor\log_\ell B\rfloor\), so

\[
E=\frac{M_B}{\ell^j}
\]

belongs to \(\mathcal E_B\). Its \(\ell\)-adic valuation is exactly
\(\alpha\). All its other prime valuations agree with those of \(M_B\).
Therefore \(r_p\mid E\), while \(r_q\nmid E\). The gate again gives a
proper factor.

This proves the exact equivalence

\[
\exists E\in\mathcal E_B:\ 1<\gcd(u^E-1,N)<N
\]

if and only if

\[
r_p\ne r_q
\quad\text{and}\quad
\min\{\sigma(r_p),\sigma(r_q)\}\le B.
\]

### Bank size and deterministic cost

The pairs \((\ell,j)\) used in the bank correspond injectively to the
distinct prime powers

\[
\ell^j\in\{2,\ldots,B\}.
\]

For \(B\ge2\), there are at most \(B-1\) such pairs. Thus

\[
|\mathcal E_B|
\le 1+\sum_{\ell\le B\ {\rm prime}}\lfloor\log_\ell B\rfloor
\le B,
\]

which is slightly stronger than the requested \(B+1\) bound. The case
\(B=1\) has the singleton bank \(\{1\}\) and also satisfies the requested
bound.

Also,

\[
M_B\le B!,
\qquad
\log_2M_B\le B\log_2B.
\]

When \(B=\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\), every exponent has polynomial bit
length. One can construct \(M_B\) by iterative gcd-and-lcm operations,
enumerate the at most \(B-1\) prime powers, and form every punctured exponent
by exact division. The full bank description has polynomial total bit
length. Repeated squaring followed by a gcd tests every exponent in
deterministic polynomial total time.

### Why ordinary smoothness is insufficient

Standard \(B\)-smoothness says only that every prime divisor of \(a\) is at
most \(B\). The condition \(\sigma(a)\le B\) says more: every complete
primary component \(\ell^{v_\ell(a)}\) must itself be at most \(B\).

For example, \(16\) is \(9\)-smooth because its only prime divisor is \(2\),
but

\[
\sigma(16)=16>9,
\]

and \(16\nmid M_9\). Thus standard \(B\)-smoothness cannot replace the
\(\sigma\)-condition in the bank theorem.

## 6. The fixed order data for \(N=4033\)

First,

\[
37\cdot109=4033.
\]

Both factors are prime: \(37\) has no prime divisor at most
\(\sqrt{37}<7\), and \(109\) has none among \(2,3,5,7\), the primes at most
\(\sqrt{109}<11\).

Modulo \(37\),

\[
5^4=625\equiv-4,
\qquad
5^8\equiv16,
\qquad
5^9\equiv6,
\qquad
5^{18}\equiv-1.
\]

Thus the order divides \(36\) but not \(18\). Also,

\[
5^{12}\equiv5^8 5^4\equiv16(-4)\equiv10\not\equiv1\pmod{37}.
\]

The only divisors of \(36\) that do not divide \(18\) are \(4,12,36\).
The last calculation excludes \(4\) and \(12\), so

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{37}(5)=36.
\]

Modulo \(109\),

\[
5^3=125\equiv16,
\qquad
5^9\equiv16^3\equiv63.
\]

Furthermore,

\[
63^2\equiv45,
\qquad
63^3\equiv45\cdot63\equiv1\pmod{109}.
\]

Hence \(5^{27}\equiv1\), while \(5^9\not\equiv1\). Since all proper
divisors of \(27\) divide \(9\),

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{109}(5)=27.
\]

The complete primary components are

\[
36=2^2 3^2,
\qquad
27=3^3,
\]

so

\[
\sigma(36)=\max(4,9)=9,
\qquad
\sigma(27)=27.
\]

Finally,

\[
M_9=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,9)
=2^3 3^2 5\cdot7
=2520.
\]

The order \(36\) divides \(2520\), while \(27\) does not because
\(v_3(2520)=2<3\). Thus \(M_9\) separates the two local orders. Equivalently,

\[
5^{2520}\equiv1\pmod{37},
\qquad
5^{2520}\equiv5^9\equiv63\not\equiv1\pmod{109}.
\]

## 7. The phase-only example

Use additive notation. Every nonzero element of the additive group
\(\mathbb F_L\) has order \(L\). Since \(a,b\ne0\), both coordinates of
\(z=(a,b)\) have order \(L\).

For a scalar \(t\in\mathbb F_L\), if \(t=0\), then \(tz=(0,0)\). If
\(t\ne0\), then both \(ta\) and \(tb\) are nonzero. Thus no scalar multiple
of \(z\) has exactly one zero coordinate, so no pure power in the
multiplicative analogy is a separator.

The vectors \(g=(1,1)\) and \(z=(a,b)\) have determinant

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\1&b\end{pmatrix}=b-a\ne0.
\]

They form a basis, and therefore

\[
\langle H,z\rangle
=\operatorname{span}\{g,z\}
=\mathbb F_L^2.
\]

Moreover,

\[
z-ag=(a,b)-(a,a)=(0,b-a),
\]

whose second coordinate is nonzero. It is a separator. The example isolates
a phase effect: equal local orders prevent pure-power separation, while an
additional diagonal element can cancel exactly one coordinate.

## 8. Scope

The punctured-lcm theorem is an exact conditional characterization. It does
not say that either hidden local order satisfies the \(\sigma\)-bound on
every input, and ordinary \(B\)-smoothness is not enough. The bank is public
and executable without knowing the orders, but the theorem gives no
all-input success guarantee or density estimate for suitable units or
feedback descendants.

The final vector-space construction is only an abstract
order-versus-phase example. It is not asserted to arise from canonical
integer inverses, integer block refinement, or any particular feedback
source law. Nothing here is a computational lower bound, a hard family, or
a general factoring algorithm.
