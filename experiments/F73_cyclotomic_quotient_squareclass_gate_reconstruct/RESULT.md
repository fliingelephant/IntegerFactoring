# Verdict: PASS

All claims in the narrow statement follow from the stated hypotheses. The
subgroup gate and the square-class gate are separate: the former always opens,
whereas the latter opens exactly when \(b=(c+1)/3\) is a square.

## 1. Order-three source

First,

\[
N-(c+2)(c-1)=3,
\]

so every common divisor of \(N\) and \(c-1\) divides \(3\). Since \(3\nmid N\),

\[
\gcd(N,c-1)=1.
\]

Let \(q^e\parallel N\). The identity

\[
c^3=1+(c-1)N
\]

shows that the order of \(c\) modulo \(q^e\) divides \(3\). It cannot be \(1\),
because that would give \(q\mid c-1\), contrary to the displayed gcd. Hence
the order is exactly \(3\) in every prime-power component. Its order modulo
\(N\) is therefore also \(3\), and

\[
H_0=\langle c\rangle=\{1,c,c^2\}\pmod N.
\]

These three representatives are distinct because \(1<c<c^2<N\).

The odd prime \(c\) is not \(3\), since \(c=3\) would give the prime \(N=13\).
If \(c\equiv1\pmod3\), then \(3\mid N\). Thus

\[
c\equiv2\pmod3,
\qquad
b=\frac{c+1}{3}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]

In fact \(c\equiv5\pmod6\), so \(b\) is even.

## 2. Promoted quotient and its inverse

The quotient of the old relation is

\[
g=\frac{A_0-1}{N}=c-1.
\]

Because \(c\equiv2\pmod3\), the integer

\[
w=\frac{c^2-1}{3}=(c-1)\frac{c+1}{3}=gb
\]

is well-defined. Direct expansion gives

\[
gw=\frac{(c-1)(c^2-1)}3
   =1+\frac{c-2}{3}N.
\]

Thus \(gw\equiv1\pmod N\). Also \(w>0\), and

\[
3(N-w)=2c^2+3c+4>0.
\]

Hence \(0<w<N\), so \(w\) is the canonical least positive inverse of \(g\),
and

\[
A_1=gw=g^2b=1+\frac{c-2}{3}N.
\]

All of \(g,w,c,c^2\) are units modulo \(N\). Finally, the representatives
\(1,c,c^2,g\) all lie in \([1,N-1]\), and \(g=c-1\) differs from each of
\(1,c,c^2\). Therefore

\[
g\notin H_0.
\]

## 3. Complete gcd refinement and subgroup expansion

The old endpoint pair is \((c,c^2)\), and the new pair is \((g,w)\). The old
generator cannot be mixed with a new endpoint because

\[
\gcd(c,g)=1,
\qquad
\gcd(c,w)=1.
\]

For the second equality, \(c\mid w\) would imply
\(c\mid 3w=c^2-1\), which is impossible. Thus refinement of the old pair
leaves the generator \(c\), with \(c^2\) represented as its square.

The new refinement can also be given explicitly. Put

\[
t=\frac b2,
\qquad
h=\frac g2=3t-1.
\]

Then

\[
\gcd(g,w)=g,
\qquad
\gcd(g,b)=\gcd(3b-2,b)=2,
\qquad
\gcd(t,h)=1.
\]

Write \(t=2^\alpha u\) and \(h=2^\beta v\), where \(u,v\) are odd. Since
\(h=3t-1\), exactly one of \(\alpha,\beta\) is zero and
\(\gcd(u,v)=1\). Up to omission of factors equal to \(1\), a complete
pairwise-coprime gcd basis is

\[
c,\quad 2,\quad u,\quad v,
\]

with endpoint representations

\[
c^2=c^2,
\qquad
g=2^{\beta+1}v,
\qquad
w=2^{\alpha+\beta+2}uv.
\]

Further splitting of \(u\) or \(v\) does not change the argument. Every
refined factor is a unit modulo \(N\), because it divides a unit endpoint.
Most importantly, the refined factors assigned to \(g\), with multiplicity,
have product exactly \(g\).

Consequently the subgroup \(K\) generated by all refined endpoint factors
contains both \(c\) and \(g\). Hence

\[
H_0=\langle c\rangle\subsetneq K,
\]

because \(g\notin H_0\). No square-class property of \(b\) was used.

## 4. Exact two-column square-class gate

In \(\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}/(\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times})^2\),

\[
[A_0]=[c^3]=[c],
\qquad
[A_1]=[g^2b]=[b].
\]

Moreover,

\[
\gcd(c,b)=1,
\]

because a common divisor would divide both \(c\) and \(3b-c=1\). The class
\([c]\) is nontrivial because \(c\) is prime. Equality \([b]=[c]\) would make
\(bc\) a rational square, but the \(c\)-adic valuation of \(bc\) is \(1\).
Thus

\[
[b]\ne[c].
\]

A parity relation that uses the new column has the form

\[
[b][c]^\varepsilon=1,
\qquad \varepsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]

For \(\varepsilon=1\), this would say \([b]=[c]\), which is impossible. For
\(\varepsilon=0\), it says \([b]=1\). A positive integer has trivial rational
square class exactly when it is an integer square. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{the new column closes immediately}
\iff b\text{ is an integer square}.}
\]

### Complete \(2\)-saturation

Let

\[
\Gamma=\langle A_0,A_1\rangle\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times},
\]

and define its complete \(2\)-saturation by allowing all iterated square
roots that remain rational.

If \(b\) is not a square, then \([c]\) and \([b]\) are two distinct nontrivial
elements of an elementary abelian \(2\)-group, so they are linearly
independent. Suppose

\[
x^{2^k}=A_0^mA_1^n
\]

for \(k>0\) and \(m,n\in\mathbb Z\). The right side is a square, so square
classes force \(m,n\) to be even. Positivity and uniqueness of the positive
square root then give

\[
x^{2^{k-1}}=A_0^{m/2}A_1^{n/2}.
\]

Iteration shows that \(m,n\) are divisible by \(2^k\) and \(x\in\Gamma\).
Thus \(\Gamma\) is already completely \(2\)-saturated in the nonsquare branch;
there is no missed relation.

Now suppose \(b=s^2\). Then

\[
A_1=g^2s^2=R^2,
\qquad
R=s(c-1),
\]

and the exact parity kernel above is generated by the new column alone. Thus
the first new positive root is \(R\).

This root is genuinely new, and saturation stops there. To see this, first
note that \(s\) is even; write \(s=2t\). Then

\[
R=4t(6t^2-1),
\qquad
\gcd(t,6t^2-1)=1.
\]

If \(R\) were a square, then \(t(6t^2-1)\) would be a square. Its coprime
factors would each be squares, but

\[
6t^2-1\equiv2\pmod3
\]

cannot be a square. Hence \([R]\ne1\). Also \(c=3s^2-1>s\), so
\(\gcd(c,R)=1\), and the same odd-valuation argument gives
\([R]\ne[c]\). Therefore \([A_0]=[c]\) and \([R]\) are independent.

It follows by the preceding divisibility argument that
\(\langle A_0,R\rangle\) is completely \(2\)-saturated. Hence, exactly,

\[
\operatorname{Sat}_2(\Gamma)=\langle A_0,R\rangle.
\]

There is one new root generator, \(R\), and no further \(2\)-division changes
the immediate gate.

## 5. Root gate and factor extraction

From \(b=s^2\),

\[
c=3s^2-1.
\]

Since \(c\) is odd, \(3s^2=c+1\) is even, so \(s\) is even. Define

\[
A=3s^2-3s+1,
\qquad
B=3s^2+3s+1.
\]

Then

\[
AB=(3s^2+1)^2-(3s)^2
  =9s^4-3s^2+1
  =c^2+c+1=N.
\]

If \(d\mid A\) and \(d\mid B\), then \(d\mid6s\). Since
\(A\equiv1\pmod s\), one has \(\gcd(d,s)=1\), so \(d\mid6\). Both \(A,B\)
are odd and both are \(1\pmod3\). Therefore

\[
\gcd(A,B)=1.
\]

Using \(R=s(3s^2-2)\), direct multiplication gives

\[
R-1=(s-1)B,
\qquad
R+1=(s+1)A.
\]

Also

\[
A\equiv1\pmod{s-1},
\qquad
B\equiv1\pmod{s+1}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\gcd(R-1,N)
 &=B\gcd(s-1,A)=B,\\
\gcd(R+1,N)
 &=A\gcd(s+1,B)=A.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(s\ge2\), so \(A,B>1\); these are proper coprime factors of \(N\).

For \(c=11\),

\[
b=4,
\quad s=2,
\quad N=133,
\quad R=20,
\quad A=7,
\quad B=19.
\]

Indeed \(\gcd(19,133)=19\) and \(\gcd(21,133)=7\).

## 6. Classification and scope

The narrow framework statement is proved: quotient promotion from this
order-three source always passes the subgroup-expansion gate, and its first
new column passes the immediate complete-square-decoder gate exactly when
its square class is trivial, equivalently when \(b\) is a square.

The proof covers only this seed and its first promoted quotient. It proves no
frequency or infinitude result for primes \(c=3s^2-1\). It also proves no
claim about a general quotient sampler, all inputs, novelty, or an
unrestricted factoring algorithm.
