## 1. Prime-power facts and the input certificate

For an odd prime power (R=p^\alpha), the unit group
(U_R=(\mathbf Z/R\mathbf Z)^\times) is cyclic. Its reduction kernel in
(U_R\to U_p) is a (p)-group. Consequently:

1. (U_R) has a unique subgroup of every order dividing (\varphi(R)).
2. Reduction modulo (p) preserves the order of every element whose order
   is coprime to (p).

The valid incoming state has (M<N). Hence `gcd(M,N)` cannot be (N).
It is either a proper factor, which is returned, or it is one. On the latter
branch, every prime divisor of (M) is different from every (p_j).

Let (r_j=\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(g)). Equation (2) gives (r_j\mid M).
If (r_j<M), choose a prime (\ell\mid M/r_j). Then
(r_j\mid M/\ell), so (g^{M/\ell}=1\pmod {R_j}). This makes (p_j)
divide the gcd in (3), contrary to that gcd being one. Thus

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(g)=M
\]

for every component. In particular, (H_j=\langle g\rangle) is the unique
subgroup of (U_{R_j}) of order (M).

## 2. The first-nonone scan

For every positive integer (r),

\[
d^{rM}=1\pmod {R_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^r\in H_j.
\tag{A}
\]

The reverse implication is immediate. For the forward implication,
(d^r) has order dividing (M), so cyclicity and uniqueness put it in the
unique order-(M) subgroup (H_j). Therefore (e_j(d)\mid r) exactly when
the left side of (A) holds.

A nontrivial (D_r) that is smaller than (N) is already a proper factor;
this includes partial powers of a hidden prime. If the first nonone result
is (D_e=N), then (A) gives (e_j(d)\mid e) for every (j). If some
(e_j(d)<e), the earlier value (D_{e_j(d)}) would be divisible by (p_j)
and would not be one. Hence

\[
e_j(d)=e\qquad\text{for all }j.
\]

If instead every (D_r) for (1\le r\le B) is one, (A) shows that no local
relative order can be at most (B). Thus every (e_j(d)>B). This proves
the exact scan trichotomy.

On a common return, (e) divides

\[
|U_{R_j}/H_j|=\frac{\varphi(p_j^{\alpha_j})}{M}
\]

for every (j). Since (p_j\nmid M), the right side has (p_j)-adic
valuation (\alpha_j-1). Thus (p_j^{\alpha_j}\nmid e), so (N\nmid e).
It follows that `gcd(e,N)` is either a proper factor or one; it cannot equal
(N). On its surviving branch, (\gcd(e,N)=1).

## 3. Factor-first Pohlig--Hellman alignment

Put (x=d^e). A common scan return gives (x^M=1\pmod N). In each
component, the order of (x) divides (M), so the uniqueness above gives
a unique local logarithm

\[
x=g^{a_j}\pmod {R_j},\qquad a_j\pmod M.
\]

Fix (\ell^c\Vert M), and use the definitions in the statement. Locally,
(G) has order (\ell^c), (X=G^{a_j}), and (H) has order (\ell).
Assume the lower (j) digits have survived and have the common value
(A_j). If (b_{j,R}) is the next local digit in component (R), then

\[
(XG^{-A_j})^{\ell^{c-1-j}}=H^{b_{j,R}}\pmod R.
\tag{B}
\]

Because (\ell\nmid p), reduction modulo (p) preserves the order (\ell)
of (H). Hence, for (0\le b<\ell), the difference in (17) is zero
modulo all of (R) exactly in the components whose next digit is (b),
and it is a unit modulo (p) in every other component.

If the local digits differ, choosing any digit that occurs makes the gcd in
(17) the product of a nonempty proper subset of the (R_j), hence a proper
factor. If all digits agree, exactly one test is (N), and every other test
is one. Induction over the digits aligns the logarithms modulo (\ell^c),
or returns a factor. Integer CRT over the distinct prime powers dividing
(M) then gives one (a\pmod M). It is unique because (g) has order
(M). This proves the subroutine's exact interface, including the case
(M=1), where the empty CRT returns (a=0).

There are at most (c\ell) gcd tests for (\ell^c). The same bound can
also cover modular exponentiations without hiding setup work. Compute (G)
and (X) once; precompute (G^{\ell^j}) by (c-1) successive
(\ell)-th powers; maintain (XG^{-A_j}); and exponentiate it only for
the first (c-1) digits. This uses at most (2+(c-1)+(c-1)=2c\le c\ell)
modular exponentiations. Powers by a digit and all (H^b) can be tabled by
modular multiplication. Finally,

\[
\sum_i c_i\ell_i
\le B\sum_i c_i
\le B\sum_i c_i\log_2\ell_i
=B\log_2M.
\]

## 4. The aligned presentation and cyclic growth

Assume (e\ge2) and alignment has returned (d^e=g^a\pmod N). In each
component, (K_j=\langle g,d\rangle) contains (H_j), and its quotient by
(H_j) is generated by (dH_j) with exact order (e). Thus

\[
|K_j|=Me.
\tag{C}
\]

Consider the common presentation

\[
P=\mathbf Z^2/\langle(M,0),(-a,e)\rangle.
\tag{D}
\]

It has order (Me), and the word ((r,t)\mapsto g^r d^t) maps it
onto every (K_j). Equation (C) makes every such map an isomorphism.
Since (K_j) is a subgroup of the cyclic group (U_{R_j}), it is cyclic.
The first Smith invariant of (D) is

\[
\gcd(M,a,e),
\]

so cyclicity forces (\gcd(M,a,e)=1).

Globally, (\langle g\rangle) has order (M). The coset of (d) has order
exactly (e): its (e)-th power is aligned in (\langle g\rangle), while
membership at a smaller positive exponent would contradict the exact local
relative order. Hence the global group (K) also has order (Me) and has
the same presentation (D). It is therefore cyclic of exact order
(L=Me), and the local groups have that same exact order. Since (e\ge2),
this group is strictly larger than the old diagonal subgroup.

If (e=1), the same alignment calculation instead gives (d=g^a\pmod N).
This is exactly the old diagonal-subgroup outcome.

## 5. The deterministic Smith word and its certificate

Let (E_0) and (s) be as in (19). The CRT is valid because
(\gcd(M,E_0)=1). For every prime (q\mid e):

- if (q\nmid M), then (s=1\pmod q);
- if (q\mid M), then (\gcd(M,a,e)=1) gives (q\nmid a), and
  (s=a\pmod q).

Thus (\gcd(s,e)=1), so extended Euclid supplies (ue+vs=1). Replacing
(a) by (s) does not change the presentation because (s-a) is a
multiple of (M). In the relation lattice generated by
((M,0)) and ((-s,e)), put (w=(u,v)). Then

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}-s&u\\e&v\end{pmatrix}
=-sv-eu=-1.
\]

Therefore the relation vector ((-s,e)) together with (w) spans
(\mathbf Z^2). The class of (w) generates the quotient (D). Hence

\[
h=g^u d^v
\]

has exact order (L) globally and in every component. Negative powers are
computable because (g) and (d) are public units.

The two gcd screens also give a valid public next-state certificate. The
two earlier surviving screens imply (\gcd(L,N)=1). For every prime
(\ell\mid L), the local element (h^{L/\ell}) has exact order (\ell).
Because (\ell\ne p_j), reduction modulo (p_j) preserves that order.
Thus it is not one even modulo (p_j), and

\[
\gcd(h^{L/\ell}-1,N)=1.
\]

Together with (h^L=1\pmod N), these are precisely the common-order
certificate. Trial division of (e\le B), combined with the supplied
factorization of (M), gives the complete factorization of (L). Its
prime factors are all at most (B).

## 6. Optional sampler

Both (g) and (d) have order dividing (L), and the map

\[
(\mathbf Z/L\mathbf Z)^2\longrightarrow K,
\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto g^u d^v
\]

is a surjective homomorphism. Equal fibers show that uniform independent
(u,v) produce a uniform element of (K). In the cyclic group of order
(L), exactly (\varphi(L)) elements generate. The order screens accept
exactly those generators. A proper screen gcd is a valid factor; an (N)
screen proves failure of that order test and causes rejection. Thus a
no-factor trial succeeds with probability (\varphi(L)/L).

If the distinct prime divisors of (L) are
(q_1<\cdots<q_r), then (q_i\ge i+1), and

\[
\frac L{\varphi(L)}
=\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{q_i}{q_i-1}
\le\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{i+1}{i}
=r+1
=\omega(L)+1
\le\log_2L+1.
\]

The geometric sampler therefore terminates almost surely and has the
claimed expected-trial bound. The deterministic word is stronger because
it avoids sampling.

\[
B\le 2^{C(\log_2(n+1))^k},

\[
M_t=M_0\prod_{i=1}^t e_i\ge 2^tM_0.

(\varphi(R_j)). Therefore

\[
2^tM_0\le M_t\le\gcd_j\varphi(R_j)<N.

(e_i) retains a known (B)-smooth factorization. Fewer than (n)
