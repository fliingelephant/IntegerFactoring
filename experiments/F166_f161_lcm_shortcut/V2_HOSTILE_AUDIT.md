## 1. Arbitrary odd prime-power CRT scope

The proof does not silently replace the CRT components by fields. For an odd
prime power

\[
R_j=p_j^{\alpha_j},
\]

the unit group is cyclic. This is exactly the local fact used for the lcm
identity and local subgroup equality.

The reduction step in the final order certificate is also valid for prime
powers. Its kernel is a `p_j`-group. On the no-factor branch,
`gcd(L,N)=1`, so every prime `ell|L` differs from `p_j`. An element of exact
order `ell` cannot vanish in that reduction kernel. Therefore

\[
h^{L/\ell}\not\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\]

for every component, and the displayed gcd is exactly one.

If an intermediate gcd contains only part of a prime-power component, it is
still a proper divisor of `N`. The algorithm returns it. Thus partial
prime-power divisibility does not create an unhandled branch.

## 2. Exact common local order of `d`

Divisor stripping starts from the known multiple `Q=eM`. It keeps a multiple
of the global order of `d`. At termination, if the retained `m` were larger
than the exact global order, one prime valuation of `m` could be decreased
while keeping a multiple. This contradicts the terminal power test. Hence

\[
m=\operatorname{ord}_N(d).
\]

Let `m_j` be the order modulo `R_j`. If `m_j<m`, choose a prime `ell` whose
valuation is smaller in `m_j`. Then `m_j` divides `m/ell`, so the corresponding
component divides

\[
\gcd(d^{m/\ell}-1,N).
\]

Exactness of the global order prevents this gcd from being `N`. It is
therefore proper. On the no-factor branch every local order is exactly `m`.

The separate `gcd(m,N)` screen is necessary for the later reduction
argument. It is safe: `m` is an element order, so `m<=phi(N)<N`. This gcd
cannot equal `N`.

## 3. The identity `lcm(M,m)=Me`

Fix one component. The old subgroup `H_j=<g_j>` has order `M`. The assumed
relative-order return says that `d_j H_j` has exact order `e`. Therefore

\[
|\langle g_j,d_j\rangle|=Me.
\]

The same local group is cyclic. Its two named elements have exact orders
`M` and `m`. In a cyclic group, the subgroup generated by elements of these
orders has order `lcm(M,m)`. Equating the two expressions proves

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(M,m)=Me.
\]

This argument is valuation-exact and does not assume `gcd(M,e)=1` or
`gcd(M,m)=1`. Shared prime factors are covered.

This is also the point at which the local-order screens are essential. A
common relative index alone would not make the local orders of `d` equal.
F166 runs those screens before it invokes the identity.

## 4. Prime-primary construction of `h`

For a prime `ell|L`, let

\[
a=v_\ell(M),\qquad b=v_\ell(m),\qquad t=\max(a,b).
\]

If `a>=b`, the selected power of `g` has exact order `ell^a=ell^t` in every
component. If `b>a`, the selected power of `d` has exact order
`ell^b=ell^t` in every component.

The selected words commute. Their orders are powers of distinct primes.
The order of their product is therefore the product of these orders, not a
proper divisor caused by cancellation. Thus

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(h)
=\prod_{\ell\mid L}\ell^{v_\ell(L)}
=L=Me
\]

for every component. The global order is the lcm of these identical local
orders and is also `L`.

No local logarithm, negative exponent, or hidden CRT choice enters this
word.

## 5. The edge case `e=1`

When `e=1`, the lcm identity gives `L=M`. It also implies
`v_ell(m)<=v_ell(M)` for every prime. Therefore every prime-primary choice
in the displayed rule comes from `g` when the valuations tie or when the
valuation of `g` is larger. Their product still has exact common order `M`.

This does not prove that `d` is one global power of `g`. Local membership can
use different powers in different CRT components. The statement correctly
keeps alignment as the membership test and factor opportunity. For the
degenerate `M=L=1` case, the empty product is the standard identity word and
the certificate remains valid.

## 6. Local and global subgroup boundary

Locally, both `<h_j>` and `<g_j,d_j>` are subgroups of the same cyclic unit
group and have the same order `L`. Hence they are equal.

Globally, only

\[
\langle h\rangle\subseteq\langle g,d\rangle
\]

follows. The global two-generator group can be a noncyclic subdirect product
of its cyclic local images. F166 states this boundary explicitly and retains
the old words and provenance.

The `N=341` coordinate certificate has the correct structural form. The two
local relations for `d^2` use different powers of `g`. Thus local subgroup
orders agree while the global two-generator group is larger than the cyclic
group generated by `h`. This supports the boundary; the theorem does not
depend on the finite example.

## 7. Consequences for F164

The shortcut is sufficient for later local quotient fingerprints. In every
component, the new base subgroup `<h_j>` is exactly the old local subgroup
`<g_j,d_j>`. Thus F164 membership tests can use `(h,L)` as their next
certified local state.

The shortcut is not an aligned lattice row. It gives no single exponent
`a` with

\[
d^e=g^a\pmod N,
\]

and it does not express every discarded coordinate as a global power of
`h`. Therefore it cannot justify F164 rank-volume closure or global
coordinate compression. The statement correctly requires retention of

relations are needed.

an exact global subgroup order.

## 8. Quasipolynomial bit cost

The known factorization of `Q=eM` makes divisor stripping explicit. There
are at most `O(log Q)` successful prime removals and final failed prime
tests. Each step is one modular exponentiation with an exponent of
`O(log N+log e)` bits.

The local-order screens and lcm word use at most one item per distinct prime
factor of known integers. The new order satisfies `L=Me` and is a local
subgroup order, so `L<N`. Its complete factorization is obtained by merging
the supplied factorizations of `M` and `e`.

Under the F161 quasipolynomial cap, the number of operations, exponent bit
lengths, integer arithmetic, gcds, and storage are all quasipolynomial in the
input bit length. Strict updates have `e>=2`, so fewer than `log2 N` such
updates can occur. The unresolved `e=1` and source branches are not claimed
to terminate.
