# Proof of the F166 alignment-free lcm update

## 1. Exact global order from a known multiple

Equation (2) shows that the global order

\[
r=\operatorname{ord}_N(d)
\]

divides \(Q=eM\). The divisor-stripping procedure always keeps a multiple
of \(r\). At termination, for every prime \(\ell\mid m\),

\[
d^{m/\ell}\ne1\pmod N.
\tag{12}
\]

If \(m\ne r\), some prime \(\ell\) has
\(v_\ell(m)>v_\ell(r)\). Then \(r\mid m/\ell\), contrary to (12).
Therefore the output is exactly

\[
m=r=\operatorname{ord}_N(d).
\]

The factorization of \(m\) is obtained by decrementing the known prime
exponents of \(Q\). No unknown group order is factored.

Since \(m\) is an element order in \((\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\),
\(m\le\varphi(N)<N\). Hence \(\gcd(m,N)\) cannot equal \(N\). It is one
or a proper factor. In the F161 setting it is already one because
\(m\mid eM\) and both \(e\) and \(M\) survived their gcd screens.

## 2. The local-order screens are exact

Let

\[
m_j=\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(d).
\]

Every \(m_j\) divides the global order \(m\). Suppose \(m_j<m\). Choose
a prime \(\ell\) for which

\[
v_\ell(m_j)<v_\ell(m).
\]

Then \(m_j\mid m/\ell\), so

\[
d^{m/\ell}=1\pmod {R_j}.
\]

Consequently \(p_j\) divides \(E_\ell\). Exactness of the global order
gives \(E_\ell<N\), because \(d^{m/\ell}\ne1\pmod N\). Thus
\(E_\ell\) is a proper factor. This reasoning also covers a gcd that
contains only part of \(R_j\).

Therefore, if no proper gcd occurs, no local order can be smaller than
\(m\). Since each one divides \(m\), all are exactly \(m\). This proves
equation (5) for any number of odd prime-power CRT components.

## 3. Relative order forces the lcm identity

Fix one component. Its unit group is cyclic. The subgroup
\(H_j=\langle g_j\rangle\) has order \(M\). Equation (1) says that the
image of \(d_j\) in the quotient by \(H_j\) has exact order \(e\).
Therefore

\[
|\langle H_j,d_j\rangle|=M e.
\tag{13}
\]

In a cyclic group, the subgroup generated by two elements of orders \(M\)
and \(m\) has order \(\operatorname{lcm}(M,m)\). One way to see this is
that the two cyclic subgroups are the unique subgroups of their respective
orders. Their generated subgroup is the unique subgroup whose prime-power
order is the maximum of the two prime-power orders. Hence

\[
|\langle g_j,d_j\rangle|=\operatorname{lcm}(M,m).
\tag{14}
\]

Equating (13) and (14) proves

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(M,m)=Me.
\]

This proof compares only orders. It never compares the local exponents in
\(d_j^e=g_j^{a_j}\). It also covers shared prime factors. For each prime
\(\ell\), it gives the valuation identity

\[
\max(v_\ell(M),v_\ell(m))
=v_\ell(M)+v_\ell(e).
\tag{15}
\]

## 4. The prime-power lcm word has exact common order

Fix \(\ell\mid L\). If \(a_\ell\ge b_\ell\), the exact local order of
\(g\) is \(M\), so

\[
g^{M/\ell^{a_\ell}}
\]

has exact order \(\ell^{a_\ell}=\ell^{t_\ell}\) in every component. If
\(b_\ell>a_\ell\), the exact local order of \(d\) is \(m\), so

\[
d^{m/\ell^{b_\ell}}
\]

has exact order \(\ell^{b_\ell}=\ell^{t_\ell}\) in every component.
This proves the claimed order of each \(y_\ell\).

All unit groups are abelian. The factors \(y_\ell\) commute, and their
orders are powers of distinct primes. The order of their product is
therefore the product of their orders:

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(h)
=\prod_{\ell\mid L}\ell^{t_\ell}
=L.
\]

For example, if \(h^r=1\), raising this equation to the product of all
prime-power orders except \(\ell^{t_\ell}\) isolates an element with exact
order \(\ell^{t_\ell}\). Hence every \(\ell^{t_\ell}\) divides \(r\),
so \(L\mid r\). The reverse divisibility is immediate.

The global order modulo \(N\) is the lcm of the local orders, so it is also
\(L\). Since each local group \(\langle g_j,d_j\rangle\) has order \(L\)
and contains \(h_j\),

\[
\langle h_j\rangle=\langle g_j,d_j\rangle.
\tag{16}
\]

The F161 input screens give \(\gcd(L,N)=\gcd(Me,N)=1\). For a prime
\(\ell\mid L\), the local element \(h^{L/\ell}\) has exact order
\(\ell\). The reduction kernel from units modulo \(p_j^{\alpha_j}\) to
units modulo \(p_j\) is a \(p_j\)-group. Since \(\ell\ne p_j\), reduction
preserves this order. Thus \(h^{L/\ell}\ne1\pmod {p_j}\) for every
component, which proves

\[
\gcd(h^{L/\ell}-1,N)=1.
\]

This establishes the public common-order certificate. Formula (7) uses
only positive exponents. Modular inverses would also make negative exponent
variants harmless, but none are needed here.

## 5. Why local generation does not imply global generation

The global group \(\langle g,d\rangle\) maps onto every local cyclic group
in (16). The kernels of these projections can differ. Therefore this
global group can be a noncyclic subdirect product with order larger than
\(L\). The word \(h\) has global order only \(L\), so it need not generate
the full group.

The certificate in Section 6.2 of the statement makes this exact. In local
order-ten coordinates,

\[
g=(t_1^2,t_2^2),
\qquad
d=(t_1,t_2^3).
\]

A relation \(g^a d^b=1\), with \(a\) modulo five and \(b\) modulo ten,
would require

\[
2a+b=0\pmod {10},
\qquad
2a+3b=0\pmod {10}.
\]

Subtraction gives \(2b=0\pmod {10}\), so \(b=0\) or \(5\). The value
\(b=5\) makes the first equation impossible, and \(b=0\) forces
\(a=0\pmod5\). Hence the map

\[
C_5\times C_{10}\longrightarrow\langle g,d\rangle
\]

is injective and the global group has order \(50\). In contrast, the lcm
word \(h=gd^5\) has order ten.

The same certificate shows why order alignment remains a factor channel.
The local orders of \(d\) agree, so the order screens are all one. But the
local logarithms of \(d^2\) relative to \(g\) are one and three. The gcd
\(\gcd(d^2-g,N)=11\) separates them.

For \(e=1\), local membership only says that each coordinate of \(d\) is
some power of the corresponding coordinate of \(g\). The exponents can
differ. Thus the lcm stays \(M\), while the global group can still be
larger than \(\langle g\rangle\). The stated \(N=341,d=4\) example gives
exactly this behavior.

## 6. Relation to F164

F164 records aligned equations as integer lattice rows. The lcm word gives
an exact public word definition for \(h\), but it does not give equations
that express every old generator as one global power of \(h\). Therefore
it does not identify the global kernel of the old word map and cannot by
itself justify F164's rank-volume closure.

It is valid to keep \(g,d\), and all provenance while using \((h,L)\) as
the base for later local quotient fingerprints. Equation (16) makes its
local subgroup the desired old local group. If a later step needs to
compress or eliminate the old coordinates, it must first align them
against \(h\), obtain exact global relations, or return a factor.

## 7. Complexity and exact limit

The divisor-stripping loop removes at most \(\log_2 Q\) prime factors with
multiplicity. Each failed prime test occurs at most once after the last
successful removal for that prime. Thus it uses \(O(\log Q)\) modular
exponentiations. The local screens and lcm construction use at most the
number of distinct prime factors of the known integers.

All arithmetic operands have \(O(\log N+\log e)\) bits. In F161,
\(e\) is at most the fixed quasipolynomial cap and its factorization is
obtained in quasipolynomial time. The F166 update is therefore
deterministic quasipolynomial time.

When \(e\ge2\), the new order is \(Me>M\). The same bound as F161 gives
fewer than \(\log_2N\) strict enlargements. The unresolved source branches
remain unchanged: F166 does not force a released block, a bounded common
relative order, or progress when \(e=1\). It is a decoder simplification,
not a complete factoring algorithm.
