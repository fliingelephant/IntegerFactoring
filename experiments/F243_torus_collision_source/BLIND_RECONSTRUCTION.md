## 2. Collision source and amplification

For an exact integer law with masses (mu_z), direct partition by residue
class gives the collision-energy identity

\[
\kappa_m=
\sum_{a\bmod m}\left(\sum_{z\equiv a\bmod m}\mu_z\right)^2
-\sum_z\mu_z^2.
\]

(1). This identity in fact does not need squarefreeness, although only
prime moduli are used below.

If a trial has (Z\ne Z') and (Z\equiv Z'\pmod\ell), then
(ellmid\Delta). Since the full (ell)-primary exponent of a shifted
residual is less than (n), (Delta^n) contains that entire prime power.

Now grant

\[
A=\prod_j b_j^{a_j},\qquad T=\prod_j(a_j+1).
\]

A uniform divisor is obtained by choosing each exponent independently and
uniformly from ({0,\ldots,a_j\}). Reduction modulo an exterior prime
(ell) maps the (T) divisors into the subgroup (H_\ell) generated by
the (b_j), of size (h_\ell). If (c_x) is the number mapping to
(x\in H_\ell), Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\Pr(Z\equiv Z'\pmod\ell)
=\sum_x(c_x/T)^2\ge {1\over h_\ell}.
\]

Distinct divisors are distinct integers and a uniform divisor has equality
probability (1/T). Therefore

\[
\kappa_\ell\ge {1\over h_\ell}-{1\over T}.
\]

Suppose every prime in one fixed exterior residual has
(kappa_\ell\ge1/Q(n)). For (R) independent trials, the probability of
missing a fixed prime is at most

\[
(1-1/Q)^R\le e^{-R/Q}\le {1\over2n}
\]

when (R=\lceil Q(n)\log(2n)\rceil). The residual has fewer than (n)
distinct prime divisors. A union bound therefore shows that all its prime
supports are hit, and hence all their primary parts are absorbed after the
(n)-th powers, with probability at least (1/2).

Under the F242 contract, an independent uniform unit discriminant has the
required one of four Legendre pairs with probability (1/4), and the
conditional F242 success probability is at least (1/2). Multiplication of
these three conditional bounds gives

\[
{1\over2}\cdot {1\over4}\cdot {1\over2}={1\over16}.
\]

For the uniform-divisor law, (Z\le A), so (Delta<A), and

\[

\le n(R+1)\log_2 A
=O((R+1)n^2).
\]

## 3. Local norm and trace laws

If (D) is a square modulo (ell), an invertible linear change turns
(a^2-Db^2) into (xy). The zero fiber then has size (2\ell-1), and
each nonzero fiber has size (ell-1). If (D) is a nonsquare, this is the
norm from (mathbb F_{\ell^2}): the zero fiber has size (1), and each
nonzero fiber has size (ell+1). In either case, summing squared fiber
sizes yields

\[
\Pr(Q_D(V)=Q_D(V'))
={\ell^3+\ell^2-\ell\over\ell^4}
={\ell^2+\ell-1\over\ell^3}.
\]

The same-vector atom is (1/\ell^2), so the distinct-vector rate is
((\ell^2-1)/\ell^3). The difference (V-V') is uniform. Its norm is zero
on (2\ell-1) vectors in the split case and only on the zero vector in the
nonsplit case. This gives respectively
((2\ell-1)/\ell^2) and (1/\ell^2); after removing the same-vector atom,
the nonsplit useful rate is exactly zero.

For a uniform unit (z) in the split algebra or quadratic field, the map
(z\mapsto z/\bar z) is a surjective homomorphism onto the norm-one torus.
All fibers have the same size, so (U=z/\bar z) is uniform on its torus of
order (m=\ell-(D/\ell)). On that cyclic torus,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(U)=\operatorname{tr}(U')
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U'=U\text{ or }U'=U^{-1}.
\]

Exactly two points, (U=\pm1), are fixed by inversion. Hence the total
number of ordered trace-colliding pairs is (2m-2), giving

\[
\Pr(\operatorname{tr}(U)=\operatorname{tr}(U'))={2m-2\over m^2}.
\]

Removing the (m) identical ordered pairs leaves
((m-2)/m^2).

Write (z=a+bw), (z'=c+ew), with (w^2=D). Cross multiplication gives

\[
U=U'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad bc-ae=0.
\]

Also

\[
\operatorname{tr}(U)=2{a^2+Db^2\over a^2-Db^2},
\]

so equality of two same-(D) traces is equivalent to

\[
(bc-ae)(bc+ae)=0.
\]

The omitted scalar factor is a unit, so the zero condition loses (D).
For discriminants (D,E), the cross-multiplied numerator is, up to the
unit (4),

\[
Db^2c^2-Ea^2e^2.
\]

Conditional on fixed nonzero (a,b,c,e), independent uniform (D,E) make
their ratio uniform in (mathbb F_\ell^*). The displayed expression is
zero for exactly one ratio, hence with probability (1/(\ell-1)). For a
nonuniform law the same probability is precisely a multiplicative
correlation of that law. Adaptive dependence of the coefficients on the
discriminants would be outside this claim.

All these calculations concern equality after local reduction. Replacing an
exact integer difference (0) by (1) deletes such events and cannot add a
useful modular collision. For a scalar coordinate or trace, exact integer
equality can collect several local points, so the deleted mass can exceed
the atom coming only from equality of the full local object.

## 4. Canonical coordinates and discriminants

For either coordinate of (x^2-Dy^2=1), fixing its value modulo one hidden
prime leaves at most two choices for the other coordinate. CRT therefore
gives at most (2\cdot2=4) product-torus points above any fixed canonical
integer coefficient.

Let (C_a) count torus points whose chosen canonical coefficient is
congruent to (a\pmod\ell). At most (lceil N/\ell\rceil) canonical
integers occur in one residue class, so

\[
C_a\le4\lceil N/\ell\rceil,
\qquad
\Pr(Z\equiv Z'\pmod\ell)
=\sum_a(C_a/M)^2
\le {4\lceil N/\ell\rceil\over M}.
\]

For distinct odd primes, after ordering them the two smallest possible are
(3,5). For every orientation,

\[
M=(p-\epsilon_p)(q-\epsilon_q)
\ge(p-1)(q-1)
\ge {8N\over15}.
\]

Using (lceil N/\ell\rceil\le N/\ell+1) gives

\[
{4\lceil N/\ell\rceil\over M}
\le {15\over2\ell}+{15\over2N}.
\]

An exterior prime divides a shifted factor no larger than (p+1<N), so the
last quantity is strictly below (15/\ell). Deleting exact integer equality
only lowers it. A union bound for the two coordinate words is therefore
strictly below (30/\ell), which is too small to be inverse-QP when
(ell) is super-QP.

For a fixed Jacobi sign, the nontrivial quadratic character on
((\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^*) splits the (arphi(N)) units into two equal
sets. Thus a uniform conditioned discriminant has support
(S=\varphi(N)/2) and atom (1/S=2/\varphi(N)). If (c_a) is its count in
a residue class modulo (ell), then
(c_a-1< N/\ell). Its distinct-integer collision probability satisfies the
slightly stronger bound

\[
\kappa_\ell
={1\over S^2}\sum_a c_a(c_a-1)
< {N\over\ell S}
={2N\over\ell\varphi(N)}
\le {15\over4\ell}
<{15\over2\ell}.
\]

This proves the stated generic-scale conclusion. The fiber argument uses
only a single coordinate at a time. It gives no bound for cross-coordinate
determinants or arbitrary nonlinear integer carries, exactly as the
statement warns.

## 5. Factor-free gcd-divisor law

For (d\mid A), writing (X=dy) shows that

\[
\Pr(\gcd(X,A)=d)={\varphi(A/d)\over A}\le {1\over d};
\]

the formula also covers (d=A) through (X=0). Sampling (X) and taking
one gcd needs no factorization of (A).

For distinct (d<e\le A) with (e\equiv d\pmod\ell), write
(e=d+k\ell). Dropping the divisor restriction and using the pointwise
mass bound gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_\ell
&\le2\sum_{d=1}^A{1\over d}
       \sum_{k\ge1:\ d+k\ell\le A}{1\over d+k\ell}\\
&\le {2H_A^2\over\ell}
\le {2(1+\log A)^2\over\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the inner denominator is at least (k\ell), and
(H_A\le1+\log A). At an exponentially large exterior prime this is
exponentially small up to a polynomial numerator. If distinct divisors in
the support have distinct residues modulo (ell), the defining collision
event is empty and (kappa_\ell=0). This proves both stated obstructions.

## 6. Pell trace window

Modulo an exterior prime (ell), the algebra with (w^2=A) is either
split or the field (mathbb F_{\ell^2}). The element
(eta=N+w) has norm (N^2-A=1). The norm-one group has order
(ell-(A/\ell)), so

\[
h_\ell=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(\eta)
\mid \ell-\left({A\over\ell}\right).
\]

For independent (I,J) uniform on ({0,\ldots,T-1\}), equality of the
coefficient pairs is equivalent to (I\equiv J\pmod {h_\ell}). If
(T=q h_\ell+r), then (r) residue classes have (q+1) representatives
and (h_\ell-r) have (q). Counting ordered unequal pairs gives

\[
r(q+1)q+(h_\ell-r)q(q-1)
=h_\ell q(q-1)+2rq,
\]

and division by (T^2) proves the displayed collision law. Here this
quotient (q) is unrelated to the prime factor named (q).

Since (ar\eta=\eta^{-1}=N-w),

\[
X_k={\eta^k+\eta^{-k}\over2}=T_k(N).
\]

The real value (eta=N+\sqrt{N^2-1}>1) makes these (X_k) strictly
increasing for (k\ge0). Locally, trace equality in a cyclic norm-one
group is equality up to inversion. Thus

\[
X_i\equiv X_j\pmod\ell
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
i\equiv j\pmod {h_\ell}
\ \text{or}\ 
i\equiv-j\pmod {h_\ell}.
\]

For distinct (0\le i,j<T), the nonzero difference has magnitude at most
(T-1), and the positive sum is at most (2T-3). If
(h_\ell\ge2T-2), neither can be a nonzero multiple of (h_\ell), so the
distinct trace-collision probability is exactly zero.

Conversely, (eta^{h_\ell}=1) implies
(X_{h_\ell}\equiv1\pmod\ell). If (h_\ell\le H), the public word

\[
B_H=\prod_{k=1}^H(X_k-1)
\]

contains (ell) without knowing its order. Since
(log|X_k|=O(kn)), one has
(log|B_H|=O(nH^2)). It is numerical-QP when (H) is, and (B_H^n)
also supplies any full shifted (ell)-primary part. Therefore bounded
random trace windows cannot help for large meta-order, while QP-small
meta-order is already covered deterministically. This is exactly the stated
Pell-window boundary.
