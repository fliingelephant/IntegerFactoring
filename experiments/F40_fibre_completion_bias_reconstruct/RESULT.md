# Fibre completion bias: context-free reconstruction

## Setup

Let (N=pq), where (p) and (q) are distinct odd primes, let
(A=\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z), and let (R\in A^\times).  Write

\[
K=A[i],\qquad i^2=-1,
\]

with conjugation (\overline{x+yi}=x-yi) and norm
(n(x+yi)=x^2+y^2).  In the quaternion algebra generated by (i,j),
use

\[
i^2=j^2=-1,\qquad ji=-ij,
\]

so that (ja=\bar a j) for (a\in K).  A source and a completion are

\[
u=x+yi,\quad n(u)=-R,
\qquad
c=z+wi,\quad n(c)=R.
\]

Then

\[
\beta=u+cj
\]

has quaternion norm (n(u)+n(c)=0).  Over each
(k=\mathbf F_r), (r\in\{p,q\}), the quaternion algebra is split.
Thus a nonzero (\beta) is represented by a rank-one (2\times2)
matrix, with an intrinsic image line and row line in
(\mathbf P^1(k)).  Changing the splitting only applies fixed
projective coordinate changes to these two lines.

Write

\[
\chi_r=\left(\frac{-1}{r}\right).
\]

The affine source conic has

\[
\#\{(x,y)\in k^2:x^2+y^2=-R\}=r-\chi_r. \tag{1}
\]

This is the standard quadratic-character count; it is independent of
the nonzero right-hand side.

## 1. A rational branch has bounded line fibres

Let the projective source conic be

\[
\bar C^-:\ X^2+Y^2=-R T^2\subset\mathbf P^2_k.
\]

Suppose a rational completion is represented on this conic by
common-degree homogeneous forms (Z,W,H), of degree (d\leq D), so
that on the affine chart

\[
z=Z/H,\qquad w=W/H,
\]

and (Z^2+W^2=R H^2) in the function field of (\bar C^-).  After
clearing the source and completion denominators, its projective
quaternion is

\[
\widetilde\beta=(X+iY)H+T(Z+iW)j. \tag{2}
\]

Its four matrix coordinates are sections of

\[
\mathcal L=\mathcal O_{\bar C^-}(d+1).
\]

They have zero determinant, because

\[
n(\widetilde\beta)
=(X^2+Y^2)H^2+T^2(Z^2+W^2)=0.
\]

Remove their common base divisor.  Since (\bar C^-) is a smooth
projective curve, the resulting rational map extends uniquely across
all removed points to a morphism into the projective rank-one quadric

\[
Q\simeq \mathbf P^1_{\rm image}\times\mathbf P^1_{\rm row}.
\]

Under the Segre embedding,
(\mathcal O_Q(1)=\mathcal O_Q(1,1)).  If (e\) is the effective base
divisor, the two factor maps therefore satisfy

\[
\deg L_{\rm image}^*\mathcal O(1)
+\deg L_{\rm row}^*\mathcal O(1)
=\deg\bigl(\mathcal L(-e)\bigr)
\leq \deg\mathcal L
=2(d+1). \tag{3}
\]

Here (\deg\mathcal O_{\bar C^-}(1)=2).  Consequently, if either
intrinsic line map (L) is nonconstant, then for every geometric line
(\ell\)

\[
\#L^{-1}(\ell)\leq\deg L\leq2(d+1)\leq2(D+1), \tag{4}
\]

where points are already bounded with multiplicity.  This remains
valid for inseparable maps: a fibre's scheme length is the degree of
the morphism.

This argument also explains all boundary cases.  Points at infinity
were included from the outset.  A pole of the affine completion either
has no algorithmic output or has the value supplied by the unique
projective extension.  A common base point is handled by cancelling
the base divisor on the smooth curve.  Assigning an unrelated ad hoc
value at an undefined base point would define a different branch and
is not covered by the intrinsic-map assertion.

Now let (U_r) be uniform on the affine source conic and suppose a
current-point selector may choose any one of (B) displayed branches.
The selector may depend arbitrarily on the current point and on other
available information.  Assume, however, that every displayed branch
under consideration reduces locally to a nonconstant intrinsic line
map satisfying the degree bound above.  For every fixed
(\ell\in\mathbf P^1(k)),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr[\text{no factor is output and the selected line is }\ell]\\
&\quad\leq
\Pr[\text{at least one displayed branch takes the value }\ell]\\
&\quad\leq \frac{2B(D+1)}{r-\chi_r}. \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus (5) is an **unconditional output subprobability** bound.  It is
not a bound after conditioning on the no-factor event.  The right-hand
side may of course exceed (1), in which case the assertion is only
the corresponding trivial upper bound.

## 2. Algebraic graph components

The same statement has a representation-independent local form.
Compactify the completion conic as

\[
\bar C^+:\ Z^2+W^2=R S^2.
\]

Let (\Gamma_i\) be a selectable irreducible one-dimensional graph
component in (\bar C^-\times\bar C^+), and let

\[
\nu_i:\widetilde\Gamma_i\longrightarrow\Gamma_i
\]

be its normalization.  On the product, the cleared quaternion

\[
(X+iY)S+(Z+iW)Tj \tag{6}
\]

has bidegree ((1,1)).  After cancelling its base divisor on
(\widetilde\Gamma_i), it gives image and row maps to the two factors
of the rank-one Segre quadric.  Put

\[
\delta_i=\deg\nu_i^*\mathcal O(1,1).
\]

For either line map (L_i),

\[
\deg L_i^*\mathcal O(1)\leq\delta_i. \tag{7}
\]

If (L_i) is nonconstant, each of its geometric fibres therefore has
at most (\delta_i) points, counted with multiplicity.  For an
arbitrary selector among nonconstant components, a union bound on the
normalizations gives

\[
\#\{\text{selectable normalized graph points over a fixed line}\}
\leq\sum_i\delta_i. \tag{8}
\]

Projecting these points to the source can only decrease the count.

There is one essential exception.  If (L_i) is constant and
(\Gamma_i\to\bar C^-) is dominant, the fibre over that constant line
is the whole component.  The section defining that fibre vanishes
identically, so (7) cannot be used as a point-count bound.  Such a
component is an explicit **constant source-dominating degeneration**
and must be reported or excluded.  A vertical component, by contrast,
is supported over a finite exceptional source set and can be counted
separately.

## 3. What marginal atom bounds do and do not imply

Let (\varepsilon) denote either the bound in (5) or its graph-degree
analogue from (8), divided by the number of affine source points.  The
valid consequences are the following.

* A fixed target line has no-factor output subprobability at most
  (\varepsilon).
* The same holds for a target measurable with respect to the past when
  the present source call is fresh and independent: condition on the
  past, apply the fixed-target bound, and average.
* A collision between independent fresh calls is bounded by the atom
  bound for the call exposed second, after conditioning on the first
  output and all earlier history.
* A menu of (M) displayed transforms of a fixed or past-measurable
  target costs at most a union factor (M).  Likewise, membership in a
  finite exceptional set (E\) costs at most (|E|\varepsilon).
* In particular, for a finite menu of nonidentity projective
  transforms, the exceptional stabilizer set is the union of their
  fixed-point sets.  Each nonidentity element of
  (\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)) has at most two geometric fixed points,
  so a menu of (M) such transforms gives at most (2M\varepsilon)
  before overlaps are removed.

There is no same-source conclusion.  Two nonconstant maps (F(U))
and (G(U)) may each have tiny marginal atoms while satisfying
(F(U)=G(U)) identically.  The example (F=G=\mathrm{id}) on
(\mathbf P^1(k)) already has this property.  Thus equality of two
maps evaluated on the same source, including two branches or two line
constructions from one source, requires a joint algebraic analysis;
it cannot be bounded from the two marginal fibre estimates.

## 4. Unequal reduced degrees of an explicitly supplied map

This detector is valid when a composed projective map is explicitly
supplied over (A) as

\[
[P(S,T):Q(S,T)], \tag{9}
\]

where (P,Q\in A[S,T]) are homogeneous binary forms of the same
declared degree (d).  Assume their reductions define a nonzero pair
over both CRT fields.  Let (G_r) be the homogeneous gcd of (P_r,Q_r)
over (\mathbf F_r).  The reduced map degree is

\[
d_r=d-\deg G_r. \tag{10}
\]

If one reduction is a zero pair, or if one individual form is zero in
only one CRT field, scanning the supplied coefficients and taking
their gcds with (N) already exposes a proper factor.

Otherwise use the homogeneous subresultant sequence of (P,Q).  Over
a field, its vanishing pattern determines (\deg G_r): all indices
below the gcd degree vanish, and the subresultant at the gcd degree is
nonzero.  If (d_p\ne d_q), then
(\deg G_p\ne\deg G_q).  Suppose, for example, that
(\deg G_p>\deg G_q=g).  The homogeneous subresultant at index (g)
is zero modulo (p) and nonzero modulo (q).  At least one of its
coefficients (a), evaluated modulo (N), therefore satisfies

\[
\gcd(a,N)=p. \tag{11}
\]

The opposite inequality similarly exposes (q).

Homogeneous subresultants can be computed by division-free
determinantal or fraction-free standard algorithms.  Scanning their
coefficients and applying the integer Euclidean algorithm is
polynomial in (d), the input coefficient length, and (\log N).  An
implementation that instead attempts a pivot inversion also succeeds:
a nonunit pivot yields the same proper gcd immediately.

This proof is deliberately limited to the explicitly supplied binary
forms (9).  It makes no claim that an implicit graph, a multivalued
correspondence, or a selector can first be converted to such forms by
an eliminant with the same complexity or semantics.  No graph-eliminant
extension is asserted.

## 5. Exact uniform sampling from one public conic point

The construction works for either norm fibre.  Let

\[
C_\kappa:\ x^2+y^2=\kappa,
\qquad \kappa\in A^\times,
\]

and suppose a public affine point ((a,b)\in C_\kappa(A)) is known.
The standard quadratic parametrization is

\[
\begin{aligned}
X(s,t)&=a(s^2-t^2)-2bst,\\
Y(s,t)&=b(s^2-t^2)+2ast,\\
H(s,t)&=s^2+t^2.
\end{aligned} \tag{12}
\]

It satisfies

\[
X^2+Y^2=\kappa H^2. \tag{13}
\]

Over every (\mathbf F_r), (12) is the usual isomorphism from
(\mathbf P^1) to the smooth projective conic.  Its projective
parameters with (H\ne0) correspond bijectively to the affine conic
points.

Choose ((s,t)\) uniformly from (A^2), evaluate (H), and compute

\[
g=\gcd(H,N).
\]

There are exactly three cases.

1. If (g=1), return
   ((XH^{-1},YH^{-1})\).  This is a direct accepted sample.
2. If (1<g<N), return (g) as a proper factor.  This includes a
   parameter that is zero or maps to infinity at exactly one CRT
   prime.
3. If (g=N), reject this parameter pair and resample.  This includes
   being zero or at infinity at both CRT primes.

For a fixed (r\), the affine conic has (r-\chi_r) points by (1), and
each corresponding projective parameter has exactly (r-1) nonzero
homogeneous representatives.  Among the (r^2) ordered parameter
pairs, the number giving a direct affine value is therefore

\[
(r-1)(r-\chi_r).
\]

CRT independence gives the exact direct-acceptance probability

\[
\boxed{
\Pr[g=1]
=\prod_{r\in\{p,q\}}
\frac{(r-1)(r-\chi_r)}{r^2}.} \tag{14}
\]

Moreover, every point of (C_\kappa(A)) has exactly
((p-1)(q-1)) accepted parameter-pair preimages.  Hence the returned
point, conditional on direct acceptance, is exactly uniform on the
same norm fibre.  Formula (14) counts only direct samples; proper-gcd
factor outcomes are not incorrectly folded into rejection or
acceptance.

## 6. Explicit constant-line completion families

Suppose one public source/completion pair is known:

\[
n(u_0)=-R,\qquad n(c_0)=R.
\]

Because (R) is a unit, (u_0) is invertible.  Set

\[
h=u_0^{-1}c_0.
\]

Then

\[
n(h)=\frac{n(c_0)}{n(u_0)}=-1. \tag{15}
\]

For every source (u) with (n(u)=-R), choose

\[
c=uh.
\]

It has (n(c)=R), and

\[
\beta=u+(uh)j=u(1+hj). \tag{16}
\]

At either CRT prime, the matrix of (u) is invertible, while
(1+hj) is a fixed nonzero norm-zero, hence rank-one, matrix.  Left
multiplication by an invertible matrix does not change row space.
Thus (16) has a constant row line at both CRT primes.

There is a dual family.  Choose

\[
c=h\bar u.
\]

Using (ju=\bar u j),

\[
\beta=u+(h\bar u)j=u+hju=(1+hj)u. \tag{17}
\]

Right multiplication by an invertible matrix does not change image.
Thus (17) has a constant image line at both CRT primes.  These are
exactly constant source-dominating degenerations of the kind isolated
in Section 2.

## 7. Independent check of the (N=21) example

Take (N=21), (R=1),

\[
u_0=2-4i,\qquad c_0=-1.
\]

Modulo (21),

\[
n(u_0)=4+16=20=-1,
\qquad n(c_0)=1.
\]

For

\[
h=2+4i,
\]

one has

\[
u_0h=(2-4i)(2+4i)=20=-1=c_0,
\]

so (h=u_0^{-1}c_0), and (n(h)=20=-1).  With (k=ij),

\[
\alpha=1+hj=1+2j+4k. \tag{18}
\]

For a direct split-matrix check, take (s=2,t=4).  Since
(s^2+t^2=20=-1\pmod {21}), define

\[
\rho(i)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\rho(j)=
\begin{pmatrix}s&t\\t&-s\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These matrices square to (-I) and anticommute.  With

\[
\rho(k)=\rho(i)\rho(j)
=\begin{pmatrix}t&-s\\-s&-t\end{pmatrix},
\]

equation (18) gives

\[
\rho(\alpha)=I+s\rho(j)+t\rho(k)
=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}. \tag{19}
\]

Therefore, for (u=x+yi),

\[
\rho(u\alpha)
=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\-y&x\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}
=\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}0&2y\\0&2x\end{pmatrix}}. \tag{20}
\]

At both (3) and (7), (u) is invertible and (2) is nonzero, so
this matrix has rank one and constant row line ([0:1]), exactly as
claimed.

## 8. Detector-relative conclusion

The constant families prove a geometric degeneration, not a universal
security or detectability statement.  Constancy at both CRT primes
does not by itself force the underlying quaternions or completions at
two source points to be equal: in (16), (u(1+hj)) generally varies
with (u).  It does not force the constant line to lie in the fixed
set of any particular finite menu of tested nonidentity transforms.
And both local reduced line-map degrees are (0), so an
unequal-reduced-degree detector has no discrepancy to expose.

If a detector directly compares the repeated row lines themselves, it
will of course observe equality; equality at both CRT primes normally
produces no proper-factor asymmetry.  Beyond these precise detector
predicates, metric tests, nonlinear invariants, and other uses of the
strong correlation may still distinguish or exploit the family.  No
claim about those possibilities is made here.
