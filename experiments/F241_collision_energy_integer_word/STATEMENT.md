# F241 candidate — collision energy is the exact Las Vegas integer-word source

## Status and scope

This is a proof-only conditional source theorem for P205.  It has three
parts.

1. The deterministic word `(N-1)^n` removes every residual primary whose
   rational prime already divides `N-1`.
2. Differences of two integer samples have an exact modular-collision
   energy.  This energy gives the exact expected P205 residual reduction.
3. Uniform random divisors of a completely factored `N-1` have an exact
   character formula.  They give a constant-success word under a small
   subgroup-image condition, but can have zero useful collision energy.

This is not an all-input factoring algorithm.  It does not prove the small
subgroup-image condition.  It also grants the complete factorization of
`N-1` for the random-divisor construction.

## 1. Deterministic removal of the `N-1` support

Let

\[
N=pq
\]

for distinct odd primes, and put

\[
n=\lceil\log _2(N+1)\rceil,
\qquad d=\gcd(p-1,q-1),
\qquad s_p={p-1\over d},\quad s_q={q-1\over d}.
\]

For a positive integer `s`, define its part coprime to `N-1` by

\[
s^{\perp}=
\prod_{\substack{\ell^e\parallel s\\ \ell\nmid N-1}}\ell^e.
\]

The public word

\[
W_0=(N-1)^n
\]

has `O(n^2)` bits and gives the exact P205 residuals

\[
\boxed{r_p=s_p^{\perp},\qquad r_q=s_q^{\perp}.}
\]

Thus P205 already factors in expected numerical-QP time if either
`s_p^perp` or `s_q^perp` is numerical QP.  The remaining hard residuals
may be assumed coprime to `N-1`.

## 2. Exact collision-energy identity

Let `Z` be any public exactly samplable positive-integer law, and let
`Z,Z'` be independent samples.  Define

\[
\Delta=
\begin{cases}
|Z-Z'|,&Z\ne Z',\\
1,&Z=Z',
\end{cases}
\qquad W_\Delta=\Delta^n.
\]

For every squarefree positive integer `m`, define the distinct-sample
collision probability

\[
\kappa_m=
\Pr\bigl(Z\ne Z'\ \hbox{and}\ Z\equiv Z'\pmod m\bigr).
\]

Let `s<N`, with prime-power factorization

\[
s=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P}\ell^{e_\ell}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,{\gcd(s,W_\Delta)\over s}
= {1\over s}
\left[
1+
\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\mathcal P}
\left(\prod_{\ell\in S}(\ell^{e_\ell}-1)\right)
\kappa_{\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell}
\right].
}
\tag{CE}
\]

For the P205 residual associated with `s`, the left side is exactly
`E[1/r]`.  Therefore, after sampling the word and then a fresh uniform
unit, one complete P205 trial has factor probability at least

\[
\boxed{
{1\over2}
\max\left{
\mathbb E{1\over r_p},
\mathbb E{1\over r_q}
\right}.
}
\tag{P205-CE}
\]

This implication is history-wise.  The integer law may depend on the
public history, provided the two samples and the later P205 unit are fresh
conditional on that history.

The nonempty sum in `(CE)` is the useful modular collision energy.  The
ordinary collision probability `Pr(Z=Z')` is excluded because equal
integers produce `Delta=1`.  A bound on the number of modular residues in
the support is useful only when it forces collisions between *different*
integers.

## 3. Uniform divisors of `N-1`

Write the granted complete factorization

\[
A=N-1=\prod_{j=1}^k b_j^{a_j},
\qquad
T=\tau(A)=\prod_{j=1}^k(a_j+1).
\]

Choose independent uniform exponents `J_j in {0,...,a_j}` and put

\[
Z=\prod_{j=1}^k b_j^{J_j}.
\]

Thus `Z` is exactly uniform on the positive divisors of `A`.  For every
squarefree `m` coprime to `A`, let `G_m=(Z/mZ)^times`.  Then

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_m=
{1\over\varphi(m)}
\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G_m}
\prod_{j=1}^k
\left|
{1\over a_j+1}\sum_{e=0}^{a_j}\chi(b_j)^e
\right|^2
-{1\over T}.
}
\tag{D}
\]

Let `H_m` be the subgroup of `G_m` generated by the reductions of the
`b_j`, and put `h_m=|H_m|`.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_m\ge {1\over h_m}-{1\over T}.
}
\tag{H}
\]

Fix a public numerical-QP value `Q(n)`.  Suppose that for every rational
prime `ell` dividing one of `s_p^perp,s_q^perp`, say `s_i^perp`,

\[
h_\ell\le Q(n),
\qquad T\ge2Q(n).
\tag{S}
\]

Take

\[
R=\left\lceil2Q(n)\log(2n)\right\rceil
\]

independent divisor pairs, with differences `Delta_1,...,Delta_R`, and
form the unfactored public word

\[
W=(N-1)^n\prod_{t=1}^R\Delta_t^n.
\]

Then

\[
\Pr(s_i\mid W)\ge {1\over2}.
\]

Conditional on this event, P205 factors with probability at least `1/2`.
Hence the complete bank factors with probability at least `1/4`.  Its word
length is `O((R+1)n^2)`, and its remaining work is numerical QP under the
granted factorization and exact sampler.

## 4. Sharp obstruction and radix interpretation

The subtraction `-1/T` in `(D)` is essential.  For `A=b^a`, put
`h=ord_m(b)` and `T=a+1`.  Write

\[
T=qh+r,
\qquad0\le r<h.
\]

Uniform divisors `1,b,...,b^a` have the exact distinct-sample collision law

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_m={h q(q-1)+2rq\over T^2}.
}
\tag{P}
\]

In particular, `kappa_m=0` whenever `h>=T`: reduction modulo `m` is
injective on the complete integer sample support.  Randomness and support
size alone then give no residual progress.

The same collision interface describes random zero-defect radices.  If `B`
is a divisor of `N-1` and `H=(N-1)/B`, then for `1<=u<B`,

\[
uN=(uH)B+u.
\]

The map `B -> H` permutes the divisor set.  Thus any random-radix law is
only a known pushforward law on complementary divisors.  For example, at
`u=1`, every `B>1` has Euclidean quotient `H` and remainder one; the
exceptional divisor `B=1` can be handled as one separate public atom.  For
a residual modulus coprime to `u(N-1)`, two quotient bases collide exactly
when the corresponding complementary divisors collide.  The quotient
carry adds no independent entropy.

More generally, recursively factored binary-prefix children can provide
public prime bases for the same construction.  Formula `(CE)` still
applies.  It gives no all-input bound unless their products have
inverse-QP distinct-sample collision energy modulo one hidden residual.

The exact missing statement is therefore narrower than a generic random
word law: prove inverse-QP *distinct-integer modular aliasing* with enough
prime-power weight in one P205 residual.
