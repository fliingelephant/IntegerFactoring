## 2. Exact orbit-collapse theorem: PASS

From

\[
N=(a^m-1)/k
\]

one gets

\[
a^r a^{m-r}=a^m=1+kN.
\]

Thus \(a^{m-r}\) is an inverse of \(a^r\) modulo \(N\). The two stated
size hypotheses put it in \(1,\ldots,N-1\), so uniqueness of that residue
makes it the least positive inverse. The congruence also proves that the
endpoints are units.

All exact relation values on the trajectory are the same integer \(a^m\).
If \(m\) is odd and \(a\) is one basis block, every nonzero parity column is
the same. A dependency has an even number \(2t\) of copies and exact root

\[
R=(a^m)^t=(1+kN)^t\equiv1\pmod N.
\]

Because \(N\) is odd, the two gcds are exactly \(N\) and \(1\). If one copy
was already retained, every new kernel direction is generated modulo the
old kernel by pairing a new copy with that retained copy. Its normalized
root is \(+1\). Hence duplicate columns increase raw nullity but do not
enlarge the root image.

When \(a\) is already a prime basis block, every endpoint is a power of that
same block. Exact gcd refinement has no new prime support to expose. The
claimed no-split conclusion is correct.

## 4. CRT private-row family: PASS

The existence proof is sound.

For \(K_e=2^e-1\), Bertrand gives a prime

\[
2^{T+e}<q_e<2^{T+e+1}.
\]

The intervals are disjoint, so the primes are distinct. Also
\(K_e<2^T<q_e\), so every required inverse modulo \(q_e^2\) exists.

The CRT class \(a\) is reduced modulo

\[
M=2^T\prod_e q_e^2.
\]

It is odd, and modulo each \(q_e\) it is the nonzero class
\(-K_e^{-1}\). The classes reducing to 1 and \(a\) modulo \(M\) each have
six lifts with least positive representative above \(M\); at most one lift
in each set is divisible by 7. Thus reduced lifts modulo \(7M\) exist. They
are different because \(a\not\equiv1\pmod M\).

Dirichlet supplies primes in both classes. The classes are different, so
the two primes are different. Linnik supplies choices bounded by
\((7M)^{O(1)}\). Their least positive class representatives exceed \(M\),
so both primes exceed \(M\). Their product satisfies \(N_T\equiv a\pmod
M\).

The size estimate is exact:

\[
\log M
=T\log2+2\sum_{e=1}^T\log q_e
=\Theta(T^2).
\]

The lower bound \(p_T,r_T>M\) and the Linnik upper bound give
\(\log N_T=\Theta(\log M)=\Theta(T^2)\). Hence the bit length is
\(n_T=\Theta(T^2)\) and the trajectory length is
\(T=\Theta(\sqrt{n_T})\).

For \(1\le e\le T\), congruence \(N_T\equiv1\pmod {2^T}\) makes

\[
w_e=\frac{1+(2^e-1)N_T}{2^e}
\]

an integer. It is positive and strictly below \(N_T\), so it is the
canonical inverse of \(2^e\). The exact relation is

\[
P_e=1+K_eN_T.
\]

The selected CRT row gives

\[
P_e\equiv q_e\pmod {q_e^2},
\]

so \(v_{q_e}(P_e)=1\). For \(j\ne e\),

\[
P_j\equiv(K_e-K_j)K_e^{-1}\not\equiv0\pmod {q_e},
\]

because \(0<|K_e-K_j|<2^T<q_e\). Thus every column has a private odd
valuation row. The square classes are linearly independent, including
after complete exact refinement.

The candidate correctly disclaims P98 stability, direct-screen failure,
no-split behavior, and an \(n_T^2\)-long trajectory for this family. Its
conclusion is only that polynomially many consecutive power residues do not
force a binary closure from consecutiveness alone.

## 5. F98 scope claim: FAIL

```text

```

F99 says that the reported 166-relation witness still needs the number of
unique exact values, a kernel after duplicate-root directions are removed,
and a non-global root or block split. It then calls this a “missing progress
certificate.” This is not true of the current F98 artifact:

- F98 reports 9,414 distinct exact relation values.
- Its decoder removes the 3,134 repeated exact relation values before it
  forms the parity matrix.
- The resulting matrix has kernel dimension 488.
- The selected 166-column circuit uses 166 distinct exact values.
- Its root has proper gcds 19,727 and 10,267.

Therefore F98 already supplies all three requested certificates. F98 does
not claim that its 166 columns are 166 separate opportunities. It claims
one useful amortized circuit, and the proper gcd proves that claim.

F99 legitimately proves that raw record count and raw nullity can be false
progress. It does not create a live objection to F98's current deduplicated,
non-global 166-column certificate. Calling that certificate missing is
materially misleading and exceeds what the duplicate-value theorem proves.

The smallest correction is to replace the F98 paragraph with:

> This obstruction does not apply to the current F98 certificate. F98
> removes repeated exact relation values before decoding, reports that all
> 166 selected values are distinct, and exhibits a non-global root that
> factors its modulus. F99 only shows that future progress claims cannot use
> raw relation count or raw nullity alone.

After that correction, the mathematical core is suitable for re-audit.
