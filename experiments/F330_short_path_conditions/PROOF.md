# Exact identities for the ranked arithmetic path

**Family:** route:F31

Status: author proof. Independent reconstruction has not been requested.
No path-length or success-probability theorem is asserted.

## 1. A small-remainder reciprocity

Keep the definitions of Q and C in DESIGN.md. Let r=rep(a*t). Then

\[
\begin{split}
r>0:\quad
2Q(t)&=t+1+C_t(1,r-1)-C_t(h+1,h+r),\\
r=-s<0:\quad
2Q(t)&=t-1+C_t(h+1-s,h)-C_t(N-s+1,N-1).
\end{split} \tag{1}
\]

In particular,

\[
|2Q(t)-t|\leq |r|. \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Put I(k)=1 when the ordinary residue of k belongs to [1,h],
and put I(k)=0 otherwise. Pair the indices y and t-y in [1,t-1]. Their
multiplied residues are k and r-k modulo N. Neither residue is zero:
invertibility of a and 0<y,t-y<N exclude that event. Also k is not r.

For r>0, I(k)+I(r-k) equals 1 except for these disjoint intervals:

- k in [1,r-1] contributes 2;
- k in [h+1,h+r] contributes 0.

Summing over y in [1,t-1] gives

\[
2Q(t-1)=t-1+C_t(1,r-1)-C_t(h+1,h+r).
\]

The endpoint t has positive multiplied residue r, so add 2. This proves
the first line. The argument counts every index, including a self-pair
when t is even; no division into distinct pairs is needed.

For r=-s<0, the two exceptional intervals are instead [h+1-s,h], which
contributes 2, and [N-s+1,N-1], which contributes 0. The endpoint has
negative residue, so Q(t)=Q(t-1). This proves the second line.

The two positive-remainder counts lie in [0,r-1] and [0,r]. Therefore
1-r <= 2Q(t)-t <= r. The negative-remainder counts lie in [0,s] and
[0,s-1], giving -s <= 2Q(t)-t <= s-1. These imply (2). The proof uses
all residues and works for prime powers and repeated factors. QED.

Each right side uses at most 2|r|-1 public inverse-image membership tests
once b=a^(-1) mod N is known. This is a symbolic short-remainder identity;
ordinary Euclidean floor sums already evaluate Q in polynomial bit time.
It is not an asymptotic improvement over those floor sums when |r| is large.

## 2. Exact affine defects in a reflection step

Assume additionally Jacobi(a,N)=1, as in P246. Let L=Q(h) and d=L+h+1.
For an accepted positive y, put E(y)=2Q(y)-y. Write B=R^(-1) A R for
the auxiliary rank reflection with center L. Then

\[
\begin{split}
B(-y)&=(y+E(y))/2,\\
B(z)&=-2z+E(y)\quad(1\leq z\leq L,\ y=\min\{u:Q(u)=z\}),\\
B(z)&=d-z\quad(L<z\leq h),\qquad B(0)=0.
\end{split} \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** The rank of -y is L-Q(y), so its reflection has rank L+Q(y),
the rank of the positive coordinate Q(y). A positive z<=L has reflected
rank L-z, whose selected coordinate is -y with Q(y)=z. At z>L the
reflected rank wraps by d; subtracting L gives d-z. These are exactly
P246's rank and selection formulas. QED.

Thus every actual reflection step is an affine map of its F image,
with a discrete defect E at the selected negative coordinate. Formula (1)
provides a short description of the defect near small modular remainders.
It does not show that most defects are small or that repeated steps finish
quickly. Inferring either conclusion from the near-half shape would be a gap.

## 3. The constant a=-1 family

The interval and involution in this section are prior art: Jeřábek,
*Integer factoring and modular square roots*, Lemma 4.5, explicitly gives
FacRoot_{-1} on this interval and attributes this special reduction to
Buresh-Oppenheim. The matrix coordinates below analyze one explicit
traversal of that existing involution. They are not a new reduction or
a claim of external novelty.

Suppose N>1 and N=1 mod 4, and set a=N-1. Then Jacobi(a,N)=1,
L=0, d=(N+1)/2, and D={0,...,h}. Away from 0 and 1, a unit x obeys

\[
F(x)=|\operatorname{rep}(x^{-1})|. \tag{4}
\]

The start is 0. After its first nonterminal F/A stage the coordinate is h.
Every later nonterminal unit stage satisfies

\[
x'=(N+1)/2-|\operatorname{rep}(x^{-1})|. \tag{5}
\]

In the modular coordinate z=2x-1 this is

\[
z'=\sigma\frac{4}{z+1}\pmod N,
\qquad
\sigma=-\operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{rep}(x^{-1})). \tag{6}
\]

It is generated by the two constant projective matrices

\[
M_\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0&4\sigma\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7}
\]

Their characteristic discriminants are 17 for sigma=+1 and -15 for
sigma=-1. Their determinants are -4*sigma and hence units modulo odd N.

**Proof.** No negative y is accepted when a=-1, so Q vanishes and L=0.
For positive unit x, a*x is negative. If its centered inverse is positive,
P246's first branch returns that inverse; otherwise the scaled-inverse
branch negates it. This proves (4). Rank reflection on [0,h] fixes zero
and sends each positive v to d-v, proving (5). Multiplying (5) by two and
subtracting one gives -2*sign(w)*w modulo N, where w=rep(x^(-1)). Since
w=2/(z+1) modulo N, this gives (6). The matrix and discriminants follow
by direct calculation. QED.

One can also retain an exact integer pair (U,V) with z=U/V modulo N,
starting at (-2,1) after the first stage. Its update is

\[
(U,V)\longmapsto (4\sigma V,U+V). \tag{8}
\]

For each reached unit stage, V and U+V are units modulo N. A proper gcd
of U+V with N is exactly a nonunit event for x; V is still invertible.
From the first update onward, U is divisible by 4 and V is odd. The pair
is primitive over the integers: gcd(U,V)=1 holds initially and is
preserved because gcd(4V,U+V)=1 when U is divisible by 4, V is odd, and
gcd(U,V)=1. The first update gives (+/-4,-1), which begins that invariant.
After T updates, max(|U|,|V|)<=2*4^T, so retaining the integer lift costs
O(T) bits per coordinate. This is not a bound on the number of updates.

The fixed-point test must still occur before (5). A unit fixed by (4)
can be a nontrivial square root of one or a square root of -1, as prescribed
by P246's decoder. A zero gcd of U+V is not silently interpreted as a
proper factor. The original path and decoder remain authoritative.

For N with a prime divisor equal to 3 mod 4, -1 has no square root modulo
N, so every terminal P246 output in this a=-1 family is a proper divisor.
The domain parity assumption N=1 mod 4 still applies. For other N, a
returned square root of -1 need not by itself reveal a factor. This family
does not invoke or satisfy the random-square sampling hypothesis of P02.

The missing result is an actual-path hitting or path-length bound for the
sign word in (6). Matrix powering of a guessed repeated sign block does
not certify that the actual sign stayed constant, and an unproved
distributional assumption is not a Las Vegas bound.

## 4. Adjacent matching in the same constant family

Keep N=1 mod 4 and a=-1. The delete-adjacent auxiliary fixes zero and
sends each positive y to y+(-1)^(y+1). Consequently every nonterminal
unit stage away from x=1 has the exact modular form

\[
x'=\epsilon+\frac{s}{x}\pmod N,
\quad w=\operatorname{rep}(x^{-1}),\quad
s=\operatorname{sign}(w),\quad
\epsilon=(-1)^{|w|+1}. \tag{9}
\]

The four matrices are

\[
C_{\epsilon,s}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&s\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]

This follows directly from y=|w|=s*w and the auxiliary pairing. When
s=-1, a direct multiplication gives

\[
C_{\epsilon,-1}^3=-\epsilon I.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus an open, nonterminal arithmetic path cannot make three consecutive
moves with the same branch (epsilon,s=-1): the third move would return
to its earlier coordinate, contradicting the simple alternating-path
property in P246. This is a bounded-word restriction, not a running-time
bound; the branch can change instead.

An integer lift (U,V) for x=U/V updates by

\[
(U,V)\longmapsto(\epsilon U+sV,U). \tag{12}
\]

Starting after the first move at x=2, use (U,V)=(2,1). Each matrix is
unimodular, so primitive integer pairs remain primitive. Their height is
at most twice its previous value, giving O(T) bits after T stages. A
nonunit event for x is gcd(U,N)>1 while V remains a unit. Again, the
fixed-point decoder is tested before taking the next matrix step.
