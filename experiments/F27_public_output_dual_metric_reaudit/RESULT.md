rederived the general Smith-form quotient theorem, including its transpose

isotropic lines, colliding bounded directions, ties, and the smallest odd

## 1. CRT intersections, cosets, and uniform targets

Let \(A\in\mathbb Z^{t\times d}\), let \(N=pq\) for distinct primes, and
write

\[
\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r)=A\mathbb Z^d+r\mathbb Z^t
\qquad(r=p,q,N).
\]

The CRT-intersection identity is exact:

\[
\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,N)
=\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,p)\cap\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,q).
\tag{1.1}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, local coefficient vectors \(x_p,x_q\) can be
lifted coordinatewise to one \(x\in\mathbb Z^d\).  Then \(y-Ax\) is
divisible by both distinct primes and hence by \(N\).  This argument does
not require the two local images to have compatible bases.

Since the public lattice is contained in each local lattice, for every
target \(t_0\in\mathbb Z^t\), every
\(v\in\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,N)\), and \(r\in\{p,q\}\),

\[
t_0-v\in\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r)
\iff
t_0\in\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus even an adaptively or metrically selected public-lattice vector cannot
change the target's local output-module coset.  This says nothing about the
ordinary coordinate gcd of \(v\) or \(t_0-v\), a distinction the candidate
states explicitly.

If \(s_r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_r}(A\bmod r)\), the local image
has \(r^{s_r}\) residues among \(r^t\).  A target uniform modulo \(N\)
therefore has exact one-local probability

\[
p^{-(t-s_p)}+q^{-(t-s_q)}
-2p^{-(t-s_p)}q^{-(t-s_q)}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

CRT makes the two target residues independent; no independence assumption
about samples or hidden factors is being inserted.  When the common
codimension is zero both local modules are all of \(\mathbb Z^t\), so their
symmetric difference is empty.  When it is positive and the primes are
balanced, (1.3) is exponentially small in the input length.

All these statements transfer to

\[
\Lambda_{\rm dual}(A,r)
=r\mathbb Z^d+A^{\mathsf T}\mathbb Z^t
=\Lambda_{\rm out}(A^{\mathsf T},r).
\tag{1.4}
\]

The changed ambient dimension is \(d\), so the corresponding exponents are
\(d-s_p\) and \(d-s_q\), exactly as in the candidate.

## 2. Smith form and the general faithful quotient

Take an integer Smith decomposition

\[
UAV=S=\operatorname{diag}
(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_\rho,0,\ldots,0),
\]

where \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_t(\mathbb Z)\),
\(V\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z)\), and
\(\rho=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}A\).  Put
\(\delta_i=\gcd(\sigma_i,N)\).  Then

\[
U\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,N)
=\operatorname{diag}
(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_\rho,N,\ldots,N)\mathbb Z^t.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This follows coordinate by coordinate: the ideal generated by
\(\sigma_i\) and \(N\) is \(\delta_i\mathbb Z\), while a zero Smith row
contributes \(N\mathbb Z\).

If \(1<\delta_i<N\), the displayed gcd is already a proper factor.  Suppose
no such entry occurs.  Since the Smith entries form a divisibility chain,
the \(\delta_i\)'s are a block of \(1\)'s followed by a block of
\(N\)'s.  Let \(u\) be the number of \(1\)'s.  Crucially, this gives the
local Smith entries too:

- for \(i\le u\), \(\gcd(\sigma_i,N)=1\), so
  \(\gcd(\sigma_i,p)=\gcd(\sigma_i,q)=1\);
- for \(i>u\) among the nonzero Smith entries,
  \(\gcd(\sigma_i,N)=N\), so \(N\mid\sigma_i\), and the local entries
  are \(p\) and \(q\);
- every zero row likewise contributes the local modulus.

Consequently, for every \(r\in\{p,q,N\}\),

\[
U\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r)
=\mathbb Z^u\oplus r\mathbb Z^{t-u}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

In particular \(s_p=s_q=u\) on this branch.  Define

\[
E_{\rm out}=U^{-1}(\mathbb Z^u\oplus0).
\]

This is a public primitive rank-\(u\) sublattice: it is the image under a
unimodular automorphism of a coordinate direct summand.  Applying
\(U^{-1}\) to (2.2), and using
\(U^{-1}(r\mathbb Z^t)=r\mathbb Z^t\), gives the exact identities

\[
\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r)=E_{\rm out}+r\mathbb Z^t
\qquad(r=p,q,N).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let \(\pi_{\rm out}\) be orthogonal projection off
\(E_{\rm out}\otimes\mathbb R\).  Its image has dimension \(t-u\), and
primitivity makes \(\pi_{\rm out}(\mathbb Z^t)\) a full lattice in that
space.  Projection of (2.3) yields, with equality rather than just equal
determinants,

\[
\pi_{\rm out}\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r)
=r\,\pi_{\rm out}(\mathbb Z^t).
\tag{2.4}
\]

For completeness, if \(\Delta(E_{\rm out})\) is the intrinsic covolume of
\(E_{\rm out}\), extending a basis of this primitive lattice to a
unimodular basis gives

\[
\det \pi_{\rm out}(\mathbb Z^t)
=\Delta(E_{\rm out})^{-1},
\qquad
\det \pi_{\rm out}\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,r)
=\frac{r^{t-u}}{\Delta(E_{\rm out})}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The rank-zero and quotient-rank-zero cases follow with the standard
zero-dimensional covolume convention.

The transpose calculation is equally exact.  Transposing \(UAV=S\) gives

\[
A^{\mathsf T}=V^{-\mathsf T}S^{\mathsf T}U^{-\mathsf T}.
\]

Because \(U^{-\mathsf T}\mathbb Z^t=\mathbb Z^t\), applying
\(V^{\mathsf T}\) to the scaled dual produces the same local diagonal
entries, now in ambient dimension \(d\).  Thus, with

\[
E_{\rm dual}=V^{-\mathsf T}(\mathbb Z^u\oplus0),
\]

one has

\[
\Lambda_{\rm dual}(A,r)=E_{\rm dual}+r\mathbb Z^d,
\qquad
\pi_{\rm dual}\Lambda_{\rm dual}(A,r)
=r\,\pi_{\rm dual}(\mathbb Z^d)
\tag{2.6}
\]

for \(r=p,q,N\).  Here \(E_{\rm dual}\) is primitive of rank \(u\), the
quotient dimension is \(d-u\), and

\[
\det \pi_{\rm dual}\Lambda_{\rm dual}(A,r)
=\frac{r^{d-u}}{\Delta(E_{\rm dual})}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

This verifies the corrected placement of the inverse transpose and rules
out a hidden local-rank mismatch inside the claimed quotient.

Equations (2.4) and (2.6) identify a public quotient shape and the exact
three scales \(p,q,N\).  They do not orthogonally split the full integer
lattice as \(E\oplus rL\).  A unimodular Smith transformation can severely
distort Euclidean lengths, and an integer complement to \(E\) need not be
orthogonal.  Therefore the candidate is right to leave full-lattice SVP,
CVP, and coordinate-gcd coupling open.

The two Euclidean-duality identities are also dimensionally and
arithmetically correct:

\[
N\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,N)^*=K_N(A^{\mathsf T}),
\qquad
NK_N(A)^*=\Lambda_{\rm out}(A^{\mathsf T},N).
\tag{2.8}
\]

For the first, the summand \(N\mathbb Z^t\) forces a dual vector to be
\(u/N\) with \(u\in\mathbb Z^t\), and pairing with
\(A\mathbb Z^d\) gives \(A^{\mathsf T}u\equiv0\pmod N\).  For the
second, CRT reduces the annihilator of the kernel to the row image over
each of the two fields.  Smith form gives the same lift over
\(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\).

## 3. The fixed-completion batch

For columns

\[
\beta_i=(x_i,y_i,z,w)^{\mathsf T},
\]

let \(A\) be the \(4\times m\) column matrix and let \(T\) have columns
\((x_i,y_i,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  Over
\(R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), the factorization

\[
T:R^m\longrightarrow R^3,
\qquad
\psi(u,v,s)=(u,v,sz,sw),
\qquad
A\bmod N=\psi\circ T
\]

is exact.

Surjectivity of \(T\) is equivalent to the maximal-minor ideal being the
unit ideal modulo both primes, hence to

\[
\gcd(N,\Delta_3(T))=1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Different minors may witness surjectivity at \(p\) and \(q\); taking their
integer gcd handles this correctly.  If all maximal minors vanish, the
natural convention \(\Delta_3(T)=0\) gives gcd \(N\), not a false factor.
A proper gcd in (3.1) is already a valid factor, while gcd \(N\) is merely
the non-surjective branch.

If \(\gcd(N,z,w)=1\), a Bezout relation modulo \(N\) shows that
\(s\mapsto(sz,sw)\) is injective.  Under (3.1),

\[
\operatorname{im}(A\bmod N)
=Re_1\oplus Re_2\oplus R(0,0,z,w)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Taking the full integer preimage gives

\[
\Lambda_{\rm out}(A,N)
=\mathbb Z^2\oplus
\bigl((z,w)\mathbb Z+N\mathbb Z^2\bigr).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The first two coordinate directions are saturated.  The last factor is a
rank-two output-side quotient; it is not the coefficient-side
\(N\mathbb Z^m+A^{\mathsf T}\mathbb Z^4\).  The candidate never transfers
rank-two metric conclusions to that rank-\(m\) lattice.

P30 supplies \(\gcd(N,z,w)=1\) when \(z^2+w^2\) is a unit modulo \(N\).
It supplies neither a common completion for independent calls nor (3.1).
P31 likewise does not supply those extra hypotheses.  The candidate states
both hypotheses rather than smuggling them in from the four-square source.

## 4. Cyclic normal form and singular cases

For \(C=(z,w)\ne0\), write

\[
g=\gcd(z,w)>0,
\qquad C=gC_0,
\qquad C_0=(z_0,w_0)
\]

with \(C_0\) primitive.  Extended Euclid gives a public
\(D\in\mathbb Z^2\) satisfying \(\det(C_0,D)=1\).  Thus every ambient
vector is uniquely \(aC_0+bD\).  If \(h=\gcd(g,N)\) and \(N_0=N/h\),
then \(\gcd(g/h,N/h)=1\), so

\[
N\mid g\det(C_0,v)
\iff
N_0\mid\det(C_0,v)
\iff
N_0\mid b.
\]

Therefore the determinant-congruence lattice has the exact form

\[
L_C=\mathbb ZC_0+N_0\mathbb ZD,
\qquad
\det L_C=N_0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

All singular branches are correct:

- \(h=1\) gives \(L_C=\mathbb ZC_0+N\mathbb ZD\);
- \(1<h<N\) exposes the proper factor \(h\);
- \(h=N\) makes the determinant condition vacuous and gives
  \(L_C=\mathbb Z^2\).

For \(C=0\), the separately stated answer \(L_C=\mathbb Z^2\) is also
correct.

The actual cyclic output lattice is

\[
O_C=C\mathbb Z+N\mathbb Z^2
=h\mathbb ZC_0+N\mathbb ZD,
\qquad \det O_C=hN.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus \(O_C=L_C\) exactly on the \(h=1\) branch used by (3.2).  In
particular, the candidate does not incorrectly identify the determinant
kernel with the output lattice in a singular branch.

Quarter rotation \(R(x,y)=(-y,x)\) turns determinant congruence into
dot-product congruence.  Directly, or by inclusion plus the determinant
check, this gives

\[
NO_C^*=R(L_C),
\qquad
NL_C^*=R(O_C).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The determinant checks are \(N/h\) and \(Nh\), respectively.  These
identities also hold in the \(C=0\) boundary, although no normalization is
then needed.

For a prime \(r\mid N\), if \(r\mid g\) the local determinant condition is
vacuous.  Otherwise

\[
L_{C,r}=\mathbb ZC_0+r\mathbb ZD.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence a singular local-rank mismatch is already visible in
\(\gcd(g,N)\).  The cheaper coordinate gcd branches for \(z\) and \(w\)
are also valid for squarefree \(N=pq\): after proper gcds have been removed,
each coordinate is either a unit or zero modulo \(N\), and residual
unimodularity prevents both from being zero.

## 5. The two scalar tests

On \(h=1\), an ambient vector \(v=aC_0+bD\) belongs to the local output
line exactly when

\[
v\in L_{C,r}\iff r\mid b,
\qquad
b=\det(C_0,v).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a public-lattice vector, write \(v=aC_0+NbD\).  Since
\((C_0,D)\) is a unimodular basis, it preserves the coordinate ideal:

\[
\gcd(N,v_1,v_2)
=\gcd(N,a,Nb)
=\gcd(N,a),
\qquad
a=\det(v,D).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the \(b\)-coordinate tests local output-module membership, which holds
at both primes for \(v\in L_C\), whereas the \(a\)-coordinate tests ordinary
coordinate divisibility.  A proper coordinate gcd is a public scalar-gcd
ticket, not an unread determinant scale.

The slice identities

\[
L_C\cap p\mathbb Z^2=pL_{C,q},
\qquad
L_C\cap q\mathbb Z^2=qL_{C,p}
\tag{5.3}
\]

follow by cancelling the other prime in the determinant congruence.  Hence
the minimum in the \(p\)-divisible slice is exactly
\(p\lambda_1(L_{C,q})\), and symmetrically at \(q\).  This identity does
not itself give a useful probability that such a slice wins.

## 6. Deterministic cyclic SVP and scaled dual

Put \(\ell=\lVert C_0\rVert_2\).  Since
\(\det(C_0,D)=1\), the component of \(D\) perpendicular to \(C_0\) has
length \(1/\ell\).  Therefore

\[
\lVert aC_0+NbD\rVert_2
\ge \frac{N|b|}{\ell}
\qquad(b\ne0).
\tag{6.1}
\]

As \(C_0\in L_C\), if \(\ell^2<N\) every vector with \(b\ne0\) is
strictly longer than \(C_0\), while the nonzero \(b=0\) vectors are its
integer multiples.  Thus the shortest-vector set is exactly
\(\{\pm C_0\}\), and (5.2) gives scalar \(\pm1\).  Strict inequality
handles ties correctly.

Projection onto \(C_0^\perp\) records the determinant coordinate divided by
\(\ell\).  Primitivity of \(C_0\) makes that coordinate surjective onto
\(\mathbb Z\), so

\[
\pi(\mathbb Z^2)=\ell^{-1}\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\pi(L_{C,r})=r\ell^{-1}\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\pi(L_C)=N\ell^{-1}\mathbb Z.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The faithful congruence quotient is rank one.

On \(h=1\), \(L_C=C_0\mathbb Z+N\mathbb Z^2\).  Primitivity makes
\(C_0\) a unimodular row modulo \(N\), so its dot-product kernel is the
cyclic line generated by \(RC_0\).  Hence

\[
NL_C^*
=\{u\in\mathbb Z^2:C_0\cdot u\equiv0\pmod N\}
=RC_0\mathbb Z+N\mathbb Z^2
=R(L_C).
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is an isometric rotation only for the rank-two cyclic quotient.  It
does not identify the original rank-\(m\) scaled dual with that quotient.

## 7. Fresh audit of the uniform-line SVP theorem

Assume here, exactly as the corrected candidate now does, that \(p<q\) are
distinct odd primes, \(q\le\kappa p\) for fixed \(\kappa\), and the
projective completion line is marginally uniform on

\[
\mathcal S_r
=\{[u:v]\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_r):u^2+v^2\ne0\},
\qquad
|\mathcal S_r|=r-\left(\frac{-1}{r}\right)
\]

at each prime.  No joint independence is assumed.

The two-dimensional Hermite bound gives

\[
\lambda_1(L_C)^2\le\gamma_2N,
\qquad \gamma_2=2/\sqrt3.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Suppose some shortest vector has proper coordinate gcd \(p\).  Then
\(v=py\) for \(y\in\mathbb Z^2\), and

\[
0<\lVert y\rVert^2
\le \gamma_2\frac qp
\le \gamma_2\kappa.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The corrected proof explicitly handles the projective zero case.  If
\(y\equiv0\pmod q\), both coordinates of \(v=py\) are divisible by
\(pq=N\), contradicting \(\gcd(N,v_1,v_2)=p\).  Thus \(y\bmod q\) is
nonzero.  Since \(v\in L_C\),

\[
q\mid\det(C_0,y),
\]

and \(g\) is a unit modulo \(q\) on \(h=1\), so

\[
[C\bmod q]=[C_0\bmod q]=[y\bmod q].
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let \(k\) be the integer coordinate gcd of \(y\) and put \(y=ky_0\) with
\(y_0\) primitive.  Because \(y\bmod q\ne0\), \(q\nmid k\); hence
division preserves both a nonzero reduction and its projective line.
Moreover \(\lVert y_0\rVert\le\lVert y\rVert\).

Define \(B_\kappa\) as the number of primitive unoriented integer directions
\(\mathbb Zy_0\) with a primitive representative of squared norm at most
\(\gamma_2\kappa\).  This is a finite constant independent of \(p,q,N\).
For a fixed \(q\), distinct such integer directions may reduce to the same
projective line; that only decreases the supported union.  A reduction that
is isotropic cannot equal the completion line, because the latter lies in
\(\mathcal S_q\).  Primitive representatives never reduce to the zero
vector modulo any prime.  Therefore marginal uniformity alone yields

\[
\Pr(\exists\hbox{ shortest }v:
\gcd(N,v_1,v_2)=p)
\le \frac{B_\kappa}{|\mathcal S_q|}
=O(1/q).
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is an existential event over the entire shortest-vector set, so no
tie-breaking convention is hidden.  The argument is valid for the smallest
odd primes as well: \(|\mathcal S_r|=r\pm1>0\), and the fixed implicit
constant absorbs finitely many small cases.

The symmetric argument for coordinate gcd \(q\) uses reduction modulo
\(p\) and gives \(O(1/p)\).  Since \(N=pq\), these are the only proper
coordinate gcds.  Hence

\[
\Pr(\exists\hbox{ factor-revealing shortest vector})
=O(1/p+1/q).
\tag{7.5}
\]

This theorem uses only the corresponding marginal at each prime, not
independence of the two completion lines.

The candidate correctly refuses to infer this premise from P30.  P30 proves
uniform quaternion row and image lines after conditioning on completion
data; it does not prove that \([(z,w)]\) is uniform.  Thus (7.5) is a
conditional obstruction for a uniform completion-line model, not a theorem
about every residual-only completion.

## 8. Cyclic CVP

Write a public target uniquely as \(t_0=AC_0+BD\).  For
\(v=aC_0+NbD\in L_C\),

\[
t_0-v=(A-a)C_0+(B-Nb)D.
\]

Equation (5.1) gives

\[
t_0-v\in L_{C,r}
\iff r\mid B
\iff t_0\in L_{C,r}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is independent of which exact or approximate closest vector is
selected.  If \(t_0\) is uniform modulo \(N\), unimodularity of
\((C_0,D)\) makes \(B\) uniform modulo \(N\), and the exact one-local
probability is

\[
\frac1p+\frac1q-\frac2N.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Rotation transfers (8.1)--(8.2) to \(NL_C^*\).  It does not transfer them
to the original rank-\(m\) scaled dual.  Nor do these equations control the
coordinate gcd of the selected closest lattice vector.  Biased targets
remain open, with the precise burden of manufacturing a public scalar
divisible by exactly one hidden prime.

## 9. Bit complexity and scope

The effectiveness claims are proportionate to the mathematics:

- Polynomial-size integer Smith form, including polynomial-bit
  transformation matrices, and the stated gcds are computable in
  deterministic polynomial bit complexity.
- Bases of \(E_{\rm out}\) and \(E_{\rm dual}\) are obtained from the
  relevant columns of \(U^{-1}\) and \(V^{-\mathsf T}\).  Their dimensions
  are \(u\), and their orthogonal complements have dimensions \(t-u\) and
  \(d-u\).
- Exact projection matrices
  \(I-B(B^{\mathsf T}B)^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}\), quotient Gram matrices, and
  squared covolumes have rational descriptions of polynomial bit length
  and are computable by exact polynomial-time linear algebra.
- Extended Euclid constructs \(C_0,D\) with polynomial-bit entries.  The
  basis \((C_0,ND)\) has polynomial bit length.
- Exact two-dimensional Gauss reduction and fixed-dimensional CVP are
  polynomial in that bit length.  No growing-dimensional SVP/CVP
  polynomial-time claim is made.

The candidate proves no factoring algorithm and no general lattice lower
bound.  In particular, it leaves open metric selection and coordinate-gcd
coupling in the full lattices, biased completion or target distributions,
batches without a shared completion or surjective \(T\), nonlinear
combinations, and constructions whose informative rank genuinely grows.

## 10. Final assessment

The exact result that survives this re-audit is narrow but stronger than the
first audited version:

1. Public output and scaled-dual lattices are CRT intersections, and their
   local-membership cosets cannot be altered by public-lattice CVP
   subtraction.
2. Smith form either exposes a factor or leaves common public primitive
   exact directions whose faithful output and transpose-side quotients are
   exact \(p,q,N\) scalings of one public lattice.
3. Under the explicit fixed-batch hypotheses, the output quotient is the
   cyclic rank-two lattice; its exact direction leaves a rank-one faithful
   quotient, and its scaled dual is only a rotation.
4. Uniform targets and, under the additional uniform-completion-line
   premise, factor-revealing shortest vectors have exponentially sparse
   one-factor events on balanced semiprimes.

Every retained disclaimer is necessary.  The proof does not cross from the
faithful quotient to the full-lattice optimizer, nor from P30's conditional
row/image uniformity to completion-line uniformity.  Subject to those stated
boundaries, the corrected F27 artifact merits a fresh **PASS**.
