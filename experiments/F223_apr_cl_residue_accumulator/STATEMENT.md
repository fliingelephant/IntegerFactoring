# F223 candidate - APR/CL residue accumulation and its Las Vegas source boundary

## Status

This is a frozen, self-audited proof candidate with one post-hoc exact
witness check. It is not a factoring algorithm.

The positive result is conditional. A compatible APR/CL orbit certificate
can enlarge the beta-two modulus and feed the known-residue factoring
terminal. The negative results show that three simple ways to obtain this
certificate do not give an all-input inverse-QP progress law:

1. a fixed finite bank of auxiliary primes;
2. a generic independent-residue heuristic used as if it were an
   all-input theorem; and
3. a uniform rational anchor at the primary prime 2.

Throughout, numerical QP means

\[
2^{(\log n)^{O(1)}},
\qquad n=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil.
\]

## Theorem A - exact local orbit equivalence

Let `q` be a prime, let `q` not divide `N`, and let `r` be a unit modulo
`q`. Put

\[
G_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times,
\qquad h_q=\operatorname{ord}_q(N).
\]

The following statements are equivalent.

1. There is an integer `i` such that
   \[
   r\equiv N^i\pmod q.
   \tag{A1}
   \]
2. The residue of `r` belongs to the cyclic orbit generated by `N`:
   \[
   r\bmod q\in\langle N\bmod q\rangle.
   \tag{A2}
   \]
3. There is one integer `i` such that, for every multiplicative character
   `chi` of `G_q`,
   \[
   \chi(r)=\chi(N)^i.
   \tag{A3}
   \]

When these statements hold, the complete set of exponents is one residue
class modulo `h_q`.

For a finite prime bank `Q`, write this class as

\[
i\equiv i_q(r)\pmod {h_q}.
\tag{A4}
\]

There is one exponent that works for every `q` in `Q` if and only if these
classes satisfy the generalized-CRT compatibility conditions

\[
i_q(r)\equiv i_{q'}(r)
\pmod{\gcd(h_q,h_{q'})}
\qquad(q,q'\in Q).
\tag{A5}
\]

Thus independent local orbit membership is not enough. The local
exponents must be compatible.

## Theorem B - what the Cohen-Lenstra compatibility supplies

Use the hypotheses and notation of Cohen-Lenstra Theorems 6.3 and 7.8.
In particular, let `t,S` be their auxiliary integers, and let `r` be any
prime divisor of the tested integer `N`.

For each primary prime `p` dividing `t`, condition (6.4) supplies a shared
`p`-adic exponent

\[
\ell_p(r)\in\mathbb Z_p.
\tag{B1}
\]

If the relevant Gauss-sum or Jacobi-sum tests pass, Theorem 7.8 gives

\[
\chi(r)=\chi(N)^{\ell_p(r)}
\tag{B2}
\]

for every tested character `chi` of `p`-power order. The same
`ell_p(r)` is used for every auxiliary prime whose character order has
that primary support. Cohen-Lenstra Theorem 6.3 then combines the primary
exponents and concludes that

\[
\boxed{r\equiv N^i\pmod S}
\tag{B3}

for one `i` in a public finite exponent set, normally a complete residue
system modulo `t`.

Equation (B3) is not justified if each auxiliary prime is allowed to choose
an unrelated exponent. The shared primary exponents are the compatibility
mechanism.

## Theorem C - conditional APR/CL plus beta-two terminal

Assume

\[
N=pq,\qquad p<q<2p,
\tag{C1}
\]

where `p,q` are distinct odd primes. Suppose the following public,
certified data are available.

1. An exact low-bit residue, with `tau>=1`,
   \[
   p\equiv b\pmod {2^\tau}.
   \tag{C2}
   \]
2. A positive integer `M` such that
   \[
   M\mid r-1
   \qquad\text{for every prime }r\mid N.
   \tag{C3}
   \]
3. A positive integer `S` coprime to `N` and a public set `I` such that
   \[
   |I|\le\operatorname{QP}(n)
   \tag{C4}
   \]
   and, for every prime `r` dividing `N`, some `i` in `I` satisfies
   \[
   r\equiv N^i\pmod S.
   \tag{C5}
   \]

The values `S` and `i` are explicitly encoded with numerical-QP bit
length, and `I` can be enumerated in numerical-QP time.

Put

\[
L_0=\operatorname{lcm}(2^\tau,M),
\qquad
L=\operatorname{lcm}(L_0,S).
\tag{C6}
\]

The first two certificates determine the true residue

\[
p\equiv s_0\pmod {L_0}
\tag{C7}
\]

by generalized CRT. For each `i` in `I`, test compatibility of

\[
x\equiv s_0\pmod {L_0},
\qquad
x\equiv N^i\pmod S.
\tag{C8}

For every compatible `i`, compute the resulting residue `s_i mod L`.
At least one retained residue is the true residue of `p` modulo `L`.

Fix a positive numerical-QP function `A(n)`. If

\[
\boxed{L\ge \frac{N^{1/4}}{A(n)},}
\tag{C9}

then enumerating `I`, screening `gcd(s_i,N)`, and applying the
Gao-Feng-Hu-Pan known-residue terminal to the surviving residues factors
`N` in numerical-QP bit complexity.

This theorem needs no single element whose exact order is `M`. The value
`M` can be an lcm of independently certified common primary orders.

## Theorem D - a fixed auxiliary-prime bank has an infinite balanced obstruction

Let `Q` be any fixed finite set of rational primes. Put

\[
R=\prod_{\substack{q\in Q\\q>2}}q,
\tag{D1}
\]

with the empty product equal to 1.

There is a number `X_0(Q)` such that, for every real `X >= X_0(Q)`, the
interval `(X,2X)` contains two distinct primes `u<v` satisfying

\[
u\equiv v\equiv-1\pmod R.
\tag{D2}
\]

Consequently `u<v<2u`, so `N=uv` is a balanced squarefree semiprime. For
every odd `q` in `Q`,

\[
N\equiv1\pmod q,
\qquad
u\equiv v\equiv-1\pmod q.
\tag{D3}

Hence neither hidden factor belongs to the local orbit generated by `N`:

\[
u,v\notin\langle N\rangle\pmod q.
\tag{D4}

At `q=2`, the unit group is the singleton, so the local orbit condition is
vacuous. Therefore a fixed finite prime bank can be forced to contribute
no odd local modulus at all.

The quantifiers are important. The bank is fixed before the pair is
chosen. This theorem does not cover a bank that depends on `N`, an
unbounded adaptive bank, or a random bank refreshed after seeing `N`.

## Theorem E - exact generic-residue tail model

This theorem is a probability model, not an assertion about all integers.

Fix a nonempty finite prime bank `Q`, nonzero public residues `a_q mod q`,
and one exponent `i`. The intended orbit model takes `a_q=N mod q`, but
the calculation does not need that specialization. Let the residues `U_q`
be independent and uniform in
`(Z/qZ)^times`. Define

\[
I_{q,i}=\mathbf1_{\{U_q=a_q^i\}},
\qquad
K_i=\sum_{q\in Q} I_{q,i},
\qquad
W_i=\sum_{q\in Q}(\log q)I_{q,i}.
\tag{E1}

Then

\[
\Pr(I_{q,i}=1)=\frac1{q-1},
\tag{E2}

the indicators are independent, and for every `lambda>0`,

\[
\Pr(K_i\ge k)
\le
e^{-\lambda k}
\prod_{q\in Q}
\left(1+\frac{e^\lambda-1}{q-1}\right).
\tag{E3}

Writing

\[
\mu=\sum_{q\in Q}\frac1{q-1},
\tag{E4}

gives the standard consequence

\[
\Pr(K_i\ge k)\le\left(\frac{e\mu}{k}\right)^k
\qquad(k\ge\mu).
\tag{E5}

Equivalently, for the accepted product

\[
P_i=\prod_{q\in Q}q^{I_{q,i}},
\tag{E6}

every `Y>1` satisfies the exact exponential-Markov bound

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(P_i\ge Y)
\le
\inf_{\lambda>0}
Y^{-\lambda}
\prod_{q\in Q}
\left(1+\frac{q^\lambda-1}{q-1}\right).}
\tag{E7}

For a public set of `T` candidate exponents, a union bound multiplies the
right sides by at most `T`. No independence between different exponents is
claimed.

## Theorem F - the primary-prime-2 uniform-anchor law is sparse

Let

\[
N=uv
\tag{F1}

be a squarefree semiprime with distinct odd primes `u,v`. Put

\[
E=\frac{N-1}{2},
\qquad
d=\gcd(u-1,v-1),
\tag{F2}

and draw `a` uniformly from `(Z/NZ)^times`. Define

\[
g_u=\gcd(E,u-1),
\qquad
g_v=\gcd(E,v-1).
\tag{F3}

The exact local return-to-one probabilities are

\[
\alpha_u=\Pr(a^E=1\bmod u)=\frac{g_u}{u-1},
\qquad
\alpha_v=\frac{g_v}{v-1}.
\tag{F4}

The two local events are independent. Thus

\[
\Pr(\text{at least one local return to }1)
=\alpha_u+\alpha_v-\alpha_u\alpha_v,
\tag{F5}

while the probability that exactly one component returns to 1, and hence
`gcd(a^E-1,N)` is proper, is

\[
\alpha_u+\alpha_v-2\alpha_u\alpha_v.
\tag{F6}

For the wider sign gate, put

\[
\delta_u=
\mathbf1_{\{g_u\mid (u-1)/2\}},
\qquad
\delta_v=
\mathbf1_{\{g_v\mid (v-1)/2\}}.
\tag{F7}

Then

\[
\Pr(a^E\in\{1,-1\}\bmod u)
=\frac{g_u(1+\delta_u)}{u-1}
\le\frac{2g_u}{u-1},
\tag{F8}

and symmetrically at `v`.

Always

\[
g_u\mid d,
\qquad
g_v\mid d,
\qquad
d\mid v-u.
\tag{F9}

Therefore, on any balanced bounded-gap family with `v-u<=H`, the
probability that either local component enters the sign gate is at most

\[
\frac{2H}{u-1}+\frac{2H}{v-1}
=2^{-n/2+O_H(1)}.
\tag{F10}

If an additional congruence setup proves `g_u=g_v=d`, then (F5) becomes

\[
\frac d{u-1}+\frac d{v-1}
-\frac{d^2}{(u-1)(v-1)}.
\tag{F11}

No general equality `g_u=g_v=d` is claimed.

## Remaining core requirement

F223 does not prove an inverse-QP progress law. A complete Las Vegas route
still needs a source which, on every nonterminal integer and conditional on
the full prior history, has inverse-QP probability of doing at least one of
the following:

1. return a verified proper factor;
2. enlarge `M` by a certified common primary order;
3. enlarge the compatible APR/CL orbit modulus `S`; or
4. certify new low bits of the target factor.

An APR/CL identity failure certifies compositeness. It does not in general
return a rational zero divisor. The original APR paper explicitly notes
that a composite which passes all extraction tests is factored in its final
consolidation stage, but that composites are normally rejected earlier.
That distinction is the unresolved Las Vegas source problem.
