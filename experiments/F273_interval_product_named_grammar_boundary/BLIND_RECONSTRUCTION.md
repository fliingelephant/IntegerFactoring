## 1. Child-product coordinate ring

Let

`phi_e : A[e_0,o_0,z] -> A[o_0,z]`

be evaluation at `e_0=0`. Write

`F = sum_i e_0^i f_i(o_0,z)`.

Then `phi_e(F)=f_0`, so `ker(phi_e)=(e_0)`. This proves (2). The analogous
evaluation at `o_0=0` has kernel `(o_0)`.

If both restrictions in (3) vanish, write `F=e_0G`. The second restriction
gives

`e_0 G(e_0,0,z)=0`.

The ring `A[e_0,z]` is a domain, so `G(e_0,0,z)=0`. Hence `o_0` divides `G`,
and `e_0o_0` divides `F`. Thus Theorem 1 is valid. The domain hypothesis is
sufficient; characteristic zero is not needed for this algebraic result.

For `F=G/H`, restriction to an axis is valid only when the restriction of
`H` is a nonzero element of that axis's polynomial ring. If `G/H` is then
zero in the corresponding fraction field, the restricted numerator is zero.
The polynomial argument applies to `G`. This is an identity on a generic
axis, not a conclusion from finitely many axis samples. A presentation whose
denominator restricts to zero is outside the claim, as stated.

### Corollary 1.1

Let `U` be the union of the leaf sets used by the `K` summaries. Then
`|U| <= Kq_0`. An expression whose leaf dependence enters only through those
summaries belongs to the ring, or fraction field if division is allowed,
generated by the leaves in `U`. If some `y_j` is absent from `U`, that
expression is independent of `y_j`, while `product_j y_j` has degree one in
`y_j` with a nonzero coefficient. Algebraic independence makes equality
impossible. Therefore every leaf lies in `U`, and

`M <= |U| <= Kq_0`.

The corollary is valid only for formal independent leaves and for the stated
exclusive access through the summaries. It gives no bound after imposing
`y_j=x+j` or any other relations.

## 2. Smith determinantal divisors

Over a field, rank at least `k` is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero
`k`-minor. Rank `m-1` modulo `p` therefore gives a minor not divisible by `p`
for every `k<m`. Full rank modulo `q` gives a minor not divisible by `q` for
every `k<=m`. Consequently neither prime divides `Delta_k` for `k<m`.

For `k=m`, the only minor is the determinant. It is zero modulo `p` and
nonzero modulo `q`. Since `N=pq`, this proves (8), including `k=0`. Full rank
modulo `q` also makes the integer determinant nonzero, so the ordinary Smith
invariants exist with

`d_k = Delta_k/Delta_(k-1)`.

The first `p`-divisible invariant is `d_m`, exactly as claimed.

For the balanced semiprime,

`p <= B < q` and `B < 2p`.

The first two inequalities follow from `p < sqrt(pq) < q`; the last follows
from `q<2p`. Thus `1,...,B` contains exactly one multiple of `p` and no
multiple of `q`. The diagonal matrix has the stated rank profile, and

`Delta_B = |det(diag(1,...,B))| = B!`.

The subsequent factorial-gate interpretation does not follow for Smith
normal form. The nonzero `(B-1)`-minors are `B!/i`, for `1<=i<=B`, and

`Delta_(B-1) = gcd_i(B!/i) = B!/lcm(1,...,B)`.

Hence the last Smith invariant is

`d_B = Delta_B/Delta_(B-1) = lcm(1,...,B)`.

It is itself factor-bearing:

`gcd(d_B,N)=p`,

because `p<=B<q`. But it is an lcm gate. The statement proves no equivalence
between evaluating this lcm and evaluating `B!`. The general rank-defect
theorem remains valid; only the claimed classification of the diagonal Smith
route is overstrong.

## 3. Full derivative resultant

The roots of `P_m` are `-1,...,-m`. For a monic polynomial with distinct
roots, the unsigned resultant with its derivative is the product of squared
pairwise root differences. Therefore

`D_m = product_(1<=i<j<=m) (j-i)^2`.

A difference `d` occurs for exactly `m-d` pairs. This gives the first product
in (13). In `product_(k=1)^(m-1) k!`, the factor `d` occurs in the factorials
`d!,...,(m-1)!`, again `m-d` times. Squaring proves the second product.
Adding the new factor `m!` to the superfactorial proves (14).

Also,

`log D_m = 2 sum_(d=1)^(m-1) (m-d) log d`.

The upper bound is `O(m^2 log(m+1))`. For large `m`, restricting the sum to
`m/4 <= d <= m/2` gives `Omega(m^2 log m)`. The finitely many small values,
including `D_1=1`, are absorbed by constants. Thus (15) holds for the usual
positive-integer bit length.

On the balanced promise, `p<=B<q` and `B<2p`. If `B=p`, then `gcd(B,N)=p`.
If that preliminary gcd is unresolved, necessarily `B>p`, hence
`p<=B-1`. The product for `D_B` contains a factor `p`, while every underlying
difference is less than `B<q`. Thus `p` divides `D_B` and `q` does not, which
proves (16). This proves factor-bearing status only. It supplies no fast
evaluator.

## 4. Literal cross-resultant closure

The roots of the two monic polynomials in (18) give

`R_c(m) = product_(i,j) (cm+j-i)`.

Every factor is positive because its minimum is `(c-1)m+1`.

Split each length-`2m` polynomial into its two consecutive length-`m`
blocks. Multiplicativity of the resultant produces four block pairs. Their
relative offsets are `2c-1`, `2c`, `2c`, and `2c+1`. This proves (19).

Let `C_l` be the offsets after `l` expansions, with `C_0={1}`. Then

`C_(l+1) = union_(c in C_l) {2c-1,2c,2c+1}`.

Induction gives

`C_l={1,...,2^(l+1)-1}`.

Thus the base level after `t` expansions has exactly `2^(t+1)-1` distinct
offsets. If all levels are counted as distinct states `(length,offset)`, the
exact DAG count is

`sum_(l=0)^t (2^(l+1)-1) = 2^(t+2)-t-3`.

Either count is `Theta(2^t)`. Literal positive-factor expansion has no
cancellation that could remove a reachable base state.

For telescoping, fix `i` and multiply over `j`:

`product_(j=0)^(m-1) (cm+j-i) = ((c+1)m-1-i)!/(cm-1-i)!`.

Multiplying over `i=0,...,m-1` and grouping consecutive factorials gives

`R_c(m) = [S((c+1)m)/S(cm)] / [S(cm)/S((c-1)m)]`,

which is (22). The convention `S(0)=1` handles `c=1`; the omitted `0!` is
one. The root-difference formula already proved gives `D_m=S(m)^2`, and the
definition gives `S(m+1)/S(m)=m!`. Thus (23) holds.

Equation (22) is an integer identity before reduction. It cannot be evaluated
modulo a composite modulus by inverting `S(cm)` unless that residue is a
unit. The warning about modular inversion is correct.

On the unresolved balanced branch, `B>p` and `B<q`. The factor `p!` occurs in
`S(B)`, so `p` divides `S(B)`. All integer factors used in `S(B)` are below
`q`, so `q` does not divide it. This proves (24).

## 5. Literal resource consequence

Put `L=M/q_0=2^t`. A fixed quasipolynomial satisfies
`log_2 Q(n)=o(n)`. From `1<=q_0<=Q(n)` and `M=2^Theta(n)`,

`log_2 L = log_2 M-log_2 q_0 = Theta(n)`,

so (25) follows.

For products of two monic polynomials,

`Disc(fg)=Disc(f)Disc(g)Res(f,g)^2`

after taking absolute values. Splitting `P_(2m)` into its two translated
length-`m` blocks proves (26).

In the expansion of `D_M`, the term `R_1(M/2)` alone expands to the base
offsets `1,...,L-1`. All levels together contain only `O(L)` literal states.
Hence the number of required literal cross-resultant states is `Theta(L)`.
If a residue modulo an `n`-bit modulus is stored for every offset, the stated
storage is `Theta(Ln)` bits. A literal traversal performs at least one state
operation for each distinct state, so streaming costs `Omega(L)` operations.
These conclusions require the stated literal grammar; algebraic compression
or a primitive remote evaluator is outside them.
