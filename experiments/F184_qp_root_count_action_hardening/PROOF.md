# Proof of the F184 root-count action-hardening theorem

## 1. One exact filter

Fix a stage \(t\), a hidden component \(R_j\), and write

\[
g_{t,j}=\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(w_t).
\]

For a prime power \(\ell^e\parallel g_{t,j}\), the order-of-a-power formula
gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z_{t,a})
=\frac{g_{t,j}}{\gcd(g_{t,j},C_a^n)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently the complete \(\ell^e\)-primary part is deleted exactly when
\(\ell\mid C_a\). Define the monic integer polynomial

\[
F_K(X)=X\prod_{k=1}^K(X^k-1).
\tag{2}
\]

Then \(C_a=F_K(a)\), and

\[
\deg F_K=1+\sum_{k=1}^Kk=D.
\tag{3}
\]

For a prime \(\ell\), the condition \(\ell\mid C_a\) is equivalent to

\[
a\equiv0\pmod\ell
\quad\text{or}\quad
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a)\le K.
\tag{4}
\]

The second alternative is read only when \(a\) is a unit modulo \(\ell\).

## 2. A stage cannot be globally extinct on every candidate

Inductively, every prime divisor of every \(g_{t,j}\) is a prime divisor of
some \(g_{0,j}\), so it exceeds \(T\). Every candidate in every block is at
most \(1+M(D+1)<T\). In particular, the \(D+1\) consecutive candidates in
one block are distinct modulo every such prime.

Assume that no proper factor occurs at stage \(t\), and suppose for
contradiction that

\[
H_{t,a}=N
\qquad(a\in\mathcal I_t).
\tag{5}
\]

The current local orders are nontrivial. Fix one component \(j\) and one
prime \(\ell\mid g_{t,j}\). Equation (5) says that
\(z_{t,a}=1\) modulo \(R_j\) for every candidate. By (1),
\(g_{t,j}\mid C_a^n\), hence

\[
F_K(a)=0\pmod\ell
\qquad(a\in\mathcal I_t).
\tag{6}
\]

This gives \(D+1\) distinct roots of the nonzero monic degree-\(D\)
polynomial \(F_K\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), a contradiction. Therefore at
least one candidate has \(H_{t,a}\ne N\). If it is proper, the algorithm
returns a factor. Otherwise it equals one, proving (7) of the statement.

## 3. Prime powers and preservation of all earlier action conditions

Every local order in the construction divides an initial \(g_{0,j}\), so
it remains coprime to the rational prime below \(R_j\). Reduction from
\((\mathbb Z/R_j\mathbb Z)^\times\) to the corresponding prime field has a
prime-power kernel and is injective on the subgroup generated by
\(z_{t,a}\). Hence

\[
z_{t,a}\equiv1\pmod {p_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z_{t,a}\equiv1\pmod {R_j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ord}_{R_j}(z_{t,a})=1.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus \(H_{t,a}=1\) makes every next local order nontrivial even for repeated
prime factors of \(N\).

If \(\ell\) survives in \(g_{t+1,j}\), equation (1) gives
\(\ell\nmid C_{a_t}\). Equation (4) therefore gives

\[
\ell\nmid a_t,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a_t)>K.
\tag{8}
\]

Later stages only delete more primary parts. Hence every prime in the final
order was present after every earlier stage, so (8) holds for all selected
bases. Roughness and coprimality are inherited from the initial orders.

The blocks in (5) are disjoint as integer intervals. Their union lies below
\(T\), so the selected bases are distinct both as public integers and modulo
every surviving prime.

## 4. Uniform quasipolynomial cost

The largest candidate satisfies

\[
a\le1+M(D+1)=2^{(\log n)^{O(1)}}.
\tag{9}
\]

For each candidate,

\[
\log_2 C_a
\le D\log_2a
=2^{(\log n)^{O(1)}}.
\tag{10}
\]

The explicit exponent \(C_a^n\) has bit length

\[
n\log_2 C_a=2^{(\log n)^{O(1)}}.
\tag{11}
\]

There are exactly \(M(D+1)=2^{(\log n)^{O(1)}}\) candidate evaluations.
Each \(C_a\) is built from \(K\) ordinary integer powers and products, and
each stage uses binary modular powering and one gcd on \(n\)-bit residues.
All intermediate integers have the bit lengths in (9)--(11). Products and
sums of fixed numerical quasipolynomial bounds remain numerical
quasipolynomial bounds. Therefore the total construction and all screens
have uniform deterministic quasipolynomial bit cost.

No auxiliary integer is factored, and no hidden order or hidden prime is
used.

## 5. Relation to the closest prior routes

F180 filters two fixed actions and uses small resultants to analyze double
extinction. F182 uses two fixed QP-size shift menus, but its action cap is
polylogarithmic because the roughness threshold must dominate every
cross-resultant. F183 factors small-base annihilators and therefore also
keeps a polylogarithmic action cap.

F184 differs materially. It never tries to decode a globally extinct
annihilator. It tests one block of \(D+1\) candidates and uses the root bound
for the single degree-\(D\) polynomial (2) to prove that one candidate must
survive or factor. This permits both \(K\) and the number \(M\) of retained
actions to be arbitrary fixed numerical quasipolynomial functions. The price
is that the selected actions are adaptive rather than a prescribed full
menu, and no common-order state is recovered from the skipped identity
branches.
