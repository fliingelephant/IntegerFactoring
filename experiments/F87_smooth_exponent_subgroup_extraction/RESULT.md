# F87 — punctured powers make every smooth-exponent separator enumerable

**Status:** proof-only candidate. No research computation was run. This is a
conditional deterministic subgroup decoder for a squarefree semiprime, not a
source of a factor-bearing subgroup or an all-input factoring algorithm.

## 1. Material difference

P87 gives a punctured power bank for one generator with unequal local orders.
F86 handles a pure phase extension by powering and enumerating the whole
subgroup. The present result unifies both branches.

For any public generated subgroup modulo a squarefree semiprime, if the
subgroup already contains a positive separator and its exponent has bounded
full prime-power components, one punctured lcm power preserves a separator
while shrinking the entire image to at most \(B^2\) elements. A capped
factor-free enumeration is then complete. No order/phase branch detector,
local order, or cancellation word is needed.

## 2. Setup

Let

\[
N=pq
\]

for distinct odd primes, and let \(K\) be a subgroup of

\[
(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong
\mathbb F_p^\times\times\mathbb F_q^\times
\]

given by a polynomial-size public generator list. Put

\[
\lambda=\exp(K).
\]

A positive separator is an element \(x\in K\) with exactly one local
coordinate equal to one. It gives a proper public gcd \(\gcd(x-1,N)\).

For a positive integer \(a\), define

\[
\sigma(a)=\max_{\ell^e\mid a}\ell^e,
\qquad
\sigma(1)=1.
\]

Let

\[
M_B=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)
\]

and define

\[
\mathcal E_B
=
\{M_B\}
\cup
\left\{
\frac{M_B}{\ell^j}:
\ell\le B\text{ prime},\
1\le j\le\lfloor\log_\ell B\rfloor
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(E\ge1\), write

\[
K^E=\{y^E:y\in K\}.
\]

## 3. Small-image separator theorem

### Theorem 1

Assume that \(K\) contains a positive separator and

\[
\boxed{\sigma(\lambda)\le B.}
\tag{2}
\]

Then some \(E\in\mathcal E_B\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{|K^E|\le B^2}
\tag{3}
\]

and \(K^E\) still contains a positive separator.

More precisely, choose a separator \(x\in K\), a prime
\(\ell\mid\operatorname{ord}(x)\), and put

\[
C=v_\ell(\operatorname{ord}(x)),
\qquad
H=v_\ell(\lambda),
\qquad
e=v_\ell(M_B).
\]

Then

\[
E=\frac{M_B}{\ell^{e-C+1}}
\tag{4}
\]

belongs to the bank, \(x^E\) is a separator of exact order \(\ell\), and
the group \(K^E\) is an \(\ell\)-group of exponent at most

\[
\ell^{H-C+1}\le B.
\tag{5}
\]

### Proof

Condition (2) is equivalent to \(\lambda\mid M_B\). Hence

\[
1\le C\le H\le e,
\]

so the puncture in (4) is one of the declared bank exponents and

\[
v_\ell(E)=C-1.
\]

For every prime \(r\ne\ell\), the exponent \(E\) contains the full
\(r\)-primary part of \(\lambda\). Thus powering by \(E\) kills every
non-\(\ell\) Sylow component of \(K\). On the \(\ell\)-primary component,
the remaining coprime part of \(E\) is an automorphism and the factor
\(\ell^{C-1}\) leaves image exponent at most
\(\ell^{H-C+1}\).

The same calculation on \(x\) kills all non-\(\ell\) components of its
order and reduces its \(\ell\)-primary order from \(\ell^C\) to \(\ell\).
Powering preserves its identity coordinate, so \(x^E\) remains a positive
separator.

It remains to bound the image size. Each \(\ell\)-Sylow subgroup of
\(\mathbb F_p^\times\) and \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) is cyclic. Therefore an
\(\ell\)-subgroup of their product has generator rank at most two. A rank-at-
most-two abelian \(\ell\)-group of exponent at most
\(\ell^{H-C+1}\) has order at most

\[
\ell^{2(H-C+1)}.
\]

Since the full prime power \(\ell^H\) divides \(\lambda\), condition (2)
gives \(\ell^H\le B\). Hence

\[
|K^E|
\le
\ell^{2(H-C+1)}
\le
\ell^{2H}
\le B^2.
\]

This proves (3). \(\square\)

## 4. Deterministic capped enumeration

The theorem gives a public algorithm that does not know the successful
separator, prime, order, or exponent.

For every \(E\in\mathcal E_B\):

1. raise each public generator of \(K\) to \(E\) modulo \(N\);
2. enumerate the subgroup generated by these powered residues by breadth-
   first closure under multiplication by the powered generators;
3. stop this exponent after more than \(B^2\) distinct residues appear;
4. for each residue \(y\) found, compute \(\gcd(y-1,N)\).

For the exponent (4), the enumeration completes within the cap and visits
the separator \(x^E\). Every gcd output is verified before return. For a
wrong exponent, the cap bounds the work even if its image subgroup is
exponentially large.

The bank has at most \(B+1\) exponents. Each exponent has
\(O(B\log B)\) bits. With a polynomial-size public generator list and
\(B=\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\), every modular power, multiplication,
equality lookup, and gcd has polynomial bit complexity. At most
\(O(B^3)\) group elements are inserted across the bank, up to the public
generator factor. Storage is polynomial. The procedure is deterministic and
factor-free.

Thus (2), together with separator existence, is a complete executable
condition for polynomial-time factor extraction from the public subgroup.

## 5. Order and phase are both covered

The theorem does not inspect how the separator entered \(K\).

- In an unequal-order branch, a power of one generator can already be a
  separator. The theorem isolates a bounded primary image containing it.
- In an equal-order phase branch, no pure power of the new block need work.
  The theorem powers every public generator and enumerates their small joint
  image, so mixed cancellation appears automatically.

For the P88 state

\[
N=2047,
\qquad
K=\langle11,2\rangle,
\]

one has \(\lambda=22\) and \(\sigma(\lambda)=11\). With \(B=11\), the
bank member \(E=2520\) produces a group of order 121 containing 20 positive
separators, as in F86.

For the P86/P87 state at \(N=4033\), the single-base power decoder gives a
smaller conditional bound and remains preferable. The present theorem is
broader but assumes bounded full prime-power components for the exponent of
the entire generated subgroup.

## 6. Scope and remaining source problem

This theorem is a decoder for a supplied public subgroup. It does not prove
that canonical feedback creates a separator, nor that the resulting subgroup
has \(\sigma(\lambda)\le\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\) on every input.

The rank-two size bound uses the two-field CRT of a squarefree semiprime. For
many prime-power components, the analogous primary image can have larger
rank and need not have polynomial size. No reduction from arbitrary
composites to this promise is supplied.

The result therefore removes both the hidden branch detector and the hidden
mixed-word selector under the smooth-exponent condition. The remaining
feedback question is source-side: can bare \(N\) create a factor-bearing
public subgroup with bounded full prime-power exponent on every input, or
with inverse-polynomial probability? No all-input law, complete factoring
algorithm, lower bound, or literature novelty is proved.
