## 3. Exact-target drift audit

The repaired potential is

\[
\Phi(L)=
\max\left\{0,
\left\lceil\log_2(J_{\rm G}/L)\right\rceil
\right\}.
\]

1. It is zero exactly at the actual integer threshold.
2. A strict lcm update has \(L'/L\ge2\), so the ceiling identity
   \(\lceil x-1\rceil=\lceil x\rceil-1\) proves a decrement of at least
   one while nonterminal.
3. Crossing \(J_{\rm G}\) sets the potential to zero and also decreases it
   by at least one.
4. Before termination, the potential is between one and
   \(\lceil\log_2J_{\rm G}\rceil=O(n)\).
5. If \(J_{\rm G}=1\), there is no nonterminal history, so the drift
   hypothesis is vacuous.
6. Stopped telescoping gives
   \(\mathbb E\tau\le\Phi_0Q(n)=O(nQ(n))\), hence almost-sure
   termination and expected numerical-QP cost.
7. A factor or \(c\nmid L\) gives decrement at least one. The decrement is
   at most \(O(n)\). This proves both directions of the useful-mass/drift
   equivalence at numerical-QP scale.

No independence is used in the drift theorem. It is used only in the iid
geometric waiting-time statement.

## 4. Corrected cyclic-direction audit

V2 adds both a fully factored \(c\) and the premise

\[
\gcd(c,N)=1.
\]

For \(R_j=r_j^{f_j}\), the kernel of

\[
(\mathbb Z/R_j\mathbb Z)^\times
\longrightarrow\mathbb F_{r_j}^\times
\]

is an \(r_j\)-group. The subgroup generated by \(g_j\) has order \(c\),
which is prime to \(r_j\). Their intersection is therefore trivial.
Reduction is injective on the entire cyclic direction.

For every exponent used during stripping, synchronized order \(c\) makes
all local components identities simultaneously or none of them identities.
In the second case, injection makes every component nonidentity already
modulo its rational prime. The gcd is consequently \(N\) or \(1\), never a
proper factor or a partial repeated-prime power.

The V1 hostile certificate \(N=63,c=3\) violates
\(\gcd(c,N)=1\). It is outside V2's cyclic theorem. This is the exact
missing premise exposed by the audit.
