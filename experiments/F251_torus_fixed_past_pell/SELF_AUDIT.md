## Algebra checks

1. From (au^2A_y=R^2\), prime valuations give (u\mid R\). Squarefreeness
   then gives (a\mid R/u\). Hence (A_y=av^2\) exactly.
2. The quotient of two solution elements is integral because both
   coefficients in
   (\alpha_2\overline{\alpha_1}\) are divisible by the common norm (a\).
3. Its real embedding is larger than one, while its conjugate is positive.
   Thus both coefficient signs used in the gap bound are forced.
4. The case (aD\) square is separate. It permits at most one solution and
   does not require Pell-unit growth.
5. The upper bound uses only (y<Y\) and is independent of (a\). A large or
   complicated squarefree kernel of (P\) cannot create extra seed classes.

## Probability checks

1. The exact-square count covers all canonical coordinates. Restricting to
   clean or otherwise admitted torus coordinates can only decrease it.
2. The supplied roots are units because (P\) and every admitted (A_y\)
   are units modulo (N\).
3. The sign choice for the positive exact root cannot change a global root
   into a mixed root or conversely.
4. Conditioning on the first of two independent rows fixes a valid positive
   product (P=A_z\). No distributional claim about the squarefree kernel is
   used.
5. A point-fibre bound (c\), rather than uniformity of canonical
   coordinates, gives the raw and powered torus estimates.

## Scope check

The result closes the previously open unrelated two-row exact-square event
for independent torus rows. It also gives a history-wise bound for one
specified past product and one fresh independent row.

It does not close the general P66 parity kernel. After all rows are visible,
a P66 decoder can choose among exponentially many past subset products.
The proof gives no simultaneous bound over that collection and no bound for
retrospective products of three or more rows.
