# Proof of the F161 factor-first relative-order updater

## 1. The common-order certificate

Equation (2) shows that the order of `g` modulo every unknown prime-power
component `R_j` divides `M`. If one component order were a proper divisor
of `M`, then it would divide `M/ell` for some prime `ell` dividing `M`.
The corresponding gcd in (3) would be divisible by `p_j`. It is one by
hypothesis. Therefore every component order is exactly `M`, which proves
(4).

The public screen `gcd(M,N)` either returns a proper factor or gives (1a).
Under the valid common-order hypotheses,
`M<=phi(R_j)<R_j<=N`, so `M<N`; this gcd cannot equal `N`.

For odd `p_j`, the group
\((\mathbf Z/R_j\mathbf Z)^\times\) is cyclic. Its elements whose `M`-th
powers are one form the unique subgroup of order `M`, namely `H_j`.
Hence, for every positive integer `e`,

\[
R_j\mid d^{eM}-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^e\in H_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e_j(d)\mid e.
\tag{28}
\]

## 2. Exactness of the bounded scan

By (28), the full component `R_j` divides `D_e` exactly when `e_j(d)`
divides `e`. A partial prime-power divisibility can only make `D_e` a
proper factor, which is already a successful terminal outcome. Therefore
on a no-factor branch every scanned gcd is either one or `N`.

Suppose the first non-one value is `D_e=N`. Every local relative order
divides `e`. If one were a proper divisor `f<e`, then `D_f` would contain
the corresponding full component and would not be one. This contradicts
the choice of `e`. Thus all local relative orders equal `e`.

If every `D_e` for `1<=e<=B` is one and one local relative order were at
most `B`, equation (28) at that relative order would make the corresponding
gcd non-one. Hence every relative order exceeds `B`. At a common return,
the additional screen `gcd(e,N)` either factors `N` or gives (6a). This
gcd cannot equal `N`, because a common local relative order satisfies
`e<=phi(R_j)/M<R_j<=N`. This proves the scan outcomes.

## 3. Factor-first discrete-log alignment

Let `x` satisfy (14). The unique order-`M` subgroup in each hidden
prime-power component is \(H_j=\langle g_j\rangle\). Thus there are unique
local logarithms

\[
x\equiv g^{a_j}\pmod {R_j},
\qquad
a_j\in\mathbf Z/M\mathbf Z
\qquad(1\le j\le s).
\tag{29}
\]

Fix a prime power `ell^c` dividing `M`. The element `G=g^{M/ell^c}` has
exact order `ell^c` in every hidden component. The element

\[
H=G^{\ell^{c-1}}
\]

has exact order `ell` in every component.

At digit position `t`, suppose the lower digits have already aligned and
form `A_t`. In component `R_j`, write the next local digit as `b_j`. The
usual Pohlig--Hellman calculation gives

\[
Y_t\equiv H^{b_j}\pmod {R_j}.
\tag{30}
\]

Because `gcd(M,N)=1`, the digit prime `ell` differs from every `p_j`.
Reduction from units modulo `R_j` to units modulo `p_j` has a `p_j`-group
kernel. It is therefore injective on the order-`ell` subgroup generated by
`H`. The powers \(1,H,\ldots,H^{\ell-1}\) are distinct even after reduction
modulo every `p_j`.

If the hidden digits are not all equal, the test for any digit that occurs
in a nonempty proper subset of the components has a gcd divisible by
exactly that subset of prime-power components. It is a proper factor. If
all hidden digits equal `b`, exactly that candidate gives gcd `N`, and
every other candidate gives gcd one. Thus each digit either gives a factor
or a common digit.

After every prime-power component is aligned, integer CRT gives one
`a mod M`. Every local logarithm equals this same `a`, so (15) holds
modulo `N`. Conversely, any mismatch is exposed at its first differing
prime-power digit. This proves the alignment interface.

There are `c_i` digits for `ell_i^c_i`, and each digit tests at most
`ell_i` candidates. Since every `ell_i<=B`,

\[
\sum_i c_i\ell_i
\le B\sum_i c_i
\le B\log_2 M.
\]

All group operations, inversions, exponentiations, and gcds are public.
This is digit-wise Pohlig--Hellman: it scans at most `ell_i` values for
each of `c_i` digits. It never scans the full prime power
`ell_i^(c_i)`. A square-root baby-step/giant-step improvement is optional;
the displayed `O(c_i ell_i)` bound is already quasipolynomial.

## 4. The aligned presentation is forced to be cyclic

Assume `e>=2`, all local relative orders equal `e`, and

\[
d^e=g^a\pmod N.
\tag{31}
\]

Consider the finite abelian presentation

\[
P=\langle G,D\mid G^M=1,\ D^e=G^a,\ GD=DG\rangle.
\tag{32}
\]

In additive notation its relation lattice is generated by

\[
(M,0),
\qquad
(-a,e).
\]

The determinant has absolute value `Me`, so

\[
|P|=Me.
\tag{33}
\]

For each hidden prime-power component `R_j`, the assignment
`(G,D)->(g_j,d_j)` gives a surjection

\[
P\twoheadrightarrow K_j=\langle g_j,d_j\rangle.
\]

The subgroup `H_j` has order `M`, and the coset `d_j H_j` has order `e`.
Therefore

\[
|K_j|=Me.
\tag{34}
\]

The surjection is between finite groups of the same size, so it is an
isomorphism. Since `K_j` is a subgroup of the cyclic group
\((\mathbf Z/R_j\mathbf Z)^\times\), it is cyclic. Hence `P` is cyclic.
Equivalently, its first Smith invariant is

\[
\gcd(M,a,e)=1.
\tag{35}
\]

The global map \(P\to(\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z)^\times\) has trivial kernel
because every CRT-component map has trivial kernel. Therefore its image

\[
K=\langle g,d\rangle
\]

is also isomorphic to `P`. This proves that `K` is cyclic of exact order
`L=Me`, globally and in every hidden component.

This argument also identifies the precise hidden flaw in a scan without
alignment. Equal relative indices can give different component relations
`D^e=G^{a_j}`. They do not give the single global presentation (32). The
factor-first logarithm step is necessary.

## 5. Public construction of a common generator

The cyclicity proof gives `gcd(M,a,e)=1`. Let `E_0` be the product in
Section 4.1. The moduli `M` and `E_0` are coprime, so the CRT system (19)
has a public solution `s`.

If a prime `ell` divides both `e` and `M`, equation (35) gives
`ell` not dividing `a`. Since `s=a mod M`, `ell` does not divide `s`.
If `ell` divides `e` but not `M`, equation (19) gives `s=1 mod ell`.
Therefore

\[
\gcd(s,e)=1.
\tag{36}
\]

Choose `u,v` by extended Euclid so that `ue+vs=1`. Define

\[
\psi:P\longrightarrow\mathbf Z/L\mathbf Z,
\qquad
\psi(G)=e,
\qquad
\psi(D)=s.
\tag{37}
\]

The first relation of (32) maps to `Me=L`. The second maps to

\[
es-ae=e(s-a),
\]

which is divisible by `Me` because `s=a mod M`. Thus `psi` is a
well-defined homomorphism. Its image contains

\[
ue+vs=1,
\]

so it is surjective. Both groups have order `L`, hence `psi` is an
isomorphism. The word

\[
h=g^u d^v
\]

maps to one and therefore has exact order `L`. This proves the deterministic
Smith construction, including the orientation and the sign in the relation
`D^e=G^a`.

Since `gcd(M,N)=gcd(e,N)=1`, also `gcd(L,N)=1`. For each component
\(R_j=p_j^{\alpha_j}\), reduction of units modulo `R_j` to units modulo `p_j`
has a `p_j`-group kernel. It is injective on the order-`L` subgroup.
Therefore `h^(L/ell)` is not one even modulo `p_j`, for every prime
`ell|L`. Every gcd in (22) is exactly one. The word `(h,L)` is the next
public common-order certificate.

For the optional Las Vegas method, both `g` and `d` have orders dividing
`L`. The homomorphism

\[
(\mathbf Z/L\mathbf Z)^2\longrightarrow K,
\qquad
(u,v)\longmapsto g^u d^v
\tag{38}
\]

is surjective. Uniform independent `u,v` therefore give a uniform element
of `K`.

The cyclic group `K` has exactly `phi(L)` generators. A generator `h` has
exact order `L` in every hidden component and passes (22).

If a tested word has unequal local order behavior at some `ell`, (22) is a
proper factor and the algorithm stops correctly. If no proper factor occurs
and every test is one, all component orders are `L`: an order dividing `L` is
exactly `L` if and only if it does not divide `L/ell` for every prime
`ell|L`. Therefore every returned pair is correct.

It remains to bound the waiting time. Let

\[
q_1<q_2<\cdots<q_w
\]

be the distinct prime factors of `L`. Since `q_i>=i+1`,

\[
\frac L{\varphi(L)}
=\prod_{i=1}^w\frac{q_i}{q_i-1}
\le\prod_{i=1}^w\frac{i+1}{i}
=w+1
=\omega(L)+1.
\tag{39}
\]

Also `omega(L)<=log_2 L`. Thus a successful generator occurs in expected
at most `log_2 L+1` trials. The success probability is positive, so the
sampler terminates almost surely. Every factor output is a verified proper
gcd. The deterministic word above makes random sampling unnecessary.

## 6. Exact finite checks

For every odd `N`, the pair `(-1,2)` passes the common-order certificate.
For `N=91` and `d=30`, direct reduction gives

\[
30^2\equiv81,
\qquad
30^4\equiv9,
\qquad
30^6\equiv1
\pmod {91}.
\]

Thus `D_1=D_2=1` and `D_3=91`. Also `30^3=64 mod 91`, and

\[
\gcd(64-1,91)=7,
\qquad
\gcd(64+1,91)=13.
\]

This proves the alignment-mismatch certificate. For `d=17`,

\[
17^2\equiv16,
\qquad
17^3\equiv-1,
\qquad
17^4\equiv74,
\qquad
17^6\equiv1
\pmod {91}.
\]

Therefore the first common return is `e=3`, the aligned exponent is `a=1`,
and the order-six certificate screens are `gcd(89,91)=gcd(15,91)=1`.

For `N=35,d=2`, the first two values are

\[
\gcd(2^2-1,35)=1,
\qquad
\gcd(2^4-1,35)=5.
\]

For `N=77,d=2,B=2`, the same values are both one. Modulo 7, the first
power of 2 in `{1,-1}` is `2^3=1`; modulo 11, it is `2^5=-1`. The local
relative orders are therefore three and five. This verifies all finite
claims in Section 5 of the statement.

## 7. Smoothness and bit complexity

Every prime factor of the old `M` is at most `B`. Since `1<=e<=B`, every
prime factor of `e` is also at most `B`. Therefore every prime factor of
`L=Me` is at most `B`.

The complete factorization of `e` can be found by trial division through
`sqrt(e)`. This takes at most `sqrt(B)` divisions, which is
quasipolynomial under (1). Merging it with the supplied factorization of
`M` preserves a complete known factorization at every successful update.

The bounded scan makes `B` modular exponentiations and gcds. Their moduli
have `n` bits, and their exponents have `O(n+log B)` bits because

\[
M\le\min_j\varphi(R_j)<N.
\]

Repeated squaring therefore gives quasipolynomial bit complexity. Equation
(18) gives the same bound for alignment. Factoring `e`, solving the small
CRT system, applying extended Euclid, and verifying the deterministic Smith
word are all quasipolynomial. Hence one complete updater call has
deterministic quasipolynomial bit complexity. The optional sampler has the
same expected bound.

## 8. Iteration and the remaining source gap

After one aligned growth, `(h,Me)` is another certified common-order pair.
It can be used as the next input. After `t` successful growth calls,

\[
M_t=M_0\prod_{i=1}^t e_i,
\qquad e_i\ge2.
\]

Every `M_t` divides every component group order `phi(R_j)`. Therefore

\[
2^tM_0\le M_t\le
\gcd_{1\le j\le s}\varphi(R_j)<N,
\]

so `t<n`. A polynomial number of deterministic quasipolynomial calls
remains quasipolynomial.

This is only a monotone bound after successful source hits. F161 gives no
method to make a released block have relative order at most `B`, no density
of such blocks, and no bound on failed source candidates. The decoder now
covers arbitrary odd CRT decompositions, including prime powers, but it
does not supply the released blocks. This omission prevents any claim of a
complete factoring algorithm.
