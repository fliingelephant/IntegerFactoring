# F218 candidate — local weight collapse and multiplicative-index boundary

## Status and scope

This is a frozen, self-audited boundary candidate for the balanced
beta-two branch.  It proves exact coefficient congruences, a named-model
big-Witt obstruction, and an exact counterexample to a Frobenius-compressed
theta isolation claim.  It does not evaluate the remaining divisor
coefficient and is not a factoring algorithm or a lower bound against
arbitrary coefficient algorithms.

Assume

\[
N=pq,\qquad p<q<2p,\qquad N\equiv3\pmod4,
\]

with distinct odd primes `p,q`, and put

\[
K=(N-1)/2.
\]

The complete factorization of `K` is granted.  Then `(N,K)=1` and every
divisor of `N` is a unit modulo `K`.

## Theorem A — the moving weight collapses locally to weight two

Let `Lambda` be any positive multiple of the Carmichael exponent
`lambda(K)`, and set

\[
k=\Lambda+2.
\]

Since `K>1` is odd, `k` is even and at least four.  For every prime power
`ell^e || K` and every positive integer `m`, write

\[
m=\ell^{v_\ell(m)}m_{(\ell)},\qquad \ell\nmid m_{(\ell)}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{k-1}(m)
\equiv\sigma_1(m_{(\ell)})\pmod{\ell^e}.}
\tag{A1}
\]

Define the arithmetic positive-index normalization

\[
\mathcal H_\ell(\tau)
=\frac{\ell E_2(\ell\tau)-E_2(\tau)}{24}.
\]

It is a weight-two modular form on `Gamma_0(ell)` with rational Fourier
coefficients, and

\[
\boxed{
[q^m]\mathcal H_\ell
=\sigma_1(m)-\ell\mathbf1_{\ell\mid m}\sigma_1(m/\ell)
=\sigma_1(m_{(\ell)}).}
\tag{A2}
\]

Thus the positive-index `q`-expansion of the moving-weight Eisenstein
series with coefficient `sigma_(k-1)(m)` reduces modulo each `ell^e || K`
to a fixed-weight, moving-level, `ell`-depleted weight-two Eisenstein
series.  At `m=N`, no depletion occurs:

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{k-1}(N)\equiv\sigma_1(N)\pmod K.}
\tag{A3}
\]

The moving weight therefore supplies no coefficient distinct from the
P29 divisor-sum target.  This is an equivalence of targets, not an
impossibility result for compressed evaluation.

## Theorem B — the prime-level eta quotient is the same local target

Let `K=r>3` be prime and

\[
P(q)=\prod_{a\ge1}(1-q^a),\qquad
F_r(q)=\frac{P(q)^r}{P(q^r)}.
\]

For `m=r^v u` with `(u,r)=1`, one has coefficientwise

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{[q^m]F_r}{r}
\equiv u^{-1}\sigma_1(u)\pmod r.}
\tag{B1}
\]

The quotient is well-defined because every positive coefficient of
`F_r-1` is divisible by `r`.  At `N=2r+1`, `u=N` and `N^(-1)=1 mod r`, so

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{[q^N]F_r}{r}\equiv\sigma_1(N)\pmod r.}
\tag{B2}
\]

Hence the first `r`-adic eta-quotient lift and Theorem A's depleted
weight-two coefficient are the same local divisor-sum datum at the target.

## Theorem C — `K`-supported big-Witt operations cannot create the affine index

Let `M_K` be the multiplicative monoid generated by the primes dividing
`K`.  In a big-Witt ghost-coordinate circuit, permit ring addition,
multiplication, constants, and Frobenius and Verschiebung operations
`F_d,V_d` only for `d in M_K`.  At ghost coordinates,

\[
w_m(F_dX)=w_{dm}(X),\qquad
w_m(V_dX)=d\mathbf1_{d\mid m}w_{m/d}(X).
\]

Define the `K`-rough part

\[
\rho_K(m)=\frac{m}{\prod_{\ell\mid K}\ell^{v_\ell(m)}}.
\]

Every dependence path from an output seed coordinate `s` to an input
coordinate `j` preserves

\[
\boxed{\rho_K(j)=\rho_K(s).}
\tag{C1}
\]

Since `(N,K)=1`, `rho_K(N)=N`.  Consequently, a circuit whose queried
seed coordinates all satisfy `s<N` cannot obtain the remote ghost
coordinate at index `N` through `K`-supported Frobenius/Verschiebung
operations.  The congruence `N=1 mod K` is affine, while these standard
Witt operations move indices only multiplicatively.

This theorem covers only the declared ghost-coordinate model.  Allowing
`F_N` names the target coordinate directly and does not evaluate it.
Cartier operators, coefficient convolution, nonlinear encodings, or new
non-Witt state are outside the claim.

## Theorem D — Frobenius-simple theta state has a nonzero cusp contaminant

Let `r>3` be prime, `N=2r+1`, and

\[
\vartheta(q)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}q^{a^2},
\qquad k=(r+1)/2.
\]

Modulo `r`, Frobenius gives

\[
\vartheta(q)^{4k}
=\vartheta(q)^{2r+2}
\equiv\vartheta(q^r)^2\vartheta(q)^2.
\tag{D1}
\]

Writing `R_2(m)=[q^m] theta(q)^2`, its target coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
[q^N]\vartheta^{2r+2}
\equiv R_2(N)+4R_2(r+1)+16\pmod r.}
\tag{D2}
\]

The odd-index coefficient of the Eisenstein projection of
`theta^(4k)` is

\[
(-1)^{k+1}
\frac{4k}{(2^{2k}-1)B_{2k}}
\sigma_{2k-1}(N).
\tag{D3}
\]

Kummer congruence and Fermat reduce (D3) to

\[
\boxed{(-1)^{k+1}8\sigma_1(N)\pmod r.}
\tag{D4}
\]

These two quantities are not equal in general, even on the balanced
prime-`K` branch.  The smallest branch instance with a nonzero difference
is

\[
r=17,\qquad N=35=5\cdot7,\qquad k=9.
\]

Here (D2) is `15 mod 17`, (D4) is `10 mod 17`, and the cusp contribution
is

\[
\boxed{5\pmod{17}\ne0.}
\tag{D5}
\]

Thus Frobenius compresses the full theta coefficient to the smaller
index `r+1`, but the Eisenstein projection that contains the factor trace
does not follow that compression: a nonzero cusp coefficient carries the
difference.

## Theorem E — the affine Cartier section has no linear recurrence

Fix any prime `r` and define

\[
b_r(j)=\sigma_1(rj+1)\bmod r,
\qquad j\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{(b_r(j))_{j\ge0}\text{ is not eventually periodic}.}
\tag{E1}
\]

Consequently, over `F_r` it satisfies no constant-coefficient homogeneous
or affine linear recurrence of any finite order, and its generating series

\[
\sum_{j\ge0}b_r(j)x^j
\]

is not rational.  The same conclusion holds with `sigma_1` replaced by
`sigma_s` for every positive integer `s`.

This rules out every fixed finite linear Cartier state for the progression
`rj+1`, not only the order-eight model in F218-D01.  It does not rule out
nonlinear, nonrational, or growing-state Cartier algorithms, nor an identity
restricted only to the single digit `j=2` while `r` varies.

## Exact remaining scope

The moving weight, the first eta lift, and the theta projection all retain
the same remote additive divisor coefficient.  `K`-supported Witt
Frobenius/Verschiebung cannot turn the affine relation `N=2K+1` into an
index contraction, and the direct affine Cartier section has no fixed
linear recurrence.  Remaining live mechanisms include a genuinely
compressed random-access coefficient evaluator, Cartier operators with
nonlinear or growing state, a computable cusp projector, nonlinear
characteristic identities, or a different integer statistic.
