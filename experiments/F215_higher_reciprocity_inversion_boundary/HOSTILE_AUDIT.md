# F215 hostile audit

## Verdict

ray-character error, Hilbert/Hensel error, failure of the exact 527 witness,
coefficient-content gap, norm-separation overclaim, Fourier-separation gap, or
unpaid unconditional runtime claim.

The result is narrow. The negative theorem applies only to the declared
product-valued, one-dimensional abelian transcript. It is not an
information-theoretic factoring lower bound: an unordered Frobenius pair, a
character trace, a non-diagonal CRT element, or a successful norm separator is
outside that transcript and can retain the inversion orbit. The frozen packet
states these exclusions.

There are three harmless precision notes:

1. In A4, notation \(\mathbb Q_r\) requires \(r\) to be a rational prime.
   The proof says “known odd primes of \(K\),” so this is the intended reading
   of “if \(r\mid K\).”
2. The quantifier \(d\mid K\) includes \(d=1\). The Frobenius statement is
   then trivial; \(\operatorname{ord}_1\) needs the conventional value one or
   an explicit \(d>1\) qualification.
3. Bach--Shallit's primary statement gives expected polynomial time under
   GRH when a multiple of \(\Phi_k(p)\) is supplied. The frozen comparison is
   correct as a hypothesis comparison, but “positive transition” must retain
   those source qualifiers. No F215 theorem uses that comparison as an
   unconditional algorithm.

## 2. Elementary inversion and recovery

For each \(d\mid K\),

\[
pq=2K+1\equiv1\pmod d.
\]

Neither factor divides \(K\), since \(2K\equiv-1\) modulo either factor.
Thus both residues are units and \(q\equiv p^{-1}\pmod d\).

The size argument also survives the smallest permitted input. Since the
factors are distinct odd primes, \(p\geq3\) and \(q\geq5\), and

\[
2(K-q)=q(p-2)-1>0.
\]

Hence \(p<q<K\). At \(d=K\), the least positive members of the true
inversion orbit are exactly \(p,q\), so recovery followed by exact division
is valid.

## 3. Composite \(d\), Frobenius, and decomposition

No squarefreeness or primality of \(d\) is needed. Because \(p,q\nmid d\),
both rational primes are unramified in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_d)\). Under the
standard cyclotomic identification,

\[
\operatorname{Frob}_p=\sigma_p,
\qquad
\operatorname{Frob}_q=\sigma_q=\sigma_p^{-1}.
\]

For an unramified prime in this abelian extension, the decomposition group is
the cyclic group generated by its Frobenius. An element and its inverse
generate the same group, including for composite \(d\). The residue degree is
the order of that element, so

\[
D_p=D_q,
\qquad
f_p=f_q=\operatorname{ord}_d(p)=\operatorname{ord}_d(q).
\]

This conclusion concerns decomposition groups and splitting type. It does not
say that the actual elements \(\sigma_p,\sigma_q\), the explicit factors of
\(\Phi_d\) over the two hidden residue fields, or an unordered pair of
separately supplied Frobenius values is unavailable or uninformative. The
proof explicitly avoids that stronger reading.

## 4. Ray characters and transcript semantics

A rational ray class group, with or without the real place in the modulus, is
a quotient of the appropriate residue group modulo \(d\). Therefore the
classes of \(p,q\) are inverse when \(d\mid K\). Every one-dimensional
character consequently gives

\[
(\chi(p),\chi(q))=(z,z^{-1}),
\qquad
\chi(N)=1.
\]

For a quadratic/genus character, \(z=z^{-1}\). For a public conductor \(c\)
not satisfying \(N\equiv1\pmod c\), multiplicativity instead gives exactly

\[
\chi(q)=\chi(N)\chi(p)^{-1}.
\]

The factor swap is therefore the affine involution
\(z\mapsto\alpha z^{-1}\), where \(\alpha=\chi(N)\) is public. The statement
about a general ray or ideal class group is correctly conditional on already
having an inverse class pair \(C,C^{-1}\); it does not assert that arbitrary
hidden ideal classes form such a pair.

For a \(K\)-supported conductor, every allowed product query evaluates to one
on every formal pair \((u,u^{-1})\). Public algebraic postprocessing and an
adaptive next-query rule cannot change identical transcripts. This induction
is valid for variable stopping as well.

This is also the exact limit of the argument. A trace
\(\chi(p)+\chi(q)\), an unordered vector of the two values, an ordered CRT
element, a selected hidden prime ideal, or any external selector is not a
product query. Such data can reduce the inversion torsor, and the frozen
definition excludes it.

## 5. Hilbert symbols and Hensel lifting

Bimultiplicativity in either slot gives, at each fixed public place where the
symbols are defined,

\[
(p,a)_{m,v}(q,a)_{m,v}=(N,a)_{m,v}.
\]

Global reciprocity similarly constrains a product of local terms. Neither
identity supplies an ordered pair of hidden values.

Let \(r\) be a prime divisor of \(K\) with \(r\nmid m\). For
\(f(X)=X^m-N\),

\[
f(1)\equiv0\pmod r,
\qquad
f'(1)=m\not\equiv0\pmod r.
\]

Hensel's lemma gives an \(m\)-th root of \(N\) already in \(\mathbb Q_r\).
It remains an \(m\)-th power after extension to a field containing
\(\mu_m\), so a Hilbert symbol with \(N\) in either slot is trivial. If
\(r\mid m\), the derivative argument fails; the packet makes no triviality
claim and retains only the public product identity. This is correct.

## 6. Exact witness \(N=527\)

The displayed arithmetic is exact:

\[
527=17\cdot31=2\cdot263+1,
\qquad
65^2\equiv17\pmod{263},
\qquad
174^2\equiv31\pmod{263}.
\]

Trial division through 13 proves 263 prime. Hence its unit group is cyclic of
order \(262=2\cdot131\). A homomorphism to \(\mu_3\) is trivial. A
homomorphism to \(\mu_4\) or \(\mu_8\) has image of order at most two and
therefore factors through the unique quadratic character. Both hidden factors
are displayed squares, so every such character is one on both. This does not
cover order-131 characters; the packet expressly says so.

For \(m=3,4,8\), one has \(263\equiv-1\pmod m\). Thus every prime above 263
in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\) has residue degree two and norm \(263^2\). A
rational residue lies in \(\mathbb F_{263}^{\times}\), and

\[
\frac{263^2-1}{m}
=262\frac{264}{m}.
\]

The multiplier \(264/m\) is respectively 88, 66, and 33. Euler's
power-residue criterion is therefore one for every nonzero rational residue,
at every prime above 263. In particular, the octic assertion does not lose one
of the two degree-two primes in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\).

As an independent finite check, exhaustive enumeration of rational residues
modulo 263 found no exception for any of the three exponents. Exhaustive
enumeration of units modulo 527 found exactly four \((N-1)\)-returning
residues:

\[
1,\ 154,\ 373,\ 526.
\]

Their local orders at 17 and 31 are respectively

\[
(1,1),\ (1,2),\ (2,1),\ (2,2).
\]

The mixed cases give proper gcds 17 and 31 from \(a^{263}\pm1\); the equal
cases are \(\pm1\pmod{527}\) and give only trivial gcds.

## 7. General order-return formulas

Reduction modulo \(p-1\) and \(q-1\) gives

\[
\gcd(N-1,p-1)=\gcd(N-1,q-1)=d_0,
\quad
d_0=\gcd(p-1,q-1).
\]

Thus every local order of an \((N-1)\)-return divides \(d_0\). On the
\(N\equiv3\pmod4\) branch, the factors have opposite classes modulo four,
so \(v_2(d_0)=1\). With \(h=(q-p)/2\), both possible orientations give
\(h\) odd and

\[
K\equiv h\pmod{p-1},
\qquad
K\equiv-h\pmod{q-1}.
\]

Also

\[
d_0=\gcd(p-1,2h)
=2\gcd(p-1,h),
\]

and symmetrically at \(q\). Therefore

\[
\gcd(K,p-1)=\gcd(K,q-1)=d_0/2.
\]

No choice of whether the smaller prime is 1 or 3 modulo four changes the
argument. For 17 and 31, \(d_0=2\), proving the claimed cap.

## 8. Full-component coefficient content

Let \(r\nmid m\) be prime. The polynomial \(X^m-1\) is squarefree over
\(\mathbb F_r\). Since

\[
X^m-1=\prod_{s\mid m}\Phi_s(X),
\]

the reductions of the cyclotomic factors are squarefree and pairwise
coprime. Every root of \(\Phi_m\) therefore has exact order \(m\). It
follows that

\[
A_m/rA_m
\]

is a product of finite fields and that, for \(a\not\equiv b\pmod m\),
\(\zeta_m^a-\zeta_m^b\) is nonzero in every factor and hence is a unit.

At \(e=a\), full-component equality modulo \(pA_m\) makes every
power-basis coefficient divisible by \(p\). The difference in the full
\(q\)-component is a unit, so it is not zero and not all coefficients are
divisible by \(q\). Because the monic quotient gives a free power basis and
\(N=pq\), the coefficient gcd is exactly \(p\). The argument at \(e=b\) is
symmetric. No domain property of \(A_m/NA_m\) is used.

The runtime claim is also charged correctly. One may keep all coefficients
reduced modulo \(N\); there are \(m\varphi(m)\) coefficient visits, and each
stored coefficient has \(O(\log N)\) bits. Cyclotomic construction and monic
polynomial reduction can be done in time polynomial in the numerical values
of \(m,\varphi(m)\) and \(\log N\), hence in numerical QP under the stated
bounds. The algorithm tries every \(e\), so it does not need the hidden
labels \(a,b\) as input.

## 9. Selected-prime norm collision

The weaker warning is necessary. Here is an explicit attempted
counterexample to the accidentally stronger statement that two distinct
selected labels suffice.

Take

\[
m=3,
\quad p=7,
\quad q=13,
\quad N=91,
\quad A_3=\mathbb Z[\zeta_3],
\]

and

\[
Y=43+56\zeta_3\pmod{91A_3}.
\]

Modulo 7, the roots of \(\Phi_3\) are 2 and 4. At the selected prime with
\(\zeta_3=2\), one has \(Y=1\). Modulo 13, the roots are 3 and 9. At the
selected prime with \(\zeta_3=3\), one has \(Y=3=\zeta_3\), so the two
selected labels are distinct. But at the conjugate prime over 13 with
\(\zeta_3=9\), one has \(Y=1\). Therefore subtracting the first label
collides above both rational primes, and indeed

\[
\operatorname N_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)/\mathbb Q}(Y-1)
=42^2-42\cdot56+56^2
=2548
=28\cdot91.
\]

The gcd is \(N\), not a factor. Thus a zero at one selected prime over
\(p\), plus a no-zero condition at every prime over \(q\), is exactly the
needed norm hypothesis. The frozen theorem includes it.

The resultant criterion is sound: modulo a rational prime, the resultant
vanishes exactly when \(\Phi_m\) and the chosen representative of
\(Y-\zeta_m^e\) share a root over the algebraic closure. One selected local
zero proves norm divisibility; absence of all conjugate collisions over the
other rational prime proves the required nondivisibility.

## 10. Fourier trace and divisor coefficient

For any finite abelian \(G\), the Fourier transform of

\[
\mu_u=\delta_u+\delta_{u^{-1}}
\]

is \(t_\chi(u)=\chi(u)+\chi(u)^{-1}\). Fourier inversion on all functions on
\(G\) is injective, including when \(u=u^{-1}\), where the point mass has
multiplicity two. Equality of every trace therefore gives equality of the
two multisets and hence \(v=u\) or \(u^{-1}\).

For \(G=(\mathbb Z/K\mathbb Z)^\times\), all four divisors of \(N\) are
units, \(q\equiv p^{-1}\), and \(\chi(N)=1\). Consequently

\[
\sum_{c\mid N}\chi(c)
=2+\chi(p)+\chi(p)^{-1}.
\]

This is the exact trace plus the public constant two. The positive transition
is conditional on all three missing algorithmic ingredients: a uniformly
constructible QP-size separating bank, exact QP coefficient evaluators, and a
QP decoder. It does not infer that the full character table is small. It also
charges exact root-of-unity representation and verification. Thus the Fourier
argument proves separation, not an omitted unconditional factoring
algorithm.

## 11. Primary-source and scope check

The three bibliographic records and their uses are accurate.

1. Bach and Shallit, [“Factoring with Cyclotomic
   Polynomials”](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1),
   *Mathematics of Computation* 52(185), 201--219 (1989), explicitly start
   with a multiple \(E\) of \(\Phi_k(p)\) for an unknown prime divisor
   \(p\). Their abstract states expected polynomial time under GRH. A
   factorization of \(pq-1\) does not itself supply the required multiple of
   \(\Phi_k(p)\). F215 uses no stronger conclusion.
2. Adleman, Pomerance, and Rumely, [“On distinguishing prime numbers from
   composite
   numbers”](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1983/117-1/p07), *Annals of
   Mathematics* 117, 173--206 (1983), is a primality/compositeness
   distinction algorithm based on higher-reciprocity machinery. It is not a
   theorem that every failed identity produces a rational zero divisor.
3. Cohen and Lenstra, [“Primality testing and Jacobi
   sums”](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1984-0726006-X), *Mathematics
   of Computation* 42, 297--330 (1984), explicitly describe tests that either
   prove compositeness or constrain possible divisors; their early gcd branch
   can sometimes find a factor, but a failed Jacobi-sum identity is not by
   itself an automatic factor. The frozen comparison says exactly this.

These are context citations only. The F215 proofs depend on elementary
cyclotomic Frobenius, character multiplicativity, Hilbert-symbol
bimultiplicativity, Hensel lifting, finite-field separability, and finite
Fourier inversion, all of which were reconstructed above.

## 12. Final scope disposition

The hostile attempts do not justify any broader conclusion than the frozen
one:

- actual or separately supplied Frobenius values can reveal the inversion
  orbit;
- order-131 characters exist on the 527 witness;
- selected-prime labels need an all-conjugates norm separator;
- nonabelian, nonlinear, ring-valued, and Archimedean methods remain open;
- a divisor-coefficient evaluator is additional arithmetic information; and
- a separately known multiple of a hidden cyclotomic group order is a
  different factoring hypothesis.

With those exclusions enforced, all frozen claims pass.
